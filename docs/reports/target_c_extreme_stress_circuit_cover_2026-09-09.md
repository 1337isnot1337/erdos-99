## LEVEL I STATUS

`STILL OPEN`

The existing extreme-stress sparsification theorem ES proves that the normalized KKT polytope has affine dimension `s_Z` and that every extreme normalized stress omits at least `s_Z` actual rows. There is a stronger exact statement hidden in the same geometry:

> the positive support of every extreme normalized stress is a **circuit of the actual planar rigidity row matroid**.

Thus an extreme stress is not merely sparse. It is a minimal dependent mixed contact/diameter framework: delete any one supported row and the remaining supported rows are independent at the actual source embedding.

Furthermore, the full actual contact/diameter row set is covered by at most `s_Z+1` such circuit supports. In the special branch `s_Z=0`, the complete mixed framework itself is one spanning `(2,3)`-circuit with exactly `2v-2` rows.

---

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
E:=C\sqcup H,
\qquad m:=|E|=e+h,
\]

on the `v` vertices of the nonisolated C65 core. Let `R` be the ordinary planar bar-rigidity matrix of the equality framework on `E`.

ZF gives

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}R=2v-3.}
\tag{EC.1}
\]

Let `W=ker(R^T)` be the signed self-stress space after changing the sign convention on the diameter coordinates as in ES. Then

\[
\dim W=s_Z+1.
\tag{EC.2}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal P
=\left\{x=(\alpha,\beta)\in W\cap\mathbb R^m_{\ge0}:
L(x):=\sum_{h\in H}\beta_h=1\right\}.
\tag{EC.3}
\]

C65 gives a relative-interior point `x^0 in P` with all `m` coordinates positive. ES proves

\[
\dim_{aff}\mathcal P=s_Z.
\tag{EC.4}
\]

---

## 2. Restricted stress space at an extreme point

Fix a vertex `x*` of `P` and write

\[
S:=\operatorname{supp}x^*\subseteq E.
\]

Define the linear self-stress subspace supported on `S` by

\[
W_S:=\{w\in W:\operatorname{supp}w\subseteq S\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem EC.1 — an extreme support has one-dimensional stress space

\[
\boxed{\dim W_S=1.}
\tag{EC.2a}
\]

### Proof

Certainly `x* in W_S`, so `dim W_S>=1`.

Suppose `dim W_S>=2`. Choose `w in W_S` not proportional to `x*` and put

\[
u:=w-L(w)x^*.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
u\in W_S,
\qquad L(u)=0.
\]

Also `u!=0`: otherwise `w=L(w)x*`, contradicting the choice of `w`.

Every coordinate of `x*` on `S` is strictly positive, and `u` vanishes outside `S`. Hence for sufficiently small `epsilon>0`,

\[
x^*+\epsilon u\ge0,
\qquad
x^*-\epsilon u\ge0.
\]

Both perturbed vectors remain in `W`, both have normalization `L=1`, and they are distinct. Therefore

\[
x^*=\frac12(x^*+\epsilon u)+\frac12(x^*-\epsilon u)
\]

is a nontrivial convex decomposition in `P`, contradicting that `x*` is a vertex.

Thus `dim W_S=1`. `square`

---

## 3. Extreme support is a rigidity-matroid circuit

The rows of `R` indexed by `S` have dependence space exactly `W_S`. By EC.1 this dependence space is one-dimensional, generated by `x*` after the contact/diameter sign conversion.

Every coordinate of `x*` on `S` is nonzero. Therefore no proper subset of `S` can be dependent: a dependence supported on `S' proper subset S` would belong to `W_S`, hence would be a scalar multiple of `x*`, impossible because `x*` is nonzero on `S setminus S'`.

### Theorem EC.2 — circuit theorem

For every extreme normalized KKT stress `x*`,

\[
\boxed{S=\operatorname{supp}x^*\text{ is a circuit of the actual rigidity row matroid}.}
\tag{EC.3a}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{rank}R_S=|S|-1,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{rank}R_{S'}=|S'|
\qquad(S'\subsetneq S).
\tag{EC.4a}
\]

This is an embedded statement at the actual source coordinates; no generic-position assumption is used.

### Vertex-subset sparsity consequence

Let `U` be a vertex subset and suppose the supported edges with both endpoints in `U` form a proper subset of `S`. Those rows are independent by EC.2. Every planar rigidity matrix on `|U|>=2` vertices has rank at most `2|U|-3`. Hence

\[
\boxed{|S[U]|\le2|U|-3.}
\tag{EC.5}
\]

Thus every extreme support obeys Laman sparsity on every proper induced supported edge set, even though special source geometry may make the full circuit smaller than the generic count `2|V(S)|-2`.

## 4. The circuit stress remains PSD and diameter-spanning

C53 applies to every normalized KKT stress, so

\[
\Omega(x^*)\succeq0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The canonical positive-support theorem also applies to every normalized stress: its positive contact graph has exactly one nontrivial component, contains every endpoint of every positive diameter edge, and spans Euclidean diameter `D`.

Therefore every extreme point of `P` supplies a mixed rigidity circuit with all of the following simultaneously:

1. positive contact coefficients and positive diameter coefficients in KKT sign convention;
2. a PSD scalar stress matrix;
3. one nontrivial positive contact component containing the positive diameter support;
4. Euclidean span exactly `D`;
5. minimal row dependence.

The circuit may leave some source-core vertices isolated. EC does not silently assert that every extreme circuit spans all `v` vertices.

## 5. At most `s_Z+1` extreme circuits cover the full actual support

Because `x^0` lies in the relative interior of the `s_Z`-dimensional polytope `P`, Caratheodory gives vertices

\[
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(k)},
\qquad k\le s_Z+1,
\]

and positive coefficients `lambda_i` summing to one such that

\[
x^0=\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i x^{(i)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since every coordinate of `x^0` is strictly positive, each actual row belongs to the support of at least one `x^(i)`. Hence:

### Theorem EC.3 — circuit cover

The complete actual mixed support satisfies

\[
\boxed{
C\cup H
=\bigcup_{i=1}^k\operatorname{supp}x^{(i)},
\qquad k\le s_Z+1,
}
\tag{EC.6}
\]

where every member of the cover is a PSD, diameter-spanning rigidity circuit in the sense of Sections 3-4.

This strengthens ES.18. ES proved that one can choose at most `s_Z+1` extreme PSD stresses whose stress-matrix kernels intersect in the affine kernel. EC additionally identifies every support in that family as a minimal rigidity dependence and observes that positivity of `x^0` makes their union cover every actual active row.

## 6. The exact equality branch `s_Z=0`

If

\[
s_Z=0,
\]

then `P` has affine dimension zero. Thus its unique normalized stress is the C65 full-positive stress itself. Its support is all of `C union H`.

ZF gives

\[
e+h=2v-2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By EC.2 the full mixed row family is a rigidity circuit. Therefore:

### Corollary EC.4 — spanning Laman-circuit equality branch

If `s_Z=0`, then

\[
\boxed{e+h=2v-2,}
\]

all mixed rows lie in one unique full-support dependence, and every proper edge subset is independent at the actual embedding.

Consequently the abstract mixed graph obeys

\[
\boxed{|E(U)|\le2|U|-3}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for every vertex subset `U` whose induced edge set is proper, while the full edge set has `2v-2` edges. Thus the full mixed graph is a `(2,3)`-circuit in the ordinary Laman count sense.

The unit square with its two diagonals is the small binding regression: its mixed graph is `K_4`, the basic planar rigidity circuit. EC.4 does not claim that large circuits are impossible.

## 7. Why this helps the live endpoint

ZL proves the exact closing defect

\[
(b+Q)-2h=-2s_Z.
\]

Thus the global contradiction must eliminate all nonnegative `s_Z` survivors.

EC turns that scalar into a concrete circuit-complexity parameter:

- `s_Z=0`: the **entire** actual mixed framework is one full-support rigidity circuit;
- `s_Z>0`: the actual active framework is covered by at most `s_Z+1` PSD diameter-spanning rigidity circuits.

This suggests two finite geometric targets that are sharper than another scalar defect bound:

1. exclude a sufficiently large full-support mixed rigidity circuit whose even edges are the planar triangle-free unit-contact graph and whose odd edges are the convex-terminal diameter thrackle;
2. more generally, prove that covering a two-dimensional near-capacity contact core by diameter-spanning circuits of this sign type requires more than `s_Z+1` circuits.

The second target would convert two-dimensional core capacity directly into a lower bound on `s_Z`; together with ZL it could force a contradiction or a much narrower equality regime.

## 8. Regression audit

- Regular even polygon shells show that arbitrarily large positive mixed rigidity circuits can exist at special embeddings. Therefore EC does **not** claim that circuit size alone is bounded.
- An extreme circuit need not contain every actual diameter row or every contact row.
- An extreme circuit may have a larger scalar-stress kernel than the relative-interior ZR stress; only the collective family recovers the affine kernel.
- No generic rigidity theorem is substituted for the actual row matroid.
- Zero multipliers in one extreme stress do not make the corresponding actual constraints weak.
