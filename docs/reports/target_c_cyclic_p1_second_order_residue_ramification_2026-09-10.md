# Erdős Problem #99 — second-order 2-adic residue ramification excludes maximal-order prime P1 families

**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Run type:** exact second-order local 2-adic argument  
**Target:** cyclic primitive `d_H=1`, P1 endpoint  
**Depends on:** PR #152 residue localization; RB residue-degree budget on this branch; P1 trace equation  
**Computation:** none

## LEVEL I STATUS

`STILL OPEN`.

The preceding RB report obtains only the first Newton-polygon denominator for a nonzero odd-order trace residue.  For

\[
m=ru,\qquad r=2^k,
\]

that first segment has slope `-1/r`, so RB records a ramification factor `r`.

The inseparable first residual polynomial actually contains another forced factor of two.  The explicit linear perturbation `4X` in the P1 trace equation determines the next valuation exactly.

### Theorem SR2 — doubled local ramification

Let `r=2^k>=2`, let `u` be odd, and let `xi` be a nontrivial residue `u`-th root of unity of odd order

\[
d>1,
\qquad d\ne3.
\]

Put

\[
\tau=\xi+\xi^{-1},
\]

and let `f_d` be the degree of its Frobenius orbit over `F_2`, equivalently

\[
f_d=\min\{f\ge1:2^f\equiv\pm1\pmod d\}.
\]

Any `Q_2`-irreducible factor of

\[
G_{ru}(T)=C_{ru}(T)+4T-6
\]

whose roots reduce to the trace orbit of `tau` has degree at least

\[
\boxed{2r f_d.}
\tag{SR2.1}
\]

Thus the first-order RB lower bound `r f_d` doubles.

### Prime-family corollary

Suppose

\[
u=p\ge5
\]

is prime and

\[
f_p=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

Then for every `r=2^k>=2` the cyclic P1 endpoint is impossible.

Indeed SR2 gives a local factor of degree at least

\[
2r\frac{p-1}{2}=r(p-1),
\]

whereas RB gives the global P1 trace-degree ceiling

\[
\deg h\le\frac{r(p-1)}2.
\]

The local factor cannot be larger than the global irreducible factor containing it.  Contradiction.

So:

\[
\boxed{
q-2=2^k p,\ k\ge1,\ p\ge5\text{ prime},\
2\text{ generates }(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}
\Longrightarrow \text{P1 impossible}.}
\tag{SR2.2}
\]

This includes `p=5` and many other prime odd parts.  No infinitude statement about such primes is needed or claimed.

## 1. Local trace lift

Let `K/Q_2` be the unramified extension generated by a Teichmüller lift `zeta` of `xi`, and put

\[
a:=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
C_u(a)=\zeta^u+\zeta^{-u}=2.
\]

Because `xi` is nontrivial and `u` is odd, the root `a` of `C_u(T)-2` has multiplicity exactly two.  More precisely, over a splitting field,

\[
C_u(T)-2=(T-2)\prod_{\{\eta,\eta^{-1}\},\ \eta^u=1,\ \eta\ne1}
(T-(\eta+\eta^{-1}))^2.
\]

Distinct nontrivial residue traces remain distinct: in characteristic two,

\[
x+x^{-1}=y+y^{-1}
\]

implies `y=x` or `x^{-1}`.  Therefore all factors other than the chosen square are units at the lift `a`.  Also `a-2` is a unit.  Hence, for

\[
U(X):=C_u(a+X)-2,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{U(X)=X^2(c+XB(X)),\qquad c\in\mathcal O_K^\times,}
\tag{1.1}
\]

with `B(X)` integral.

The condition `d!=3` is exactly what makes

\[
\boxed{a-1\in\mathcal O_K^\times.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed the residue trace equals one iff

\[
\xi+\xi^{-1}=1,
\]

which is equivalent in characteristic two to

\[
\xi^2+\xi+1=0,
\]

and hence to `ord(xi)=3`.

## 2. The power-of-two outer trace

Define

\[
F_r(V):=C_r(2+V)-2.
\]

For `r=2`,

\[
F_2(V)=V^2+4V.
\]

For every power of two `r>=4`, the standard doubling identity

\[
C_{2n}=C_n^2-2
\]

gives

\[
F_r(V)\equiv V^r\pmod4.
\]

Since both sides vanish at `V=0`, there is an integral polynomial `A_r` such that, uniformly for every power of two `r>=2`,

\[
\boxed{F_r(V)=V^r+4V A_r(V).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `r=2`, take `A_2=1`.

Using `C_{ru}=C_r\circ C_u`, the local P1 trace polynomial is therefore

\[
\boxed{
H(X):=G_{ru}(a+X)
=U(X)^r+4U(X)A_r(U(X))+4(a-1)+4X.
}
\tag{2.2}

## 3. First Newton slope and the unit variable W

Equation (1.1) and the unit condition (1.2) give the first Newton segment

\[
(0,2)\longrightarrow(2r,0),
\]

exactly as in RB.  Thus every root `X` in this residue cluster satisfies

\[
\boxed{v(X)=\frac1r,}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `v(2)=1`.

Define

\[
\boxed{W:=\frac{X^r}{2}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then `v(W)=0`.

Divide (2.2) by four.  From (1.1),

\[
\frac{U(X)^r}{4}
=W^2(c+XB(X))^r.
\]

Since `r` is a power of two at least two, every non-endpoint binomial coefficient in

\[
(c+XB)^r-c^r
\]

is even.  The final term `(XB)^r` has valuation one, and every other nonconstant term has valuation at least

\[
1+\frac1r.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U(X)^r}{4}=c^rW^2+E_1,
\qquad v(E_1)>\frac1r.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Also

\[
v(U(X)A_r(U(X)))\ge v(U(X))=\frac2r>\frac1r.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently the root equation `H(X)=0`, divided by four, has the sharp form

\[
\boxed{
c^rW^2+(a-1)+X+E=0,
\qquad v(E)>\frac1r.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

The explicit `+X` term is the first perturbation beyond the inseparable residual square.

---

## 4. The second valuation is exactly 1/(2r)

Reduce (3.5) modulo the maximal ideal.  There is a unique residue class `w_bar` satisfying

\[
\overline c^{\,r}\,w_{bar}^{\,2}+\overline{a-1}=0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

because the residue field is perfect of characteristic two.

Choose any unit lift

\[
w\in\mathcal O_K^\times
\]

of `w_bar`.  Then

\[
v(c^r w^2+a-1)\ge1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since `r>=2`,

\[
1>\frac1r.
\]

Subtract (4.2) from (3.5).  The `X` term has valuation exactly `1/r`, while every discarded term has strictly larger valuation.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
v(W^2-w^2)=\frac1r.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Put

\[
t:=v(W-w)>0.
\]

Because the residue characteristic is two,

\[
W+w=(W-w)+2w.
\]

If `t>=1`, then both factors in

\[
W^2-w^2=(W-w)(W+w)
\]

would have valuation at least one, contradicting (4.3), whose right side is at most `1/2`.  Thus `t<1`, and therefore

\[
v(W+w)=t.
\]

Equation (4.3) becomes

\[
2t=\frac1r.
\]

So

\[
\boxed{
v(W-w)=\frac1{2r}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the missing second-order valuation.

Any finite extension of `K` containing `X` also contains `W-w`.  Its value group therefore contains `1/(2r)`, so its ramification index over the unramified field `K` is divisible by

\[
\boxed{2r.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The trace residue orbit has degree `f_d` over `F_2`; hence the corresponding `Q_2` factor has degree at least

\[
2r f_d.
\]

This proves SR2.1. `square`

## 5. Prime maximal-projective-order exclusion

Let `u=p>=5` be prime.  Every nonzero residue root has exact order `p`, and its trace orbit degree is

\[
f_p=\min\{f:2^f\equiv\pm1\pmod p\}.
\]

PR #152 excludes the zero trace cluster, so every local factor of the global P1 minimal polynomial lies over a nonzero `p`-trace orbit.

Assume

\[
f_p=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

Then there is one nonzero trace orbit.  SR2 gives degree at least

\[
2r f_p=r(p-1)
\]

for any `Q_2` factor of `h`.

But RB proves

\[
\deg h\le\frac{r(p-1)}2.
\]

This is impossible.  Therefore P1 is excluded for the complete family SR2.2.

The previous RB saturation normal form is thus not merely rigid in this range: once the second-order term is retained, it is inconsistent.

## 6. Scope and regressions

### Why `r>=2` is required

For `r=1`, the first root valuation is `v(X)=1`.  Then the integer lifting error in (4.2), the explicit `X` term, and the cubic correction from `U(X)` all live at the same valuation.  Cancellation is possible and must be audited separately.  SR2 makes no claim for odd `m=u`.

### Why order three is excluded

For a residue root of order three, `a-1` is not a unit.  The first Newton polygon changes; this is exactly the exceptional cluster handled separately by PR #150 for `u=3`.

### No global irreducibility claim

SR2 is local.  It does not assert that `G_m` is irreducible over `Q`, or even that the complete nonzero residue cluster is one `Q_2` factor.  It only proves that every individual local factor in a non-order-three residue cluster has ramification index divisible by `2r`.

### No computation dependency

The proof uses only the local double-root factorization, the exact power-of-two identity (2.1), and valuation arithmetic.

## 7. Next target

The arithmetic frontier is now split cleanly.

1. **Prime odd part, `r>=2`:** maximal projective order is completely excluded.  The remaining primes satisfy
   \[
   f_p\le\frac{p-1}{4}.
   \]
   The next target is to combine the number of distinct trace residue orbits with rational-factor/Galois constraints so that a global irreducible factor cannot choose only one small orbit.

2. **Odd `r=1` branch:** resolve the coincident second-order terms explicitly.  For `p=5` direct local expansion suggests the same doubled ramification, but a uniform proof needs the exact coefficient of the valuation-one correction.

3. **Composite odd part:** each used non-order-three divisor class of order `d` costs at least `2r f_d` degrees.  Combine this with the global budget
   \[
   \deg h\le\frac{r(u-1)}2
   \]
   and the factorization of `S_u` into trace orbits.
