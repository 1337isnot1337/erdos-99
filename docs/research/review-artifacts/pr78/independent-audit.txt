## Independent proof reconstruction

Before reading the report's reconstruction subsection, I derived the main
determinant/linkage mechanism from the stated block matrix and source definitions.
The fixed matching contributes a unique diagonal monomial to `det A[Z,B]`, so the
basis block is invertible over the rational-function field of independent edge
variables. Block elimination identifies a Schur minor with its bordered determinant
divided by that denominator. Distinct supported permutations have different edge
monomials; none can cancel. Thus a nonzero bordered minor is exactly a supported
matching of its chosen rows and columns.

An arbitrary injection saturating `B union J` need not itself cover every `Z`, so
that interface requires more than just naming generic matching rank. Comparing it
with the fixed `B--Z` matching yields degree-at-most-two alternating components.
Each `J` vertex is an endpoint of a path whose other endpoint is a free `O` vertex;
all `B` vertices are matched twice in the superposition, so the paths cannot end
there. Flipping only these disjoint `J`-paths preserves saturation of every `B` and
`Z`. This supplies the required bordered row set. Conversely, flipping any such
path family gives an injection. Alternating cycles can remain and do not invalidate
the argument. The code tests cyclic as well as acyclic supports.

Every basis vertex has its unique incoming matched edge from its paired `Z`, and
every matched `Z` has that edge as its unique outgoing edge. A simple directed
terminal-to-sink path uses both endpoints of a matched pair or neither.
Contracting the pair therefore preserves vertex-disjoint linkage provided the
contracted unit keeps capacity one. Source and sink capacities must also remain one.
I inspected the submitted split-vertex residual implementation, reverse arcs and
endpoint connectors; exhaustive separator enumeration separately includes terminal
and sink vertices. The determinant permutation sign convention is valid for the
actual sorted block orders used by every call.

For a tube `T`, basis closure removes exactly the basis partners of `T cap Z`;
shield terminals are eligible only when *all* their neighbors lie in `T`. A path
meeting a removed basis vertex has just used its matched tube row, while a path
starting at a shield terminal must next visit a tube row. Hence at most `|T|`
vertex-disjoint paths meet the shield. A remaining path stays in one undirected
internal component until its final sink. Its start and end give a matching in the
coarse complete-within-sector graph even when sink neighborhoods overlap. This
proves `rank S <= |T| + nu_coarse`.

Only when `O_i` are disjoint does `nu_coarse` equal
`sum_i min(|C_i|,|O_i|)`. The decomposition of surviving sinks then gives the two
reported nullity and right-deficiency inequalities. Exterior tube sinks cancel,
but the `|T cap Z|` term remains. Sector basis balance follows because both
endpoints of every surviving fixed pair are present in the same internal component.
The old-versus-new lower bound difference is `|C_sh| + |T cap Z|`, consistent with
the strict-improvement count. All these implications were reconstructed and checked
against the code; no source-point or packing-optimality inference enters them.

The analytic rank-one count also checks out: rank one requires the two direct
terminal neighborhoods to be the same singleton sink (two choices). The two basis
edges into that sink are arbitrary (factor four). For zero, either one, or both
directed off-diagonal basis edges, the reachability enumeration gives respectively
25, 22, 22, 19 choices for `C--Z` and safe basis edges into the other sink. Thus
`2*4*(25+22+22+19)=704`; the remainder is `9216-704=8512`.

   true right deficiency zero. Summing separate sink surpluses yields the false
   lower bound two; coarse matching gives the correct lower bound zero.

   three-terminal graph, independently enumerated simple paths give packing

   Edge-disjoint paths after contraction give three. This isolates the exact
   contracted-unit capacity failure without incorrectly blaming edge capacities
   on the original uncontracted matching edge.
4. Wrong matching orientation: the listed graph has generic rank two but only
   one directed terminal-to-sink path if matched edges point left-to-right.
5. Equal-weight specialization: the shared-sink graph has generic rank two.
   With basis identity, zero `F,G`, and all four direct entries one, `S` is the
   all-ones 2-by-2 matrix, with determinant zero and rank one. Support/generic rank
   cannot be substituted for this specialization.
