# Erdős Problem #99 — Target B virtual-zonotope missing-crossing identity

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Run type:** exact convex/train identity  
**Target:** upstream transfer after the coherent phase endpoint exclusions  
**LEVEL I STATUS:** STILL OPEN

## 1. Setup

Work in a pure simply connected strict-rhombic disk. Let the train set be `mathcal T`, with one unit train vector `u_tau` for each train `tau`. Let

`R`

be the union of all rhombus faces and

`A_R=area(R)`.

Let

`K=conv A`

be the source convex hull, and let

`G=area(K)-A_R`

be the shell/convexification area.

Define the centered train zonotope

`Z=sum_(tau in mathcal T)[-u_tau/2,u_tau/2]`.

Section 25 already gives

`per(Z)=2T`,

`2V(K,Z)=sum_tau w_K(nu_tau)`,

and, in the pure disk,

`J=2(V(K,Z)-area(K))`,

with

`H_partial^K=2G+J`.

The observation below identifies the part of `area(Z)` not physically realized by rhombi.

## 2. Zonotope area counts all train pairs

For a planar zonotope generated by unit segments `[-u_tau/2,u_tau/2]`, the standard determinant formula is

`area(Z)=sum_(tau<sigma)|det(u_tau,u_sigma)|`.        (2.1)

In a strict-rhombic train disk, every rhombus is the unique crossing of two train tracks, and its area is

`|det(u_tau,u_sigma)|`.

Distinct trains cross at most once. Therefore

`A_R=sum_(tau<sigma : tau crosses sigma)|det(u_tau,u_sigma)|`.       (2.2)

Subtracting gives the exact identity

### Theorem 1 — virtual area equals noncrossing determinant mass

`area(Z)-A_R`

`=sum_(tau<sigma : tau does not cross sigma)|det(u_tau,u_sigma)|`.    (2.3)

Call this quantity `V_miss`.

It is the area carried by train-generator pairs that exist in the full zonotope but are absent from the physical rhombic disk.

## 3. The source hull lies inside the train zonotope

Train coordinates write every source vertex, up to one common translation, as

`p_v=sum_tau epsilon_tau(v)u_tau`, `epsilon_tau(v) in {0,1}`.

Subtracting the fixed vector `(1/2)sum_tau u_tau` gives

`p_v-(1/2)sum_tau u_tau=sum_tau (epsilon_tau(v)-1/2)u_tau in Z`.

Hence, after translation,

`K subseteq Z`.                                               (3.1)

Mixed area is monotone under convex inclusion, so

`V(K,Z)<=V(Z,Z)=area(Z)`.                                    (3.2)

Using the Section-25 identity

`V(K,Z)=area(K)+J/2`,

we obtain

`area(Z)-A_R >= area(K)+J/2-A_R`

`=G+J/2`

`=H_partial^K/2`.

Thus:

### Theorem 2 — support depth forces virtual noncrossing area

`sum_(tau<sigma : tau does not cross sigma)|det(u_tau,u_sigma)|`

`>= H_partial^K/2`.                                          (3.3)

This is exact and finite.

## 4. Asymptotic consequence

Section 24 gives, for sufficiently large triangle-free jammed survivors,

`H_partial^K >= (2sqrt(c)-sqrt(3)) n-O(n^(2/3))`,

where

`c=2sqrt(3)/pi`.

Therefore

`V_miss >= (sqrt(c)-sqrt(3)/2)n-O(n^(2/3))`.                  (4.1)

Numerically the leading coefficient is about

`0.1840497320... n`.

Since `n=Theta(D^2)`, the noncrossing determinant mass is necessarily `Theta(D^2)`.

This has an important interpretation for the new coherent-phase stability results: one should **not** expect the entire train-crossing graph to become asymptotically complete between its phase classes. A quadratic amount of virtual zonotope area must remain unrealized physically.

## 5. Coherent-phase interpretation

Suppose a train population is `o(1)`-coherent around three triangular projective directions. Then:

- same-phase determinants are `o(1)` per pair;
- different-phase determinants are `sqrt(3)/2+o(1)` per pair.

Consequently, if all but `o(D)` trains lie in such coherent phase classes, (3.3) implies

`# {noncrossing different-phase pairs}`

`>= H_partial^K/sqrt(3)-o(D^2)`.                              (5.1)

Thus the support-depth currency appears combinatorially as a compulsory quadratic population of missing cross-phase edges.

The currently promoted unique-max/balanced/two-max endpoint exclusions handle the opposite limit in which one coherent phase core is near its cyclic-order crossing maximum. The identity here shows that the upstream transfer cannot simply prove that *all* missing cross-phase pairs are negligible. Any valid closure has to localize the mandatory virtual area into low-degree/interface trains, or show that the required missing-pair pattern itself forces the diameter/shell contradiction.

## 6. Audit boundary

The theorem does **not** give an upper bound on `V_miss`. It therefore does not by itself contradict the shell lower bound.

It also does not imply that every noncrossing pair belongs to distinct coherent phases; low-degree/interface trains may carry a large part of the determinant mass. This is precisely the remaining localization problem.
