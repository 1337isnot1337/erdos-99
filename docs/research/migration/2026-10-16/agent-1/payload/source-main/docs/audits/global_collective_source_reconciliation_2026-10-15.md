## Separable-distance proof audit

For actual contact and diameter edges, common probability-weighted unsigned
incidence marginals cancel all vertex logarithms. The weighted logarithmic
diameter average exceeds the contact average by exactly `log D^2`.
Every-pair maximum and minimum bound these averages, including any formerly
inactive pair that becomes an extremum. Conversely, strict separation of the
two finite incidence convex hulls provides a vertex potential. For sufficiently
small positive parameter, all inactive pairs keep their finite margins and
the formal diameter/separation ratio strictly decreases. This converse has
no Euclidean realization conclusion.

Anchor inversion of each actual planar configuration divides each squared
pair distance by the product of its two anchor distances. The assumed vertex
factors leave one common scale `1/s0^2` between the inverted complete arrays.
Polarization recovers a plane isometry even in rank one; composing the two
inversions and that similarity proves the classification. The anchor maps
through infinity to its prescribed finite image. No other source label is
a pole. A single inverted label handles the two-point case.

For every odd `m>=33`, the regular unit rim has equal uniform marginals on
its cycle contacts and longest chords. A translated lattice of spacing
`t=1+R^-2` supplies the specified interior population in the open radius-`R-1`
disk. Interior contacts are absent; rim/interior distances exceed one; all
upper pairs are below the rim diameter because `2R-D<1`. Thus the actual
full contact graph is precisely the odd rim with the retained interior labels
as isolates. The count satisfies `n/D^2 -> pi/(2sqrt(3))`.

The separate strict inequality `n<Delta2 D^2` follows from `m<=2pi R`,
`R>5` and `R^2 sin^2(pi/(2m))<1/8`. Averaging the unit triangular lattice
in an open disk of radius `sqrt(n/Delta2)/2` yields at least `n` points
for some translate. Their finite diameter is strictly below the source's
diameter. This proves nonglobality independently of the restricted orbit;
leading quadratic density is not attainment at `D(n)`.

## Joint-field proof audit and regularity repair

The three dual matrices have `Lj:Ei=delta_ij`. Supported stresses have zero
work against their same-phase opening strain. Symmetry removes arbitrary
skew parts. Different phases have joint product
`-aij*tj-aji*ti<0`; same-phase products vanish. Rank-one gradient jumps and
continuous traction would force zero product, excluding a distinct-phase
joint jump in one fixed material frame.

The stress-only fixture has determinants `10/3` and `2/9`, rank-one jump
`(4/9)*(2,sqrt(3)) tensor (2,sqrt(3))`, isotropic mean and determinant
mean one. It therefore refutes a stress-only incompatibility implication.
The independently rotated fixture has exactly common positive stress and
a rank-one strain jump; a one-dimensional periodic displacement on a torus
aligned with that jump normal supplies the stated compatible two-phase field.
It refutes extending fixed-frame phase purity across arbitrary grain rotations.
Neither fixture is a unit-distance packing.

The periodic theorem now explicitly assumes bounded **both** `F` and `S`
and a periodic Lipschitz displacement. The original displayed theorem made
boundedness explicit only for `S`, despite using it for all integrals.
Periodic mollification gives `integral S:grad v=0` by distributional
divergence freedom and convergence in `L1`. The zero-trace version is stated
on a bounded Lipschitz domain with the same integrability justification.

Consequently the double integral of the joint jump product is zero. If two
phases had positive measure, their Cartesian product would have strictly
negative integrable product, a contradiction. Quantitatively the good-pair
replacement error is at most `8M*eta`, while pairs touching transition have
measure at most `2delta` and product at most `4M^2`. Ordered unequal-phase
measure is `2 sum(mu_i mu_j)`. These constants give exactly the report's
inequalities (3) and (4), retaining `2B` for arbitrary boundary work.
