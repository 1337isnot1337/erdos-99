# Target C-II — the balanced antipodal adjacent-strip KKT reaction branch is impossible

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Status:** proof-only exact structural strengthening
**Scope:** canonical full-support adjacent O--O strip
**Dependencies:** adjacent-strip sparse KKT reaction theorem; adjacent switching open-semicircle theorem; C65 positive diameter support
**LEVEL I STATUS:** `STILL OPEN. C-II REMAINS OPEN.`

The current sparse-force theorem writes the exact middle-layer switching reaction as

\[
G:=Aa+Bb
=\sum_{h\in H_{sw}}\beta_h n_h,
\qquad A,B>0,
\qquad \beta_h>0,
\tag{ZR.1}
\]

where `a,b` are the two boundary contact vectors oriented from the fixed exterior into
the strip, and each `n_h` is the length-`D` switching diameter vector oriented from its
exterior endpoint into its middle-layer endpoint.

That theorem left one degenerate force branch:

\[
G=0
\iff
b=-a\text{ and }A=B,
\tag{ZR.2}
\]

in which a positive zero-sum diameter reaction could require three switching bars.
The open-semicircle theorem removes this branch completely.

## Theorem ZR

For every adjacent O--O strip in the canonical full-support source,

\[
\boxed{G=Aa+Bb\ne0.}
\tag{ZR.3}
\]

Consequently the nondegenerate part of the sparse-force theorem applies universally:
there are one or two actual switching diameter edges `h_1,h_2` and positive
coefficients such that

\[
\boxed{
G=\lambda_1n_{h_1}
\quad\text{or}\quad
G=\lambda_1n_{h_1}+\lambda_2n_{h_2},
\qquad \lambda_i>0.
}
\tag{ZR.4}
\]

In particular:

- the positive three-diameter zero-force circuit is impossible;
- the exact KKT reaction never needs the exceptional three-bar Caratheodory branch;
- if `b=-a`, then necessarily
  \[
  \boxed{A\ne B.}
  \tag{ZR.5}
  \]

Thus every adjacent-strip KKT force obstruction is an exact one/two-diameter reaction.

## Proof

Normalize

\[
d_h=n_h/D.
\]

The adjacent switching open-semicircle theorem proves that the finite set

\[
\{d_h:h\in H_{sw}\}
\]

is contained in one open semicircle. Hence there is a unit vector `w` such that

\[
w\cdot d_h>0
\qquad(h\in H_{sw}).
\]

C65 supplies a relative-interior normalized stress with

\[
\beta_h>0
\qquad(h\in H_{sw}).
\]

Taking the dot product of the switching side of (ZR.1) with `w` gives

\[
w\cdot\sum_h\beta_hn_h
=D\sum_h\beta_h(w\cdot d_h)>0.
\]

Therefore the sum cannot vanish, proving (ZR.3).

The sparse-force theorem then applies in its `G!=0` branch and gives the one/two-bar
positive representation (ZR.4).

Finally, if `b=-a`, then

\[
G=(A-B)a.
\]

Equation (ZR.3) implies `A-B!=0`, proving (ZR.5).
