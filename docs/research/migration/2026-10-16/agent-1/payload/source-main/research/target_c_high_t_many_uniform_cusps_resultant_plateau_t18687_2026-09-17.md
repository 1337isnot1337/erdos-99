The sharpened fixed-chain theorem gives more than an `O(r)` total defect budget. Because that budget is a sum of nonnegative interface costs, a positive fraction of a growing chain must consist of **uniformly bounded** finite cusps, with constants independent of the growing depth `r_D`.

There is a second exact consequence. If `F_j=A_j u_j` is the Section-157 KKT cut resultant of row `tau_j`, then every contiguous slab reaction is exactly a difference

\[
F_p-F_q.
\]

Hence the block-reaction nondegeneracy theorem says that all cut resultants in the chain are pairwise distinct. At the same time the total switching-multiplier budget forces their normalized diameter to vanish. A hypothetical high-`t` survivor therefore contains an increasingly long **injective resultant path compressed into an `o(D)` ball**, together with linearly many pairwise support-disjoint, uniformly finite local cusp reactions.

This note records those two consequences precisely.

\tag{UR.6}

\[

\qquad
|G_D|>{r\over2},

\tag{UR.7}

The switching supports of all good interfaces were already pairwise disjoint; (UR.10) additionally allows a linearly growing subfamily whose selected strips do not even share a boundary row.

## 5. Exact cut-resultant telescoping

For row `tau_j`, let

\[
A_j:=\sum_{e\in E(\tau_j)}\alpha_e>0
\]

and let `u_j` be its rooted-shore unit vector. Let `epsilon_{hj}` be the signed rooted incidence of actual diameter bar `h` on cut `j`, with `r_h` a fixed orientation of its diameter vector. The audited Section-157 shore sum is

\[
\boxed{
F_j:=A_j u_j
=\sum_h\beta_h\varepsilon_{hj}r_h.
}
\tag{UR.23}
\]

For `0<=p<q<=r`, subtract the two cut resultants:

\[
F_p-F_q
=\sum_h\beta_h(\varepsilon_{hp}-\varepsilon_{hq})r_h.
\tag{UR.24}
\]

The block-translation theorem defines

\[
\delta_h^{p,q}:=\varepsilon_{hp}-\varepsilon_{hq}
\]

and proves that the nonzero terms are exactly the moving diameter bars

\[
H_{p,q}=\bigsqcup_{i=p}^{q-1}H_{sw,i}.
\]

With its inward slab orientation `n_h=delta_h^{p,q}r_h`, (UR.24) becomes

\[
\boxed{
F_p-F_q
=\sum_{h\in H_{p,q}}\beta_h n_h
=G_{p,q}.
}
\tag{UR.25}
\]

On the contact side this is the same identity

\[
G_{p,q}=A_pu_p-A_qu_q,
\tag{UR.26}
\]

because the two boundary contact families of the slab are precisely the complete trains `tau_p` and `tau_q`.

This is an exact finite identity, not an asymptotic approximation.

## 6. Block-reaction nondegeneracy makes the resultant path injective

The contiguous block theorem proves

\[
G_{p,q}\ne0
\qquad(0\le p<q\le r).
\tag{UR.27}
\]

Combining (UR.25) and (UR.27) gives

\[
\boxed{
F_p\ne F_q
\qquad(p\ne q).
}
\tag{UR.28}
\]

Thus all `r_D+1` Section-157 cut resultants are pairwise distinct.

For adjacent rows define

\[
\Delta F_i:=F_i-F_{i+1}=G_{i,i+1}.
\tag{UR.29}
\]

Then every contiguous partial sum is nonzero:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=p}^{q-1}\Delta F_i
=F_p-F_q\ne0.
}
\tag{UR.30}
\]

Moreover each increment is supported on the switching set of exactly one interface, and those supports are pairwise disjoint.

## 7. The injective resultant path collapses after normalization by `D`

Since every moving diameter vector has length `D`, (UR.25) gives

\[
\|F_p-F_q\|
\le D\sum_{h\in H_{p,q}}\beta_h.
\tag{UR.31}
\]

For adjacent rows,

\[
\|\Delta F_i\|\le D B_i.
\tag{UR.32}
\]

Summing and using the whole-chain switching-mass bound,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\|F_i-F_{i+1}\|
\le D\sum_iB_i
<Cr.
}
\tag{UR.33}
\]

Consequently

\[
\max_{p,q}\|F_p-F_q\|<Cr.
\tag{UR.34}
\]

On the growing diagonal, `r_D<=D^(1/2)`, so

\[
\boxed{
\max_{p,q}
\left\|{F_p\over D}-{F_q\over D}\right\|
<{Cr_D\over D}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{UR.35}
\]

Thus the chain has `r_D+1->infinity` pairwise distinct resultants, but the diameter of the normalized resultant set tends to zero.

The same is true for their magnitudes because `|F_j|=A_j`:

\[
\boxed{
\max_{p,q}{|A_p-A_q|\over D}
\le
\max_{p,q}{\|F_p-F_q\|\over D}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{UR.36}
\]

Also `A_j<=D`, since (UR.23) and `sum_h beta_h=1` give `|F_j|<=D`. After one further subsequence, compactness therefore supplies a scalar `a in [0,1]` and a unit direction `u` such that, uniformly over all rows in the growing block,

\[
\boxed{
{A_j\over D}\longrightarrow a,
\qquad
{F_j\over D}\longrightarrow a u.
}
\tag{UR.37}
\]

Here the common direction uses the already-proved phase-flatness of the growing row block together with the common-transversal sign theorem. If `a=0`, (UR.37) simply means all selected cut resultants are `o(D)` uniformly.

This gives a clean new asymptotic fork:

```text
vanishing-resultant plateau:  a=0,

or

positive-resultant plateau:   a>0,
with r_D+1 pairwise distinct exact resultants compressed into o(D)
while >r_D/2 interfaces are uniformly finite cusps.
```

## 9. What changed and what remains

The previous growing-chain normal form allowed an individual interface to have `O(r_D)` defects and gave only `o(D)` localization for an arbitrary TB2 slab. The linear whole-window sharpening removes that weakness on a positive-density subfamily:

```text
r_D -> infinity
+
> r_D/2 uniform-good interfaces
+
< 2,218,000 switching bars on every good interface
+
< 2,218,000 / D switching KKT mass on every good interface
+
opening width < 1.416e7 / D + o(1/D)
+
genuine TB2 endpoint clusters < 1.416e7 + o(1)
+
> 0.0783 D local common transverse trains on every good interface
+
pairwise disjoint reaction-generator supports across interfaces
+
all contiguous slab reactions = differences of cut resultants
+
all cut resultants pairwise distinct
+
normalized resultant diameter -> 0.
```

This does not close C-II. It does remove the possibility that the growing chain hides all of its complexity in a sparse sequence of increasingly bad interfaces. Any survivor must realize an unbounded family of uniformly finite local obstructions and simultaneously fit their exact reaction increments into one collapsing, injective cut-resultant path.

The next useful geometric step is now narrower: combine the convex diameter-thrackle order with either the `a=0` vanishing-resultant branch or the `a>0` positive-plateau branch. In the positive branch, one should exploit that many disjoint switching systems generate bounded nonzero differences between almost identical macroscopic cut resultants. In the vanishing branch, one should couple the uniformly small row resultants to the linear common transverse ladders and the global KKT virial rather than seeking another support-count estimate.

## 10. Scope audit

- The positive-density conclusion uses only nonnegativity and the strict whole-window budget; it does not assume uniformity of the original fixed-`r` `o(.)` errors.
- The local transverse family `B_i^*` may depend on the good interface `i`.
- The exact resultant identity does not imply monotonicity of the vectors `F_j` or of their magnitudes `A_j`.
- Pairwise distinct resultants can lie arbitrarily close; injectivity alone is not a quantitative lower-separation theorem.
- No lower bound on an individual diameter multiplier is used or obtained.
- The `a=0` branch remains possible.
- No exact unit triangle or strict sub-`D` source is claimed.
