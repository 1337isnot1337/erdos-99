## 3. Low response selects one near-diameter attachment cell

Let

\[
d_*:=\max_r d_r.
\]

For sufficiently large `D` there is at least one unloaded component. Otherwise every
positive response pair would be a direct loaded-loaded unit contact, and (2.1) would
force `D^2<=1+epsilon_D`.

Every response pair generated by an unloaded component lies inside its attachment
set and therefore has length at most `d_*`; every direct loaded-loaded response pair
has length one. Thus, for large `D`,

\[
D^2
\le d_*^2(1+\varepsilon_D),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
{d_*\over D}\ge{1\over\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_D}}\to1.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Choose a component `I_*` attaining `d_*`. By C40,

\[
\boxed{
\delta_*\ge d_*-2,
\qquad
\liminf_{D\to\infty}{\delta_*\over D}\ge1.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No upper bound `delta_*/D<=1+o(1)` is asserted or needed. The response hypothesis is
used nowhere else.

## 4. Keep the ZK budget split honest

\[
{\delta_*+r_*\over D}\to\gamma,

{1\over D}\sum_{r\ne *}\delta_r\to t.

\[

\tag{4.2}
\]

The last inequality does not double-spend the budget: every omitted Eppstein slack
`r_r` (`r!=*`) and `s_B` is nonnegative.

## 5. Localize the train-boundary count to `I_*`

Let

\[
G_*:=C[I_*].
\]

Use the HOF lemma. Let

- `k_*` be the outer-face edge-incidence count of `G_*`, with bridges counted twice;
- `Q_*:=sum_F(deg F-4)` over bounded faces of `G_*`;
- `f_*` be the number of bounded nonquadrilateral faces of `G_*`;
- `R_*` be the number of bounded four-faces of `G_*`; and
- `B_*` be the physical boundary-edge occurrence count of those `R_*` rhombi.

HOF identifies the bounded four-faces of `G_*` exactly with the unloaded-only strict
rhombi assigned to `I_*`.

The exact Eppstein component identity is

\[
\boxed{k_*+Q_*=2(\delta_*+r_*).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The finite train-boundary theorem gives

\[
\boxed{B_*\ge4\sqrt{R_*}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every occurrence counted by `B_*` faces either the outer face of `G_*` or one bounded
nonquadrilateral face. Therefore

\[
B_*\le k_*+4f_*+Q_*.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Every bounded nonquadrilateral face has degree at least five, so `f_*<=Q_*`. Hence

\[
B_*\le k_*+5Q_*.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Eliminate `Q_*` using (5.1):

\[
\boxed{
B_*
\le10(\delta_*+r_*)-4k_*.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the exact local analogue of the global Section-85/TQ recipient count.

## 8. The remaining component-diameter budget bounds the remaining cell area

C40 and planar isodiametry give for every `r!=*`

\[
\operatorname{area}(P_r)
\le{\pi\over4}(\delta_r+2)^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

The canonical component ledgers give `c_I=O(D)`. Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r\ne *}(\delta_r+2)^2
&=\sum_{r\ne *}\delta_r^2
 +4\sum_{r\ne *}\delta_r+4(c_I-1)\\
&\le\left(\sum_{r\ne *}\delta_r\right)^2+O(D).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Using (4.1),

\[
\boxed{
\limsup {1\over D^2}
\sum_{r\ne *}\operatorname{area}(P_r)
\le{\pi\over4}t^2.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Type-II cell tiling (2.4) now gives, whenever `0<=t<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
a\ge{\pi\over4}(1-t^2).}
\tag{8.4}
\]

If `t>1`, then (4.2) already gives `mu>=1+t>2>M_*`, so this is the only range that
needs consideration.

Insert (8.4) into (7.6):

\[
\boxed{
\gamma\ge M_*\sqrt{1-t^2},
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

because

\[
C_*{\sqrt\pi\over2}
={2\pi\over5}+{1\over5}\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt3}
={2\over5}\left(\pi+\sqrt{\pi/(2\sqrt3)}\right)
=M_*.
\tag{8.6}

## 9. Optimize the honest split

By (4.2),

\[
\mu\ge\gamma+t.
\tag{9.1}
\]

If

\[
t\ge M_*-1,
\]

then `gamma>=1` gives

\[
\mu\ge1+t\ge M_*.
\tag{9.2}
\]

It remains to consider

\[
0\le t\le M_*-1.
\]

Equation (8.5) gives

\[
\mu\ge M_*\sqrt{1-t^2}+t.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The right side is concave on `[0,1]`, so its minimum on this interval occurs at one
of the endpoints. At `t=0` it equals `M_*`.

For the other endpoint, elementary exact estimates give

\[
{3\over2}<M_*<{5\over3}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

For the lower bound, `pi>3`, `sqrt(3)<2` imply
`Delta_2>3/4`, hence `sqrt(Delta_2)>3/4` and
`M_*>(2/5)(3+3/4)=3/2`.
For the upper bound, `pi<22/7` and `sqrt(3)>5/3` imply
`Delta_2<33/35<1`, hence
`M_*<(2/5)(22/7+1)=58/35<5/3`.

Thus `M_*-1<2/3`, and

\[
M_*\sqrt{1-(M_*-1)^2}
>{3\over2}\sqrt{1-{4\over9}}
={\sqrt5\over2}>1.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Therefore at `t=M_*-1`, the right side of (9.3) is strictly larger than

\[
1+(M_*-1)=M_*.
\]

So (9.3) is at least `M_*` throughout the interval. This proves (LRTQ.1).
