# Target C-II — optimize exceptional-host escape versus flex at one third

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Status:** proof-only exact consequence of the exceptional-host boundary/flex theorem
**Scope:** any set `S` of bounded nonquadrilateral stopping faces in the canonical positive-contact core
**Dependencies:** `target_c_exceptional_host_escape_or_flex_2026-09-15.md`

Let

- `Q_S=sum_{F in S}(deg(F)-4)`;
- `E_boundary(S)` be the number of complete stopped trains with exactly one terminal face in `S`;
- `V_S` be the non-Euclidean contact-flex space generated by trains internal to `S`.

The exact exceptional-host theorem gives

\[
\dim V_S\ge {Q_S-E_{boundary}(S)\over2}-1.
\]

Rearranging gives the sharper exact tradeoff

\[
\boxed{
E_{boundary}(S)+2(\dim V_S+1)\ge Q_S.
}
\tag{OF.1}
\]

The previously recorded symmetric row used the arbitrary threshold `E_boundary=Q_S/2`, yielding `Q_S/2` escape or `Q_S/4-1` flex. The minimax balance of the two branch currencies is instead one third.

Indeed, if

\[
E_{boundary}(S)<Q_S/3,
\]

then

\[
\dim V_S
>{Q_S-Q_S/3\over2}-1
=Q_S/3-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_{boundary}(S)\ge {Q_S\over3}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\dim V_S\ge {Q_S\over3}-1.
}
\tag{OF.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\max\{E_{boundary}(S),\dim V_S+1\}\ge {Q_S\over3}.
}
\tag{OF.3}
\]

This is the optimal common threshold obtainable from the affine lower bound above: solving `x=(Q_S-x)/2` gives `x=Q_S/3`.

## All bounded exceptional faces

Take `S` to be every bounded nonquadrilateral face. Then `Q_S=Q`, and every boundary train has its other terminal occurrence on the actual outer face. Writing `E_BO` for bounded-to-outer complete stopped trains,

\[
\boxed{
E_{BO}\ge Q/3
\quad\text{or}\quad
\dim V_{BB}\ge Q/3-1.
}
\tag{OF.4}
\]

Since every pentagon contributes one unit to `Q`, the uniform high-t pentagon sea gives `Q/D>=p_5(T)-o(1)` with

\[
p_5(T)=C_{707}+\pi-5T/2.
\]

Hence throughout the positive pentagon-sea range,

\[
\boxed{
E_{BO}/D\ge p_5(T)/3-o(1)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\dim V_{BB}/D\ge p_5(T)/3-o(1).
}
\tag{OF.5}

Representative safe common floors are above `0.1179` at `T=1.88`, `0.1013` at `1.90`, `0.0846` at `1.92`, `0.0513` at `1.96`, `0.0179` at `2.00`, `0.0096` at `2.01`, and `0.0013` at `2.02`.

The gain is quantitative but strategically useful: the high-t obstruction is now forced to carry the same linear scale in either the physical bounded-to-outer train family or the dimension of the internal exceptional-host flex space. The next theorem can attack those two currencies without an avoidable factor-two asymmetry.
