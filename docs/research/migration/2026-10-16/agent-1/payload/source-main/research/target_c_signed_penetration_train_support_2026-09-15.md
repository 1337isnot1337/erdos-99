# Target C-II — signed penetration forces linear rhombic-train support

**Date:** 2026-09-15  
**Status:** proved proof-only composition; theorem-state authority unchanged  
**Scope:** current low-`t` full-core Type-II endpoint  
**LEVEL I STATUS:** `STILL OPEN`

The signed straight-event theorem and the degree/phase-gate refinement produce a large
family of distinct inward-pointing unit contacts near the outer boundary. The stopped-
train decomposition gives a separate exact dichotomy: a positive contact edge either
belongs to a non-singleton strict-rhombus train or is itself a singleton stopped train.

At the current endpoint the singleton population is too small to absorb all signed
penetrations. Quantitatively,

\[
\boxed{
E_{R,15}\ge0.23807942312745039\ldots D-o(D),
}
\]

where `E_R,15` counts distinct `15`-degree signed penetration contacts that lie on
non-singleton strict-rhombus trains. Even after restricting to the simple-outer
phase-gate population,

\[
\boxed{
E_{R,simp,15}\ge0.19214288321641658\ldots D-o(D).
}
\]

This is the first direct linear lower bound in the current stack on signed penetration
contacts that are forced onto actual rhombic train tracks rather than being allowed to
escape as isolated/singleton stopped trains.

---

## 1. Exact singleton versus rhombic-train decomposition

Let

- `T` be the total number of stopped trains in the audited Section-142 decomposition;
- `T_R=B_4/2` be the number of non-singleton trains containing at least one strict
  rhombus; and
- `S` be the number of singleton stopped trains.

The explicit rhombic-train theorem proves the exact identity

\[
\boxed{T=T_R+S.}
\tag{TS.1}
\]

A singleton stopped train is precisely a positive-contact edge lying in no strict
rhombus. Conversely, every positive-contact edge incident to at least one strict
rhombus belongs to exactly one non-singleton train: the train-track equivalence relation
is generated by pairing opposite edges in each strict rhombus.

Thus any set `E_*` of distinct positive-contact edges obeys

\[
\boxed{
\#\{e\in E_*:e\text{ lies on a non-singleton rhombic train}\}
\ge |E_*|-S.
}
\tag{TS.2}
\]

No train-occupancy assumption is used: many edges of `E_*` may still lie on the same
non-singleton train.

## 2. Current upper bound on singleton trains

The low-`t` topology/network squeeze gives at `t=t_42`

\[
{T\over D}
\le
5t_{42}-2\pi+o(1)
=2.8212578827176370437\ldots+o(1).
\tag{TS.3}
\]

The explicit robust-grid rhombic-train theorem gives

\[
{T_R\over D}
\ge
2\sqrt{\Delta_2}+0.0004-o(1)
=1.9050256137279146917\ldots-o(1),
\tag{TS.4}
\]

where

\[
\Delta_2={\pi\over2\sqrt3}.
\]

Subtract (TS.4) from (TS.3) and use (TS.1):

\[
\boxed{
{S\over D}
\le
0.9162322689897223521\ldots+o(1).
}
\tag{TS.5}
\]

This is an upper bound on the *entire* singleton stopped-train population, not just on
singletons near the outer boundary.

## 3. All signed `15`-degree penetrations: at least `0.238D` lie on rhombic trains

The near-simple signed degree/phase composition gives a set `E_all,15` of globally
distinct positive contact edges such that

\[
{E_{all,15}\over D}
\ge
1.1543116921171727469\ldots-o(1),
\tag{TS.6}
\]

and every edge is an extra contact at a `15`-degree-straight outer occurrence with
signed inward component greater than

\[
{1\over\sqrt2}.
\tag{TS.7}
\]

At most `S` distinct members of `E_all,15` can be singleton stopped trains, because a
singleton train contains exactly one physical edge. Therefore (TS.2), (TS.5), and
(TS.6) give

\[
\boxed{
{E_{R,15}\over D}
\ge
1.1543116921171727469
-0.9162322689897223521
-o(1)
}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
{E_{R,15}\over D}
\ge
0.2380794231274503948\ldots-o(1).
}
\tag{TS.8}

Every edge counted in `E_R,15` lies on a non-singleton strict-rhombus train and retains
the signed inward component bound (TS.7).

## 4. Simple-outer penetrations still force `0.192D` rhombic-train edges

The same composition proved

\[
{V_{simp,15}\over D}
\ge1.1083751522061389353\ldots-o(1).
\tag{TS.9}
\]

At each simple-outer straight vertex choose one extra contact. The global signed
injectivity theorem applies to every extra incidence, so these chosen contacts are
physical-edge distinct. Let `E_simp,15` be this set. Then

\[
|E_{simp,15}|=V_{simp,15}.
\tag{TS.10}
\]

Again at most `S` of them can be singleton trains. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
{E_{R,simp,15}\over D}
\ge
1.1083751522061389353
-0.9162322689897223521

\[
\boxed{
{E_{R,simp,15}\over D}

\tag{TS.11}

This lower bound is useful because every selected endpoint is now a simple-outer
vertex, so its non-outer angular sectors are honest bounded-face incidences.

## 5. Terminal-versus-internal rhombic-train dichotomy

A physical edge of the full strict-rhombus complex is of exactly one of two types:

1. a **terminal edge**, incident to exactly one strict rhombus; or
2. an **internal train edge**, incident to two strict rhombi.

The first type is one of the two boundary edges of its non-singleton train. The second
lies strictly inside that train track.

Split the simple-outer family from (TS.11) as

\[
E_{R,simp,15}=E_{term}+E_{int}.
\tag{TS.12}
\]

At least one side has half the mass. Therefore every current low-`t` survivor obeys
one of the following two alternatives.

### Alternative A — many terminal penetration edges

\[
\boxed{
{E_{term}\over D}
\ge0.0960714416082082916\ldots-o(1).
}
\tag{TS.13}

A non-singleton train has exactly two terminal edges. Hence the number `N_term` of
distinct rhombic trains carrying at least one selected signed penetration at a
terminal edge satisfies

\[
\boxed{
{N_{term}\over D}
\ge0.0480357208041041458\ldots-o(1).
}
\tag{TS.14}

This is a genuine distinct-train lower bound.

### Alternative B — many internally traversing penetration edges

\[
\boxed{
{E_{int}\over D}
\ge0.0960714416082082916\ldots-o(1).
}
\tag{TS.15}

Every selected edge here is shared by two strict rhombi while one endpoint is a
simple-outer straight vertex. Thus the outer boundary is penetrated by a linear family
of edges lying strictly inside rhombic tracks. These are exactly the local events for
which train occupancy, phase transmission, or a coherent boundary-parallel strip must
be controlled; they cannot be dismissed as train ends.

The terminal/internal split is physical-edge based and does not assume that different
internal edges lie on different tracks.

## 6. A parametric strengthening from quartic straight vertices

Let `V_4` be the number of degree-four `15`-degree straight vertices. The all-extra
signed-injectivity identity gives

\[
E_{all,15}=S_{15}+V_4.
\tag{TS.16}
\]

Hence the all-vertex rhombic-train support actually satisfies

\[
\boxed{
{E_{R,15}\over D}
\ge
0.2380794231274503948\ldots
+{V_4\over D}
-o(1).
}
\tag{TS.17}

So every linear quartic subpopulation transfers one-for-one into additional distinct
signed penetrations that must be paid either by the fixed singleton budget or by
non-singleton rhombic tracks. The floor (TS.8) is only the `V_4=0` worst case.

## 7. Frontier effect

The previous frontier statement said that more than `1.15D` signed penetrations must
be partitioned by stopped trains. The exact stopped-train ledger now yields a first
nontrivial answer:

```text
>= 1.1543116921 D distinct >45-degree signed penetrations
-  <= 0.9162322690 D total singleton stopped trains
=  >= 0.2380794231 D penetrations on non-singleton rhombic trains.

After restricting to simple-outer vertices:
>= 0.1921428832 D remain on rhombic trains,
so either
  >= 0.0480357208 D distinct trains are hit at terminal edges,
or
  >= 0.0960714416 D signed penetrations are internal edges of rhombic tracks.
```

This does not yet bound occupancy in Alternative B. A single long train can contain
many parallel cross-section edges. Theorem 10.6 only says its centerline is a simple
monotone pseudosegment, not that it meets the straight boundary population `O(1)`
times.

The next closing target is therefore much narrower:

> Bound the number of simple-outer signed internal penetration edges that one
> non-singleton train can carry, or prove that a train with linear such occupancy
> creates a macroscopic coherent strip that is incompatible with the near-disk /
> near-extremal Woodall diameter geometry.

**C-II remains open.**
