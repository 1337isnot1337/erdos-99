The implication being attacked is

`certified collective wall packet -> full-source finite replacement ->

## 1. A collective exterior shield

Let `F` be a convex polygon in an actual unit-separated source `A`, with
every cyclic side an actual unit contact. Let `B` be a nonempty subset of
its wall vertices such that

\[
 |v-w|\ge2\qquad(v,w\in B,\ v\ne w).
\tag{1}
\]

Write `V_F` for all original wall vertices, and define the open region

\[
 W_{F,B}=\{x\in\operatorname{int}F:
             |x-w|>1\text{ for every }w\in V_F\setminus B\}.
\tag{2}
\]

**Collective shield.** Every `x in W_{F,B}` is strictly farther than one
from every old source point outside `int F` other than the deleted vertices
`B`. This includes all retained contact isolates outside the wall.

Proof. Retained wall vertices satisfy the claim by (2). An exterior source
point not on the wall is at distance at least one from every original wall
vertex, including vertices that will be deleted.

If `x` is at distance at least one from every original wall vertex, use
the first wall exit of its segment to that exterior point. At a unit edge
with endpoints `c,d`, the weighted-square identity gives distance at least
`sqrt(3)/2` from each segment endpoint to the exit point. The total segment
length is therefore at least `sqrt(3)>1`. Exit at a wall vertex instead
gives a lower bound two. The guard inequalities need not be strict for
this argument.

Otherwise `|x-v|<1` for one deleted vertex `v`. Condition (1) implies
`|x-w|>=1` for every other deleted vertex `w`. Both neighbors `a,b` of
`v` on the wall are retained, since their distance from `v` is one.
Translate `v` to zero and let `r=|x|<1`. Their strict guards give

\[
 x\cdot a<r^2/2,\qquad x\cdot b<r^2/2.
\tag{3}
\]

The point `x` is inside the convex vertex cone generated by `a,b`. For an
exterior point `q` outside this cone's interior, its unit direction has
scalar product with `x` at most
`mu=max(x dot a,x dot b)<r^2/2`. The old guard `|q-v|>=1` gives

\[
 |x-q|^2\ge r^2+1-2\mu>1.
\tag{4}
\]

For `q` inside the cone but outside `F`, the segment cannot exit through
either side incident with `v`. Every other wall vertex is at distance at
least one from `x`, so the unit-edge exit argument above applies, again
giving distance at least `sqrt(3)`. Tangency of two deleted unit disks at
distance two causes no exception: all the other guards in this argument
are allowed to be equal to one. This proves the claim.

Adjacent wall deletions have not been authorized by this result. The
previous square-with-exterior-isolate regression still applies to them.

## 2. The exact cardinality and contact endpoint

Let `S=A intersect int F`, including every old core vertex and contact
isolate in the interior. If a finite unit-separated packet `Y` satisfies

\[
 Y\subset W_{F,B},\qquad |Y|\ge |S|+|B|,
\tag{5}
\]

delete `S union B` and insert exactly `|S|+|B|` packet points. A sufficiently
small dilation of that finite packet makes its mutual distances strictly
greater than one while keeping it inside the open region (2). The collective
shield checks every new-old lower pair. Every new point lies in the interior
of the old full-source convex hull, so every pair involving a new point is
strictly shorter than the old global `D`. Every retained old-old pair is
unchanged.

No new contact is created. The two original wall contacts incident with
each deleted vertex disappear. These are `2|B|` distinct physical contacts:
two deleted vertices cannot be neighbors under (1). The retained graph is
an induced subgraph of the old triangle-free contact graph. Thus the new
full source has the same cardinality, diameter at most `D`, remains
triangle-free, and has at least `2|B|` fewer contacts. This contradicts the
contact-minimal canonical representative. If all old diameter pairs were
incident with deleted points, it is also a strict sub-`D` competitor.

This is a finite source endpoint. It uses no chart radius, no independent
packing identification, and no upper exception allowance.

## 3. There is a linear eligible deletion bank

For any `M` chosen wall vertices, greedy selection supplies a subset `B`
satisfying (1) with

\[
 |B|\ge\lceil M/25\rceil.
\tag{6}
\]

At a chosen center, discard the remaining vertices at distance less than
two. The radius-`1/2` disks centered at these unit-separated vertices have
disjoint interiors and are all inside its radius-`5/2` disk. Area bounds
their count, including the chosen center, by 25. Each greedy step pays at
most 25 original vertices, with no reuse. This proves (6).

The bound is deliberately elementary. It supplies eligible actual wall
deletions, not replacement sites. It does not prove (5).

## 4. The capacity implication fails at a genuine global minimizer

Use a regular unit heptagon, with circumradius

\[
 R={1\over2\sin(\pi/7)},
\]

and retain its center as an eighth source point. Bezdek--Fodor's original
Theorem 1.1, on printed page 106, proves that the least diameter of eight
unit-separated points is

\[
 D(8)={1\over2\sin(\pi/14)}.
\tag{7}
\]

It also proves that every minimizer has a regular unit heptagon as its
convex hull. The center is strictly unit-clear of the seven wall vertices,
so this particular eight-point source attains (7). Its contact graph is
`C_7` plus a contact isolate; it has seven actual diameter pairs, all on
the contact core. Every minimizer has at least the seven hull contacts
and the seven hull diameter pairs. Thus this source attains both
lexicographic minima among triangle-free minimizers at this cardinality.

This literature assertion was checked in the
[local original transcription](../../extracted/text/1-s2.0-S0097316598928898-main.txt)
and the [original PDF](../../papers/primary/1-s2.0-S0097316598928898-main.pdf).
The publisher page is [Bezdek--Fodor (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1998.2889);
its live full-text fetch returned HTTP 403, which was not treated as a
negative finding. The local original supplies the theorem.

Let `B` be any nonempty set of nonadjacent heptagon vertices. Consecutive
retained vertices have angular gap at most `4pi/7`. For any `x in int F`,
with `r=|x|`, a retained vertex lies within angular distance `2pi/7`.
Consequently

\[
 \min_{w\notin B}|x-w|^2
 \le r^2+R^2-2rR\cos(2\pi/7).
\tag{8}
\]

Use `cos(2pi/7)=1-1/(2R^2)`. Subtracting one on the right of (8) gives

\[
 (r-R)\bigl(r-(R-1/R)\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]

Because `r<R`, all retained guards can be strict only if

\[
 r<R-1/R.
\tag{10}
\]

Directed exact bounds give `1<R<7/6`, hence

\[
 R-1/R<7/6-6/7=13/42<1/2.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus `W_{F,B}` has diameter strictly less than one. Its unit-separated
capacity is exactly one: the center belongs to it, and two packet points
cannot be unit-separated. The actual interior population is `|S|=1`,
so (5) fails for every such nonempty `B`, including every pairwise-distance-
at-least-two deletion bank. This rules out a capacity-gain theorem based
only on the shield, eligible deletion count, normalization, mixed core
rigidity, positive stress, and global optimality at arbitrary cardinality.

The source also has normalized positive mixed stress and PSD scalar stress
with the affine core kernel. The exact certificate has scalar rank four
and mixed rank eleven, the required `v-3` and `2v-3` for `v=7`. The isolate
is absent from those matrices and present in every full-source pair check.

This does not refute a sufficiently-large capacity theorem. `D(8)` is
below the canonical large-diameter regime. It is not a Level I disproof
or an unbounded optimal family.

## 5. Common-angle Boolean scope and the strengthened obligation

The seven stopped trains of this source are singleton wall edges. Their
unit vectors are a common rotation of `1,z,...,z^6`, with `z=exp(2pi i/7)`.
The common-angle closure polynomial has Boolean coefficients and degree
at most six. The minimal polynomial `1+z+...+z^6` shows that the only
closed patterns are all zero and all one. The former is stationary;
the latter rotates the whole core and leaves every old diameter unchanged.
Neither supplies the required strict shortening. Large real contact-flex
dimension does not supply nontrivial Boolean patterns.

For arbitrary canonical cores, the additional
[common-angle amplitude test](target_c_common_angle_train_closure_and_full_endpoint_2026-10-06.md#21-the-active-diameters-impose-a-nonuniform-amplitude-gap)
uses mixed rigidity and positive actual-diameter stress to exclude a
source-dependent interval around zero for every nontrivial closed pattern.
The exact hexagon check confirms the rank hypothesis matters: its scalar
stress is PSD with affine kernel and rank three, but its mixed rank is
eight, below the required nine. Its alternating pattern has all first
diameter variations zero and strict second-order shortening. It is a
nonglobal six-point packet, not a canonical-source exception.

The strengthened large-wall task is now explicit: prove (5) for an
actual sufficiently large canonical inactive chamber and an eligible `B`,
or construct a complete same-cardinality strict sub-`D` source. A theorem
must use an asymptotic canonical premise absent from the eight-point
packet, and must cover the actual interior population. The existing large
wall and pentagon-cap regressions still forbid replacing this premise by
wall length, residual area or arbitrary boundary pins. The previous
odd-shell matching collar shows that merely demanding one blocker per
inward proposal is also insufficient. All upper and lower endpoint checks
above remain available once the packet is certified.
