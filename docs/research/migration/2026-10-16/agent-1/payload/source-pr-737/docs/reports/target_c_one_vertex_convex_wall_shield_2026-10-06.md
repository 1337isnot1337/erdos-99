# Target C: strict refill after deleting one convex wall vertex

Date: 2026-10-06. Authority: `583203ac1dc1d7bc49f0ca0574d75e85f9de2ee1`,
state `2026-10-05.audit.62`. Classification: **proved candidate awaiting
independent audit**. No promotion is requested or performed in this report.
C-II and Level I remain open.

The implication under attack is

`one-vertex protected packet in an actual convex contact wall ->
full-source same-cardinality finite endpoint -> fewer contacts or strict
sub-D diameter -> exclusion of that source geometry`.

This changes the packet region in audit.54. It does not infer a packet
from area, wall length, phase residual or an abstract diagram.

## 1. One-vertex exterior shield

Let `F` be the closed region of a convex polygon whose cyclic sides are
actual unit contacts in a unit-separated source `A`. Choose one wall vertex
`v`, with wall neighbors `a,b`. Define

\[
 W_{F,v}=\{x\in\operatorname{int}F:
 |x-w|>1\ \text{for every wall vertex }w\ne v\}.
\]

**Lemma.** Every `x in W_{F,v}` is strictly farther than one from every
old source point outside `int F` other than `v`. More generally this holds
for every exterior point `q` unit-clear from every original wall vertex.

Proof. Retained wall vertices satisfy the conclusion by definition. If
`|x-v|>1`, use the ordinary unit-edge crossing proof from canonical
`C-EMBEDDED-WALL-REFILL`: `x` is clear of every original wall vertex.

Otherwise translate `v` to zero and put `r=|x|<=1`. Let `K_v` be the
closed convex vertex cone generated by the unit vectors `a,b`; its angle
is at most `pi`. The interior point `x` lies in its interior. From
`|x-a|>1`, `|x-b|>1` we get

\[
 x\cdot a<r^2/2,\qquad x\cdot b<r^2/2.
\tag{1}
\]

If `q` is outside the interior of this cone, its unit direction `u`
satisfies

\[
 x\cdot u\le\mu:=\max(x\cdot a,x\cdot b)<r^2/2\le1/2.
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed the closest exterior unit direction to an interior cone direction
is one of the two boundary rays. Since `t=|q|>=1` by source separation
from the old vertex `v`, the function `t^2-2t mu` is increasing for
`t>=1`. Therefore

\[
 |x-q|^2\ge r^2+t^2-2t\mu
 \ge r^2+1-2\mu>1.
\tag{3}
\]

If `q` lies in the interior of `K_v` but outside `F`, the segment `[x,q]`
stays in that cone's interior. It cannot leave `F` through either edge
incident to `v`, or through `v`. Its first wall exit is a retained wall
vertex or an edge with both endpoints retained. The former gives distance
greater than two. For the latter, at `z=(1-s)c+sd`, `0<s<1`, the usual
weighted-square identity gives `|x-z|>sqrt(3)/2` and
`|q-z|>=sqrt(3)/2`; hence `|x-q|>sqrt(3)`. This covers all exterior points.
The old point `v` is used as a separation guard in (3); it is deleted
from the endpoint, rather than silently ignored in the source. QED.

The proof needs convexity and exactly one unguarded wall vertex. It uses
no stress, affine kernel, source chart radius or contact-isolate deletion.

## 2. Exact full-source endpoint

Put `S=A cap int F`. Suppose a finite unit-separated packet
`Y subset W_{F,v}` has

\[
 |Y|\ge |S|+1.
\tag{4}
\]

Delete `S union {v}` and insert exactly `|S|+1` packet points. A sufficiently
small dilation of the finite packet makes every new-new distance strictly
greater than one while preserving its open interior and wall guards. The
lemma checks every new-old lower pair, including retained contact isolates
outside the wall. No initially strict pair is omitted.

All inserted points lie strictly inside the old source hull, so every
pair involving an inserted point is strictly shorter than the old global
diameter `D`. Every retained old-old pair is unchanged. Cardinality is
unchanged; the new diameter is at most `D`. The retained contact graph
is an induced old graph, and no new contact is created. At least the two
old wall contacts incident with `v` disappear.

Consequently a canonical contact-minimal triangle-free global minimizer
cannot contain this wall/packet. A strict diameter decrease contradicts
global optimality; diameter equal to `D` contradicts contact minimality
among triangle-free global minimizers. This uses the contact-first
normalization, not a claim that a newly triangular competitor refutes a
triangle-free source.

If the deleted vertices additionally cover all actual old diameter pairs,
the endpoint diameter is strictly below `D`. A separately deleted diameter
cover `T` can be paid by the stronger packet requirement
`|Y|>=|S|+1+|T\{v}|`. Guards remain valid when more old vertices disappear.

For several convex walls with disjoint interiors and **disjoint vertex
sets**, one may sum such packet capacities. Each new point in wall `j`
is strictly clear of every original vertex of wall `i`, by the shield for
wall `j`. The shield for wall `i` then applies to that new point as an
exterior point and proves new-new compatibility. No assertion is made for
packets from walls sharing deleted guard vertices without another check.

## 3. The one-deletion threshold is six, not five

If the convex unit wall has at most five vertices, `W_{F,v}` is empty.
Thus this theorem does not bypass the terminal pentagon obstruction.

First the ear triangle `a,v,b` is covered by the two closed unit disks
about `a,b`: split it along `v` to the midpoint of `ab`; each half is a
convex hull of three points in the corresponding unit disk, since
`|av|=|bv|=1` and `|ab|<=2`. A point strict-clear from both neighbors
must therefore lie inside the convex hull of the remaining vertices.
It cannot lie on the chord `ab`, which is also covered by those disks.

The remaining polygon has at most three unit edges and one chord of
length at most two. About a point strictly farther than one from all its
vertices, each unit edge subtends an angle strictly below `pi/3`, by the
same cosine-law inequality as canonical `C-SHORT-WALL-ZERO-CAPACITY`.
The closing chord subtends an angle strictly below `pi`. Convexity and
interiority require their positive angles to sum to `2pi`, but their sum
is strictly below `3(pi/3)+pi=2pi`. This is impossible. Walls with three
vertices are already covered by the ear argument.

The proof is independent of triangle-free status and does not assume an
interior source point is an affine-kernel coordinate.

## 4. A certified six-edge packet

Let `r=17/16` and let the rational rotation `U` have cosine `161/289`
and sine `240/289`. Set

\[
 p_j=rU^j(1,0),\quad 0\le j\le4,
 \qquad e=-U^2(1,0),
\]

\[
 H={31679\over16\cdot4913},\qquad
 h={\sqrt{808669}\over4913},\qquad p_5=(H+h)e.
\tag{5}
\]

The cyclic polygon `p_0,...,p_5` is strictly convex, has six unit sides,
and every nonconsecutive wall pair has distance greater than one. Its
origin is strictly interior. The five vertices `p_0,...,p_4` have squared
radius `289/256`, whereas `|p_5|<1`. Consequently the origin is a strict
site in `W_{F,p_5}`, with squared retained-wall margin `33/256`.

If this polygon is an actual bounded physical contact face of a canonical
source, its interior contains no source point. The connected positive
contact core has no vertex in a physical face interior. Any remaining
interior point would be a contact isolate, strict-clear from the six wall
vertices, contradicting canonical short-wall zero capacity. Equation (4)
therefore holds with one site and `S` empty. Such a physical face is
excluded by the full-source endpoint of Section 2, irrespective of extra
external contacts at the deleted vertex.

The finite six-point packet itself is only feasible geometry. It is not
claimed globally optimal, or an actual loaded positive-stress source.
Its replacement changes six contacts to four and keeps its local packet
diameter unchanged. The exclusion of the wall in a canonical source comes
from the proved full-source shield and contact-first normalization.

More generally, every bounded physical unit hexagon of a canonical source
must have **twofold closed-disk coverage**: every interior point must be
at distance at most one from at least two wall vertices. Short-wall zero
capacity supplies the first vertex; if there were only one, the new
shield and the empty physical face would supply a forbidden replacement.
This is an actual additional source premise for a face-packet search.

The regular unit hexagon satisfies that premise and still has
`W_{F,v}` empty for every single deleted vertex. Its five retained unit
radius directions have largest circular gap `2pi/3`. Every interior
nonzero point of radius `r<1` has dot product at least `r/2>r^2/2`
with a retained vertex, hence distance below one from it. At the center
the retained distances are exactly one. Thus the new endpoint cannot
eliminate every hexagon by a one-site argument.

## 5. Two-deletion and pentagon regressions

The shield does not permit deleting two wall vertices without further
guards. Take the unit square `(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)` and the contact
isolate `q=(1/2,-9/10)`. These five source points are unit-separated and
their only contacts are the four square sides. Delete the two bottom
vertices. The candidate `x=(1/2,1/20)` is inside the square and strictly
clear of the two retained top vertices, but

\[
 |x-q|^2=361/400<1.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus one cannot recursively apply the one-vertex theorem to an opened
wall, drop the outside isolate, or replace all wall vertices based on
retained-wall clearance alone. A collective wall opening needs a new
certified shield or direct full-source checks.

The pentagon argument in Section 3 kills the proposed isolated inward
jump of an unloaded flat middle: although a jump can open its two unit
boundary contacts, no interior endpoint clears the other four wall
vertices. Its inability to move does not supply a new spare site.

## 6. Exact verifier and continuation

[The verifier](../../research/verify_target_c_one_vertex_wall_shield_2026_10_06.py)
uses exact arithmetic in `Q(sqrt(808669))`. It checks all 15 old and 15
new packet pairs, six convexity signs, six interior signs, the strict
five-vertex guard, old/new contact counts and the unchanged local maximum.
It also checks all ten source pairs of the two-deletion regression and
the retained contact isolate's exact failed new pair. The universal
exterior lemma and five-edge impossibility are analytic proofs, not finite
enumerations.

This removes the demand to clear **one deleted wall vertex** in a convex
protected packet. It does not supply packets for arbitrary pentagons,
large locked walls or macroscopic containers. The next obligation is a
source-specific packet extraction theorem for the enlarged region, or a
collective wall opening with separately certified exterior guards. The
regular hexagon and near-triangular strip walls remain to be checked;
positive wall area is still insufficient. Full-core crossing and
macroscopic inactive-container branches remain open.
