**Level I:** OPEN

The candidate closing chain under examination was

```text
high density + collective rigidity -> an exact unit triangle
  -> use a rigid periodic realization of a finite global source
  -> Level I contradiction.
```

The first implication is false, even on triangular tori with unbounded site
counts. The second implication also needs a theorem: fixed-period constraints
are not the actual diameter constraints of a finite Euclidean source. The
family below proves the first failure exactly. A finite global square example
certifies the latter distinction. No other worker's solution is used.

## 1. An explicit family on triangular tori

For an integer m>=3 set

\[
 N=m^2-1,\quad d=m^2-m+1,\quad
 c=\frac{m^2-4m+1}{2d},\quad
 s=\frac{\sqrt3\,N}{2d}.
\]

Then -1/2<c<1/2, s>0, and c^2+s^2=1. Define unit vectors

\[
 u=(1,0),\qquad v=(c,s)
\]

and the full planar unit lattice P=Z u+Z v. Its chosen period lattice is

\[
 \Lambda_m=\mathbb Z(mu+v)+\mathbb Z(u+mv).
\tag{1}
\]

Its quotient has exactly N sites, since the index matrix has determinant N.
The two period generators have the same squared length

\[
 M=m^2+1+2mc=N^2/d
\]

and inner product

\[
 2m+(m^2+1)c=M/2.
\]

Thus Lambda_m is a triangular period lattice. It is not silently identified
with the primitive lattice P; these are different lattices.

For integer a,b, the squared length of au+bv is

\[
 a^2+b^2+2cab.
\]

If ab!=0, this is strictly greater than a^2+b^2-|ab|>=1. On the two axes
it equals one only at (a,b)=(+/-1,0),(0,+/-1). Hence the packing is unit
separated, and its entire actual unit-contact graph uses only +/-u,+/-v.
That graph is bipartite and contains no unit triangle.

Its disk density, for radius-1/2 disks, is

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_m=\frac{\pi}{4s}
 =\frac dN\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt3}
 =\left(1-\frac{m-2}{m^2-1}\right)\delta_\triangle
 \longrightarrow\delta_\triangle.
 }
\tag{2}
\]

The minimum triangle slack in the whole infinite packing is

\[
 \gamma_m=1-2|c|>0,
 \qquad \gamma_m=3m/d\quad(m\ge4).
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed the shortest nonunit vector has squared length 2-2|c|=1+gamma_m.
Any triple with at most one unit side has slack at least 2gamma_m. A triple
with two unit sides either uses opposite vectors on one axis, giving slack
three, or two different axes, giving slack 1+/-2c. The smaller value in (3)
is attained by 0,u,v if c>=0, or 0,u,-v if c<0.

For the shortest-vector assertion, when |a|,|b|>=1 put x=|a|,y=|b| and
r=|c|<1/2. The expression is at least

\[
 x^2+y^2-2rxy=(x-y)^2+2(1-r)xy\ge2(1-r),
\]

with equality at x=y=1 and the appropriate signs. Nonunit axis vectors
have squared length at least four.

## 2. Collective jamming for the original fixed period lattice

Label the N sites by ell=j-mi modulo N for a point iu+jv. The kernel of
this map is precisely the index sublattice generated by (m,1),(1,m).
Moving by v changes ell by +1, and moving by u changes ell by -m.
Both are Hamilton cycles on the N labels, since gcd(m,m^2-1)=1.

Let w_ell be an infinitesimal velocity on the fixed torus. Every actual unit
contact is a strut, so the signed derivative along either oriented cycle is
nonnegative. Sum the derivatives around the v-cycle. The sum telescopes to
zero, so each derivative is zero and v dot w_ell is independent of ell.
The same argument on the u-cycle makes u dot w_ell independent of ell.
Since u,v are independent, all velocities are equal. Only translations remain.

Equivalently the bar rows have exact rank 2N-2: in the two scalar projections
they are the incidence matrices of two connected cycles, each of rank N-1.
Uniform positive contact weights give equilibrium at every site because the
four physical contact vectors are +/-u,+/-v.

This infinitesimal conclusion proves collective jamming. For completeness,
fix one site to remove translation. If nontrivial feasible placements approached
the original one, divide their displacement vectors by their Euclidean norm
and pass to a convergent subsequence. Each exact strut inequality yields a
nonnegative limiting first variation. The resulting normalized velocity would
be nonzero with one site fixed, contradicting the preceding calculation. Thus
the original placement is locally isolated modulo translation. A continuous
nontranslation packing motion cannot leave that isolated neighborhood.

Therefore these are **collectively jammed triangle-free packings on triangular
tori**, with N tending to infinity and density tending to the triangular maximum.
This does not assert global optimality for N sites in that torus. It also does
not assert strict jamming when the period lattice is allowed to change shape.

## 3. An exact flex after doubling the periods

On the torus with period lattice 2Lambda_m, use all 4N sites of the same P.
Let gamma=gamma_m and t=gamma/8. Define

\[
 q_{ij}=iu+jv+t(i\bmod2)Ju,\qquad J(x,y)=(-y,x).
\tag{4}
\]

This is periodic under 2Lambda_m, since both of its generators change i by
an even integer. It is not periodic under Lambda_m: the generator u+mv
changes i parity.

Old horizontal unit contacts acquire squared length 1+t^2; old vertical
unit contacts retain length one. A noncontact pair originally has length at
least sqrt(1+gamma); its relative displacement in (4) has norm at most t.
But

\[
 (1+t)^2=1+\gamma/4+\gamma^2/64<1+\gamma
 \qquad(0<\gamma\le1).
\]

Thus every originally nonunit pair remains strictly above one. This proves
every lower constraint in the infinite periodic packing, not just a bounded
sample. The motion parameter may vary continuously from zero to t. Its
velocities are nonconstant, and the minimum separation stays one through
the unchanged vertical contacts. The period lattice and its area are fixed.

Consequently P is not collectively jammed on the doubled torus. Rigidity of
a chosen fundamental period does not imply rigidity on larger covers.

There is also a density-increasing motion if arbitrary lattice strain is
allowed. For m>=4, decrease theta in (pi/3,pi/2) toward pi/3. For m=3,
increase theta in (pi/2,2pi/3) toward 2pi/3. Unit separation is preserved
and sin(theta) decreases in either case. This changes the period lattice
and distinguishes collective jamming from strict jamming.

## 4. Why this does not produce a Level I counterexample

The N sites are torus equivalence classes. Representatives 0,v,...,(N-1)v
are collinear in the plane, with diameter N-1; their period-image contacts
are not all contacts between those finite Euclidean representatives. Neither
the periodic density nor the rigidity rank certifies minimum Euclidean diameter.
Choosing other representatives does not supply the missing global certificate.

A simpler exact example already disproves the automatic rigidity transfer.
The unit square is a global four-point Euclidean diameter minimizer with
diameter sqrt(2). To check optimality, a convex quadrilateral has an angle
at least pi/2, so its adjacent sides of length at least one force a diagonal
at least sqrt(2). If one point x lies in the triangle of the other three,
write x=sum lambda_i p_i with lambda_i>=0 and sum lambda_i=1. Then

\[
 1\le\sum_i\lambda_i|p_i-x|^2
 =\sum_{i<j}\lambda_i\lambda_j|p_i-p_j|^2\le D^2/3,
\]

which gives the stronger D>=sqrt(3). Collinear sets are immediate.

Repeating squares gives the unit square lattice. The row-shear motion (4)
with u=(1,0),v=(0,1) is a valid fixed doubled-period packing flex. Restricted
to a single square, its two diagonal squared lengths are

\[
 2+2t+t^2,\qquad 2-2t+t^2.
\]

The finite-source diameter increases for every nonzero sufficiently small t.
Thus an actual finite global minimum does not make its periodic repetition
collectively jammed. Its upper-distance constraints are exactly what the
periodic argument has lost. The example is finite and does not refute Level I.
