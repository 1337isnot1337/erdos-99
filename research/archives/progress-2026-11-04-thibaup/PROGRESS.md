## What the records support, and what they leave open

- **Near-optimal triangle-free configurations:** the notes record fixed-size
  generic perturbation and rescaling with diameters tending to the optimum.
  This removes a positive diameter-gap route; it does not establish attainment
  by a triangle-free optimizer. Earlier project work and Plasma AI priority are
  recorded in the attribution ledger.
- **Density and stress mechanisms:** records develop many bounded six-sided
  Voronoi cells, frozen loaded contact paths and contact matchings. These do not
  produce an exact unit triangle. Near-unit distances and stress-bearing cores
  are insufficient. Prior KKT/stress work, including Das and earlier project
  connectivity arguments, must retain the source attribution in the ledger.
- **Finite common-midpoint descent:** the Wave134 proposal assumes an admissible
  oblique lattice with basis vectors of length one and inner product of absolute
  value below one half, a finite subset with diameter greater than the square
  root of three, and a common midpoint for all diameter pairs. It records a
  polynomial motion opening all original unit contacts while shortening the
  diameter pairs, with finite slack control. An archived Carver review accepts
  this scoped mechanism. Earlier quadratic and angular mechanisms are credited
  to the previous ROOT/browser work; Schürmann's asymptotic lattice exclusion
  is prior literature. External novelty of the finite refinement is unverified.
  None of this covers arbitrary unrestricted optimizers.
- **Signed stresses and second-order repair:** the records distinguish
  necessary finite-stress inequalities, strict second-order correction and
  treatment of weak contacts by cubic dilation. They do not establish a
  universal negative direction for all triangle-free global candidates. A
  numerical stress optimum or a successful local motion is not a global proof.
- **Restricted lattice flexes:** Wave133 contains an index-range error in a
  claimed exact-contact flex and Meitner's enlarged-range repair. Ohm's recorded
  finite irrational-angle exclusion is also restricted to its lattice setting.
  Those are local mechanisms for nonglobal/restricted candidates, not
  counterexamples to the original eventual statement.
- **Corner and component barriers:** the corner estimates give a scoped
  diameter-degree bound of order diameter to the two-thirds power. Matching
  triangle-free near-optimal examples and earlier component-translation work
  already existed in the project. These examples are not certified global
  optimizers. The ledger corrects rediscovery and priority claims.
- **A local-trap warning:** an archived Meitner review accepts a regular odd
  unit-polygon rim with oblique triangle-free interior bulk as an ordinary local
  diameter minimum, and an explicit lattice comparison makes it nonglobal.
  The boundary can carry all the stress while interior points remain slack.
  A later connected three-bridge variant has an unavailable native follow-up;
  acceptance of the disconnected construction does not certify that variant.
- **Algebraic/finiteness routes:** a Raman algebraic reformulation is recorded,
  together with an objection to optimizer-closure irreducibility. Label symmetry
  and mandatory unit pairs already force reducibility in the proposed setting;
  component counting alone supplies no new global bridge.
- **Conditional finite multiplicity:** the supplied-source audit pins
  `Naveen18888/Erdos-99-with-Finite-Multiplicity` at
  `f6d89fb989e9328649652c6dd4b376842929c7ad`. The recorded Part02 and Part03
  dependencies use full configurations/distance patterns, and Part09 is
  conditional on the required finiteness. A rigid core does not imply finite
  full patterns: the recorded eight-point heptagon-with-rattler example varies
  the latter continuously. Galileo, Poincaré and Dewey's earlier discovery,
  definition, build and dependency audits are credited in the source ledger.
  The exporter did not rebuild Lean or prove the finiteness premise.
