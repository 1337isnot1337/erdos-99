\[
\boxed{

This is a four-currency statement entirely in actual source objects.  No outer-walk

The local geometry carried by the two new currencies is quantitative:

## 1. Metric detour pays every non-shallow tooth

Consider one genuine two-edge outer tooth.  Audited Section 126 identifies it with an
actual augmented triangular pocket

\[
a-x-b,
\qquad |a-x|=|x-b|=1,
\qquad 1<|a-b|\le2,
\]

whose artificial base `ab` is a nonunit loaded force-hull side and whose apex `x` is
unloaded.

Write

\[
\angle axb=\pi-\eta,
\qquad 0<\eta<2\pi/3.
\]

The isosceles-triangle identity gives

\[
|a-b|=2\cos{\eta\over2}.
\]

Since the distinguished outer route has length exactly two, this tooth contributes
exactly

\[
2-|a-b|
=2\left(1-\cos{\eta\over2}\right)
\tag{OT.7}
\]

to `d_out`.  The right side is increasing in `eta`.  Therefore every tooth with apex
angle below `pi-epsilon`, equivalently `eta>epsilon`, contributes at least
`d_epsilon` to the total outer detour.

If `N_2(epsilon)` is the number of `epsilon`-shallow teeth, then

\[
\boxed{
N_2-N_2(\epsilon)
\le {d_{out}\over d_\epsilon}.
}
\tag{OT.8}
\]

No asymptotics enter this step.

## 2. Shallow teeth have distinct actual apices

The triangular pocket is empty of positive-core vertices.  Hence at its apex the two
tooth contact rays are consecutive in the cyclic contact-ray order and bound an empty
angular sector of size at least `pi-epsilon`.

Suppose two distinct `epsilon`-shallow teeth shared the same apex `x`.  The two pockets
have different loaded hull bases, so they determine two distinct empty cyclic contact
sectors at `x`.  Audited tooth exactness says `x` is unloaded, and positive equilibrium
at an unloaded positive-core vertex gives contact degree at least three.  Thus there is
at least one further cyclic contact gap.

Triangle-freeness makes every cyclic gap between consecutive unit-contact rays strictly
larger than `pi/3`; equality would give an exact unit equilateral triangle.  Therefore

\[
2\pi
>
2(\pi-\epsilon)+{\pi\over3}.
\]

For `epsilon<pi/6` the right side exceeds `2pi`, a contradiction.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{different `epsilon`-shallow teeth have different unloaded source apices.}
}
\tag{OT.9}
\]

This is the source-level injection missing from the occurrence-safe tooth theorem.

## 3. A shallow apex has contact degree three or four

Fix one shallow tooth apex `x`.  Its tooth sector has angle

\[
A=\pi-\eta,
\qquad 0<\eta\le\epsilon.
\]

All remaining contact rays lie in the complementary angular sector of size

\[
2\pi-A=\pi+\eta\le\pi+\epsilon.
\]

If `deg_C(x)>=5`, the complementary sector contains at least four consecutive contact
ray gaps.  Each is strictly larger than `pi/3`, so their sum exceeds `4pi/3`, while
`pi+epsilon<7pi/6`; impossible.  Since `x` is unloaded, positive equilibrium also gives
`deg_C(x)>=3`.  Thus

\[
\boxed{\deg_C(x)\in\{3,4\}.}
\tag{OT.10}
\]

By (OT.9), if `V_4^{tooth}(epsilon)` denotes the number of degree-four shallow apices,
then

\[
N_2(\epsilon)
=V_3^{tooth}(\epsilon)+V_4^{tooth}(\epsilon).
\tag{OT.11}
\]

## 4. Degree-three shallow apices have a weak third contact

At a degree-three shallow apex let the cyclic gaps be

\[
A=\pi-\eta,
\qquad B,
\qquad C,
\]

with the tooth-side contact bars bordering `A` and the third contact ray between the
other two gaps.  Positive equilibrium among three unit directions gives the standard
sine-law ratios

\[
\alpha_y:\alpha_a:\alpha_b
=
\sin A:\sin B:\sin C.
\tag{OT.12}
\]

Triangle-freeness gives `B,C>pi/3`, while

\[
B+C=\pi+\eta\le\pi+\epsilon.
\]

Hence

\[
B,C<2\pi/3+\epsilon.
\]

For `epsilon<pi/6`, throughout that interval

\[
\sin B,\sin C
\ge \sin(\pi/3-\epsilon),
\qquad
\sin A=\sin\eta\le\sin\epsilon.
\]

This proves (OT.5).

## 5. Degree-four shallow apices generate many distinct hidden pairs

At a degree-four shallow apex the complementary sector is split into three cyclic gaps

\[
\beta_j={\pi\over3}+e_j,
\qquad e_j>0,
\qquad e_1+e_2+e_3=\eta\le\epsilon.
\tag{OT.13}
\]

Each consecutive pair of contact neighbors across one of these three gaps is an actual
source pair with common unit-contact center `x`.  Its distance is

\[
d_j=2\sin{\beta_j\over2}.
\]

Triangle-freeness gives `d_j>1`, while (OT.13) gives the upper bound (OT.6).
Thus each degree-four shallow apex generates three inactive hidden-pair occurrences.

A fixed unordered source pair `{u,v}` can be generated by at most two distinct centers,
because every generating center lies in the intersection of the two unit circles
centered at `u` and `v`, and two circles have at most two intersection points.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
|P_4^{tooth}(\epsilon)|
\ge {3\over2}V_4^{tooth}(\epsilon).
}
\tag{OT.14}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
V_4^{tooth}(\epsilon)
\le {2\over3}|P_4^{tooth}(\epsilon)|.
\tag{OT.15}
\]

Combining (OT.8), (OT.11), and (OT.15) gives

\[
N_2
\le
V_3^{tooth}(\epsilon)
+{2\over3}|P_4^{tooth}(\epsilon)|
+{d_{out}\over d_\epsilon}.
\tag{OT.16}
\]

## 10. Frontier effect

The outer-boundary obstruction has now been converted into four local source objects.
A closing theorem no longer needs to reason directly about repeated outer-walk tooth
occurrences.

The remaining mechanisms are concrete:

1. **weak trivalent tooth apices:** exploit a linear family of positive contacts whose
   third KKT weight is small relative to two almost-antipodal unit bars;
2. **degree-four tooth apices:** activate or exchange a linear set of distinct inactive
   near-unit pairs, with aggregate activation gap `o(D)` on the vanishing-detour branch;
3. **loaded degree slack:** charge the forced missing loaded contacts into the existing
   loaded-chord / diameter budgets; or
4. **strong inward contacts:** compare a linear family of loaded-to-unloaded contacts
   with inward projection `>sqrt(3)/2` against the near-extremal diameter-thrackle
   geometry.

No one of these consequences is yet a strict sub-`D` source or an exact unit triangle.
But the previous metric long-side and tooth-occurrence escapes have both disappeared
from the final inequality.
