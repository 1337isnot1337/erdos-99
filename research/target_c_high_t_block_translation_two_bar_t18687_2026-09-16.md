# Target C-II — every contiguous block of the high-`t` cusp chain has an exact one/two-bar aggregate reaction

**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Status:** proof-only high-`t` structural strengthening
**Scope:** the fixed-length cusp chain in the canonical full-core/no-macroscopic-container Type-II branch with `t=(q-k)/D<=1.8687`
**Dependencies:** fixed-length high-`t` cusp chain; exact O--O shore cutsets and interval support; common diameter-routing hub; common-transversal/rooted-shore sign; adjacent strip opening/reaction geometry
**Certificate:** `research/verify_target_c_high_t_block_translation_two_bar_t18687.py`
**LEVEL I STATUS:** `STILL OPEN. C-II REMAINS OPEN.`

Fix `r` and take the `r+1` consecutive rows

\[
\tau_0,\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_r
\]

from the fixed-chain theorem. They lie on one side of the common diameter-routing hub. This note shows that the adjacent-strip translation is not intrinsically adjacent: **every contiguous union of layers in the chain can be translated as one rigid slab**, and its complete diameter obstruction is the disjoint union of the switching sets at the interfaces inside that slab.

The result is an interval family of exact finite obstruction problems. For every pair

\[
0\le p<q\le r,
\]

the slab between `tau_p` and `tau_q` has:

\[
\boxed{
|H_{p,q}|<1{,}109{,}000r,
\qquad
\sum_{h\in H_{p,q}}\beta_h
<{1{,}109{,}000r\over D},
}
\tag{BT.1}
\]

an exact nonzero KKT reaction generated by one or two actual bars from `H_{p,q}`, and physical contact-opening width

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{p,q}
<{1.416\times10^7r\over D}+o(D^{-1}).
}
\tag{BT.2}
\]

If the aggregate obstruction is genuinely two-bar, its two bars have projective direction gap below the same inverse-diameter bound and corresponding endpoint clusters of diameter

\[
\boxed{
<1.416\times10^7r+o(1).
}
\tag{BT.3}
\]

Because switching sets at different chain interfaces are disjoint, the one/two aggregate reaction bars come from at most two interface tags. Thus an arbitrarily long fixed chain carries a **two-bar interval reaction system**: every one of its `r(r+1)/2` contiguous block translations is obstructed by at most two actual diameter bars, even though successive single-strip reactions use fresh bars.

## 1. A nonadjacent slab is the difference of two nested shores

Use the rooted far-shore indicators `chi_j` of the selected O--O cuts. On one side of the hub the shores are nested. For `p<q`, put

\[
R_{p,q}:=S_p\setminus S_q,
\qquad
\mathbf1_{R_{p,q}}=\chi_p-\chi_q.
\tag{BT.4}
\]

The O--O shore cutset theorem says that a positive contact crosses the boundary of `S_j` exactly when it is a contact edge of `tau_j`. Therefore every contact edge with exactly one endpoint in `R_{p,q}` belongs to one of the two outer boundary trains:

\[
\boxed{
\delta_C(R_{p,q})=E(\tau_p)\cup E(\tau_q).
}
\tag{BT.5}
\]

The selected rows are distinct internally noncrossing trains, so the two boundary edge sets are disjoint. Contacts on intermediate rows have both endpoints inside the slab or both outside it and do not move relatively.

Translate every source vertex in `R_{p,q}` by one vector `v`. Then every old contact outside the two boundary trains is invariant exactly, while the two boundary contact vectors become

\[
a+v,\qquad b+v,
\tag{BT.6}
\]

when oriented from the fixed exterior into the translated slab.

Exactly as for one adjacent layer, arbitrarily small nonzero vectors open both boundary contact families. If `a+b!=0`, a sufficiently small positive multiple of `a+b` works; if `b=-a`, any sufficiently small nonzero vector perpendicular to `a` works.

## 2. The moving diameter set is exactly the union of internal switching sets

For an oriented source pair `h=xy`, define rooted cut incidence

\[
\varepsilon_{hj}=\chi_j(y)-\chi_j(x).
\]

Under slab translation its relative vector is

\[
r_h(v)=r_h+\delta_h^{p,q}v,
\qquad
\delta_h^{p,q}:=\varepsilon_{hp}-\varepsilon_{hq}.
\tag{BT.7}
\]

Because the chain lies on one side of the hub, active diameter sets are nested toward the hub. Suppose for definiteness that the chain lies on the `j<k` side. Every switch is then a unique interval **start**. Thus

\[
\delta_h^{p,q}\ne0
\]

exactly when the unique start of `h` occurs at one of the interfaces

\[
p,p+1,\ldots,q-1.
\]

The right side of the hub is identical with starts replaced by ends. Therefore, with `H_{sw,i}` denoting the switching set at interface `i`,

\[
\boxed{
H_{p,q}
:=\{h:\delta_h^{p,q}\ne0\}
=\bigsqcup_{i=p}^{q-1}H_{sw,i}.
}
\tag{BT.8}
\]

The union is disjoint by the one-sided hub theorem.

Every moving actual diameter bar has exact inequality

\[
\boxed{
|r_h(v)|^2
=D^2+2n_h\cdot v+|v|^2\le D^2,
}
\tag{BT.9}
\]

where `n_h:=\delta_h^{p,q}r_h` is oriented from the fixed exterior endpoint into the translated slab.

All nonswitching source-pair distances are invariant exactly under this slab translation.

## 3. Uniform finite support and KKT mass for every subblock

The sharpened fixed-chain theorem controls the **whole chain** rather than each interface separately:

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}N_i<1{,}109{,}000r,
\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}B_i<{1{,}109{,}000r\over D}.
\tag{BT.10}
\]

Using the disjoint union (BT.8), every subblock is a sub-union of that same whole-chain support. Hence

\[
|H_{p,q}|
=\sum_{i=p}^{q-1}N_i
\le\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}N_i
<1{,}109{,}000r,
\tag{BT.11}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{h\in H_{p,q}}\beta_h
=\sum_{i=p}^{q-1}B_i
\le\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}B_i
<{1{,}109{,}000r\over D}.
\tag{BT.12}
\]

This proves (BT.1). The complete moving diameter system of **every** contiguous subblock is controlled by the same `O(r)` support and `O(r/D)` switching-mass budget; there is no quadratic loss from the block length.

## 4. The wide-slab reaction is nonzero

Sum KKT equilibrium over all source vertices in `R_{p,q}`. Internal contact and diameter forces cancel. The only contact forces crossing the slab boundary are the two train families, so for positive boundary contact masses `A_{p,q},B_{p,q}>0`,

\[
\boxed{
G_{p,q}
=A_{p,q}a+B_{p,q}b
=\sum_{h\in H_{p,q}}\beta_h n_h.
}
\tag{BT.13}
\]

On one side of the common hub, every moving diameter bar joins a slab endpoint to an endpoint in the same hub-side outer region. All hub-side exterior endpoints therefore lie in one cyclic interval of the convex-hull vertices, while all slab endpoints lie in the complementary interval.

The convex-position separation lemma used in the adjacent reaction-nondegeneracy theorem supplies a linear functional `phi` such that, with the orientation in (BT.9),

\[
\phi(n_h)>0
\qquad(h\in H_{p,q}).
\tag{BT.14}
\]

All actual diameter multipliers are positive. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\phi(G_{p,q})
=\sum_{h\in H_{p,q}}\beta_h\phi(n_h)>0,
}
\tag{BT.15}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{G_{p,q}\ne0.}
\tag{BT.16}
\]

Thus the balanced zero-reaction degeneration is absent for every contiguous slab, not only for adjacent strips.

## 5. Every slab reaction compresses to one or two actual diameter bars

Equation (BT.13) places the nonzero vector `G_{p,q}` in the positive cone generated by the actual moving diameter vectors. Planar conic Caratheodory therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
G_{p,q}
=\lambda_1n_{h_1}+\lambda_2n_{h_2},
\qquad
\lambda_j>0,
}
\tag{BT.17}
\]

using one or two bars from `H_{p,q}`, with the second term omitted when unnecessary.

For any tangent direction `w` opening both outer boundary contacts,

\[
a\cdot w\ge0,
\qquad
b\cdot w\ge0,
\]

so

\[
G_{p,q}\cdot w
=A_{p,q}(a\cdot w)+B_{p,q}(b\cdot w)\ge0.
\tag{BT.18}
\]

Therefore the same one/two reaction bars form a tangent obstruction certificate for the slab translation. This agrees with the interval-Helly reduction applied directly to the diameter inequalities (BT.9).

By (BT.8), each selected bar has a unique interface tag. Hence (BT.17) uses bars from at most two of the interfaces `p,...,q-1`.

## 6. Every slab has an inverse-diameter physical opening cone

The fixed-chain theorem supplies a common oriented transverse ladder through all selected rows. Its rooted-shore sign theorem therefore identifies the real row directions with the physical rooted shore vectors simultaneously across the block.

For the slab between rows `tau_p,tau_q`,

\[
a=u_p,
\qquad
b=-u_q.
\tag{BT.19}
\]

Every selected row satisfies `S_j<1,109,000r` by the whole-window budget. Hence the non-telescoping block phase estimate gives

\[
|\alpha_p-\alpha_q|
<{2(1{,}109{,}000)r\over0.1567D-o(D)}.
\tag{BT.20}
\]

The exact certificate verifies

\[
{2(1{,}109{,}000)\over0.1567}
<1.416\times10^7.
\tag{BT.21}
\]

Since `a` and `b` are oppositely rooted boundary vectors, the physical contact-opening arc has width exactly

\[
\varepsilon_{p,q}=|\alpha_p-\alpha_q|.
\tag{BT.22}
\]

This proves (BT.2), uniformly for **every** contiguous subblock of the fixed chain.

## 7. Genuine aggregate two-bar reactions have bounded endpoint clusters

If a one-bar certificate exists for a slab, retain that branch. Otherwise choose the two-bar representation in (BT.17); because no single bar is a certificate, the pair is minimal.

The audited two-bar geometry theorem gives projective direction gap

\[
\eta_{p,q}\le\varepsilon_{p,q}.
\tag{BT.23}
\]

Orient the two unordered diameter segments to realize `eta_{p,q}`:

\[
y_j-x_j=Dv_j,
\qquad j=1,2.
\]

Quantitative diameter uniqueness gives

\[
|x_2-x_1|,\ |y_2-y_1|
\le2D\sin(\eta_{p,q}/2)
\le D\eta_{p,q}.
\tag{BT.24}
\]

Use (BT.2) to obtain (BT.3).

Thus every genuine aggregate TB2 obstruction, no matter how many consecutive layers are translated together, is represented by two almost-parallel actual diameter bars with two `O_r(1)` endpoint clusters.

## 8. Interval reaction normal form

The extracted `r`-chain has `r(r+1)/2` nonempty contiguous interface intervals. For each one, Sections 1--7 give

```text
one rigid translation of the entire contiguous slab
+ only its two outer row-contact families move
+ every other old contact is exactly invariant
+ moving diameters = disjoint union of the tagged switching sets inside the interval
+ <1,109,000 r moving actual diameter bars
+ <1,109,000 r / D switching KKT mass
+ exact nonzero aggregate KKT reaction
+ reaction/tangent obstruction generated by <=2 actual moving bars
+ the <=2 bars come from <=2 interface tags
+ physical opening width <1.416e7 r / D + o(1/D)
+ genuine TB2 endpoint clusters <1.416e7 r + o(1).
```

This removes another possible multi-strip regression. A long fixed chain cannot require a large collection of diameter bars to block the rigid translation of one contiguous subblock: **two bars always suffice at the aggregate level**. The remaining question is whether one can choose a slab whose one/two aggregate blockers are incompatible with the simultaneous local reaction structure, the convex-hull thrackle order, or a multi-shear perturbation.

No such incompatibility is claimed here.
