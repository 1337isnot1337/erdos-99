## Candidate lemma and why it can change the route

Consider a pure simply connected strict-rhombic disk with two distinct components
surviving the exact Section-50.1 high-defect deletion and degree pruning. Suppose
each contains a complete coherent `m`-by-`m` crossing grid with positive PC1
trim depth `d=min(k,l)-2`. Let `V_12` be the noncrossing determinant mass between
their train sets, `P_12` the determinant mass of physical crossings between
them, and `H` and `J` the canonical boundary support-depth and support-turning
terms. The route-relevant question is whether positive `d`, deleted physical
crossings and the support terms force a direct payment of `V_12` in the Target-B
shell ledger. This changes the cross-component part of `M` and the `G+E`
endpoint.

The explicit candidate test is the generous unit-for-unit rule

`V_12 <= P_12 + (2E + H - J) + a0 B`,

where `a0=sqrt(3)/2`, `B` is the rhombic boundary-edge count, and `E` is the
face-area excess. A proof would give a direct two-grain payment. A counterexample
would show that the support correction and the boundary-scale allowance do not
by themselves couple `V_12` to the endpoint. The stop condition is one exact
scalable geometry with every physical, deleted, virtual and boundary term
displayed; no L-patch enumeration or GPI repetition is in scope.

## Construction

Let `m>=12` be divisible by four, `q=m^2`,

`p=floor(q/sqrt(3))+1`, `t=p/q`,
`c=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2)`, and `s=2t/(1+t^2)`.

These are exact rationals, `t>1/sqrt(3)`, `0<c<1/2`, `s^2+c^2=1`, and
`theta=2 atan(t)` lies in `(pi/3,pi/2)`. Put

`x=(1,0)`, `y=(0,1)`, `v=(-c,-s)`, `u=(-s,c)`.

Tile the union of three edge-to-edge blocks:

- Grain 1 has vertices `i*x+j*v`, `0<=i,j<=m`, and its `m^2` rhombi are
  generated by `x,v`.
- Grain 2 has vertices `i*y+j*u`, `0<=i,j<=m`, and its `m^2` rhombi are
  generated by `y,u`.
- The interface is the single unit square generated by `x,y` at the origin.

Grain 1 shares the unit edge `[0,x]` with the square; grain 2 shares `[0,y]`.
Grains 1 and 2 meet only at the origin. Their interiors lie in `y<=0` and
`x<=0,y>=0`, respectively; the square lies in `x>=0,y>=0`. The union is a
simply connected disk. Its boundary is one cycle with `8m` unit edges.

Every tile is a strict rhombus: the two grid types have acute angle `theta`,
and the interface is square. The point set is unit-separated. Within each
grid, a difference with both integer coordinates nonzero has squared length
strictly greater than one because `2c<1`; axis differences have integer length.
Between grain 1 and grain 2, write the points as

`(i-cj,-sj)` and `(-sk,l+ck)` with nonnegative integers `i,j,k,l`.

Their vertical gap is `sj+l+ck`. If `j>=2`, this is greater than one since
`2s>1`. If `j=1` and either `k` or `l` is positive, it is greater than one
since `s+c>1` and `s+1>1`; if
`(j,k,l)=(1,0,0)`, the nearest horizontal gap is `c`, giving squared distance
`c^2+s^2=1`. When `j=0`, `k>=2` gives horizontal gap at least `2s>1`,
`k=1,l=0` gives nearest gaps `s,c` and squared distance one, `k=1,l>=1`
has vertical gap at least `1+c>1`, and `k=0` is an integer vertical/axis
comparison. In the `j=1,k=l=0` case, the next horizontal coordinate `i=1`
has squared distance `2(1-c)>1`; all larger `i` are farther. The same
nearest-coordinate check against the four square vertices shows that every
unit contact is a tile edge. More explicitly, outside the square edges and
the grid edges along the shared axes, the only square-to-grain contacts are
the two `v`-edges from `(0,0)` and `(1,0)` into grain 1; grain 2 has the
rotated pair. The contact graph is bipartite:
each rectangular grid is bipartite by coordinate parity, and the colorings agree
on the two shared edges and at the origin. Hence there is no unit-contact
triangle. The verifier checks all pair distances and contacts with rational
arithmetic for `m=12,16,20`.

The vertex, edge and face counts are

`N=2(m+1)^2`, `E_cell=4m^2+4m+2`, `R=2m^2+1`,
`N-E_cell+R=1`, and `B=8m`.

The two grid patches and one square are a disk, with no other unit contacts.

## Section 50 and positive PC1 depth

Opposite-edge equivalence gives `4m` train tracks: `m` in each of the
directions `x,v,y,u`. The crossing graph has two `K_(m,m)` blocks with `m^2`
edges each and one interface edge. The internal crossing defect is
`delta=theta-pi/3`. Since `t-1/sqrt(3)<1/m^2` and the derivative of
`2 atan(t)` is at most two, `0<delta<2/m^2`. The interface defect is `pi/6`.

Take Section-50.1 parameters `eta=1/2` and `lambda=1/4`. The diameter `D`
satisfies `m<=D<4m`: the source contains a length-`m` grid side. The full
coordinate box has width `(1+s)m<2m` and height `(s+1+c)m<5m/2`, so its
diagonal is less than `sqrt(41/4)m<4m`. For this family
with `m>=12`, `delta<2/m^2<1/(2 sqrt(m))<D^(-1/2)`, while
`D^(-1/2)<=1/sqrt(m)<pi/6`. Thus the internal edges survive the initial
deletion and the one interface edge is deleted. After that deletion every train has degree
`m>=lambda D`, so pruning removes no vertices. The final retained graph therefore
has exactly two components, each of size `2m`, minimum degree `m`, and internal
grid count `m^2`. The high-defect sum is `2m^2 delta+pi/6=O(1)`, consistent with
the Section-50 linear budget.

In each component take trims `k=l=m/4`. The retained shores have size `m/2` and
`d=m/4-2>0`. This satisfies the positive PC1 trim-depth hypothesis. However
each trimmed vector sum has norm at most `m/2`, so
`|U+V|<=m<=D`; the PC1 excess `d(|U+V|-D)_+` is exactly zero. The positive depth
does not create a positive PC1 crossing charge in this family.

## Complete physical, deleted and virtual ledger

Let `Q_12` denote the determinant sum over every pair of trains in the two
retained components. Their direction classes each have size `m`:

`H_1: x,v`; `H_2: y,u`.

The four cross determinants are `1,c,c,1`, so

`Q_12=2(1+c)m^2`.

Exactly one cross-component pair is a physical crossing: the square interface
pair `x,y`, of determinant one. It is deleted by the initial high-defect step.
All other cross-component train pairs are noncrossing. Therefore

`P_12=1`, `E_del,12=1`, and `V_12=2(1+c)m^2-1`.

Every nonzero within-component determinant is a physical rhombus, and the only
missing nonzero determinant pairs are these cross-component pairs. Thus the
global Section-26.4 missing-crossing mass is exactly `M=V_12`. This is quadratic
while the deleted physical cross-component cost is one.

The physical rhombus determinant area is

`S=2s m^2+1`,

and the total train-zonotope determinant sum is `S+M=2(s+1+c)m^2`. Thus the
physical and virtual terms remain separate and satisfy the exact zonotope
identity.

## Boundary and support-depth ledger

The six hull vertices, scaled by `m`, are

`(-c,-s), (1-c,-s), (1,0), (0,1), (-s,1+c), (-s,c)`.

In this cyclic order their consecutive edge-turn determinants are
`s, s+c, s-c, s, s-c, s+c`, all positive (each determinant carries the common factor `m^2`). The origin lies strictly to the
left of each oriented edge. The only other distinct corner candidates are the
four square corners: the origin is interior, and `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(1,1)`
are convex combinations of the origin and the scaled hull vertices for `m>=12`.
Thus these six points are exactly the hull vertices.

Their shoelace area is `(1+2s)m^2`; hence the convexification area is

`G=(1+2s)m^2-S=m^2-1`.

The face-area excess is

`E=2m^2(s-sqrt(3)/2)+1-sqrt(3)/2`.

The support depths `d_e=h_K(nu_e)-nu_e dot midpoint(e)` sum to
`H=(4+2c)m^2-2`. The nonzero boundary contributions are:

| Boundary portion | Count | Depth per edge |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Grain-1 bottom, excluding its shared edge | `m-1` | `(1+c)m` |
| Grain-1 non-hull side in direction `v` | `m` | `(1+c)m` |
| Grain-2 side on `x=0`, excluding its shared edge | `m-1` | `m` |
| Grain-2 non-hull side in direction `u` | `m` | `m` |
| Interface top edge | `1` | `(1+c)m-1` |
| Interface right edge | `1` | `m-1` |
| Four hull-facing grid sides | `4m` | `0` |

The sum of the canonical support-turning terms is therefore

`J=H-2G=2(1+c)m^2=Q_12=V_12+1`.

This is the exact support correction: the raw support depth contains the whole
quadratic cross-component determinant sum, but the canonical identity
`H-J=2G` removes it from the Oler shell quantity.

The Target-B endpoint left side is

`2E+H-J=2(G+E)=2(1+2s-sqrt(3))m^2-sqrt(3)`.

Since `c=1/2+O(m^-2)` and `s=sqrt(3)/2+O(m^-2)`,
`V_12-(2E+H-J)=m^2+O(1)`. Even after adding the full boundary allowance
`a0 B=4 sqrt(3)m` and the deleted determinant `P_12=1`, the proposed
unit-for-unit payment fails for all sufficiently large `m`:

`V_12 - [P_12 + (2E+H-J) + a0 B]`
`=2(c-2s+sqrt(3))m^2-4sqrt(3)m+sqrt(3)-2 = m^2-O(m)>0`.

This rejects that scalar payment rule under the stronger grid, trim and
Section-50 hypotheses. It does **not** contradict the shell endpoint. In fact,
`G+E=(1+2s-sqrt(3))m^2-sqrt(3)/2`; for `m>=12`,
`G+E>m^2-sqrt(3)/2>2sqrt(3)m=(sqrt(3)/4)B`, so the disk satisfies the
endpoint lower bound and violates the strict global-minimizer shell upper
bound. This family is a geometric regression, not a global-minimizer example.
