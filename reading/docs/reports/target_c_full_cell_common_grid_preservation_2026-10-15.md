## 1. Every attachment accesses its complete cell at linear cost

P. For any `a in A` and `z in I`, that audit proves an actual contact path,
with no other loaded internal vertex, of length

`ell <= (4/sqrt(3)) |a-z| + 15 N + 5 Q_sigma`,

where N counts nearby actual attachments and Q_sigma is the nonnegative
degree-excess sum on augmented faces met by the segment. The same canonical
audit records the **single global** budget

`sum_I Q_I^+ <= 2(e_BB-k)`.

cap, and `q<=per(F)<=pi D`. Also `|a-z|<=diam(P)<=D`. Therefore every such
attachment and target, in every cell, has a route satisfying

`ell <= C_route D`, where `C_route=4/sqrt(3)+30pi`.

This direct finite consequence of the fixed-port theorem and global budget
justifies the dirty source's large-fixed-allowance full-cell conclusion.

## 2. One interior grid for the selected component's return ports

`area(P)>=alpha D^2`, `diam(P)>=d0 D`, and uniformly boundedly many cells.
As in the source, one may take `d0=sin(pi/12)/4` eventually.
For a diametral segment of length d and perpendicular cell width w,
`area(P)<=d w`, so `w>=alpha D`. One side of the line reaches height at
least w/2. Its triangle with the diametral segment has area at least
`d0 alpha D^2/4` and perimeter at most `3D`, hence inradius at least
`r0 D`, where `r0=d0 alpha/6`. Choose a disk of that radius inside P and
its concentric half-radius disk Q.

The nonnegative uniform cell deficit `area(P)-A_(4,I)=o(D^2)` leaves a
quadratic amount of actual unloaded-only rhombic area in Q. Faces crossing
its boundary lie in a fixed-width tube; unit-rhombus diameter and positive
area bound their number by O(D). Fixed-phase coherent cuts remove only
O(D) further faces and add O(D) interface perimeter. The remaining physical
components have `sum R_j=Omega(D^2)` and `sum sqrt(R_j)=O(D)` by planar
isoperimetry. Consequently one has `R_j=Omega(D^2)` and perimeter O(D).

## 3. Endpoint-inclusive rigidity-to-grid linkage

For any connected unloaded core vertex set U, triangle-free planar block
counting gives `2|U|-e(U)>=diam(U)+2`. A 2-connected block contributes at
least half its outer perimeter, which bounds its Euclidean diameter;
bridges contribute one each. Summing along a block-tree path between a
diametral pair proves the bound, including singleton and bridge cases.
If fewer than `diam(U)+2` contacts left U, its internal flex space would
have greater dimension than the crossing-row count. A nonzero flex could
then be extended by zero to the entire mixed core framework. U is unloaded,
and two distinct fixed actual diameter endpoints exclude a rigid-motion
extension, contradicting canonical `C-ZRF`. Hence

`|delta_C(U)|>=diam(U)+2`.

Let X be the connected actual grid subgraph and S **any** attachment-to-X
vertex cut, including cuts deleting loaded or grid terminals. Every
component U_j of the graph minus S meeting `X\S` is wholly unloaded and
has every leaving contact end in S. At most five contacts meet any source
vertex, so

`sum_j(diam(U_j)+2) <= 5|S|-2e(S)`.

A simple quotient path of the connected grid between a diametral pair
visits each selected component at most once. Its internal displacement is
at most that component's diameter; at most two entry/exit unit edges are
paid by its extra two units. Direct S-to-S edges are used at most once and
number at most e(S), already debited twice above. Thus

`diam(X)<=sum_j(diam(U_j)+2)+e(S)<=5|S|`.

This includes both endpoints in S, the whole grid deleted, one remaining
component or many, and a path entirely in S. Endpoint-inclusive Menger
therefore gives at least `diam(X)/5` distinct-endpoint attachment-to-grid
paths. The grid's distinct rhombi have total area at least
`(sqrt(3)/2)c_grid^2 D^2`, so isodiametry gives
`diam(X)>=gamma D`, with `gamma=c_grid sqrt(2sqrt(3)/pi)`.
The same full-source Oler quantiles apply to this separate linkage.
Its starting attachment labels need not match the return endpoints.

## 4. Finite composition bound, including overlapping source and target sets

In any finite graph, let A be disjoint from P and T, while P and T may
overlap. Suppose there are r fully vertex-disjoint P-to-A paths and s
fully vertex-disjoint A-to-T paths, counting endpoints in the disjointness.
Let N be the number of A labels. Then

`lambda(P,T)>=max(0,ceil((r+s-N)/2))`.

For every endpoint-inclusive P-to-T separator S, let R be the vertices
reachable from `P\S` after S is removed. At most |S| paths in each supplied
family meet S. Every untouched P-to-A path ends in `A intersect R`;
every untouched A-to-T path starts in `A\(R union S)`. Consequently

`r<=|S|+|A intersect R|`,
`s<=|S|+|A\(R union S)|`,
`r+s<=2|S|+N-|A intersect S|<=2|S|+N`.

Menger, including zero-length paths at `P intersect T`, proves the claim.
The original factor-three bound follows. No disjointness between the two
different linkage families is required. In the source application a positive
bound still requires `|A_I|<(kappa_I+gamma/5)D` with linear margin, or a
separate common-label/direct-linkage theorem. No canonical input here
supplies that attachment upper bound. No face payment or motion follows.

The factor two is sharp even for a triangle-free unit-contact graph. Use
seven labels at `(-2,0),(0,1),(-1,0),(1,0),(0,-1),(2,0),(0,0)`, with
P the first two, A the next two and T the following two. Both supplied
linkages have size two, but every P-to-T path meets the central label and
their maximum linkage has size one. Here A is an abstract label set,
**not** actual diameter loading. The source is nonglobal: seven sites of
a 3-by-3 square grid have diameter squared at most eight, below its sixteen.
This is a graph-composition regression only.
