## 1. Exact closure chain and outcomes

The proposed positive mechanism is

```text
actual eight-point rhombic patch with cos(theta)<=1/4
  -> finite one-vacancy transport inside the old source hull
  -> every retained lower pair and every upper pair remains feasible
  -> a stress-deleting vacancy can be moved along a supplied patch path
  -> cardinality restoration only after a separate full-source refill.
```

The transport arrow is proved here. The refill arrow is not: the same six fixed guards admit exactly the original pair of weakly separated interior sites, and admit only one strictly guarded site. Thus the hop creates no spare capacity inside its own cell.

Three mechanisms were tested. Straight sliding fails except in the square case. A curved one-point hop works above the exact angle threshold. Simultaneous two-point replacement within the fixed cell is rigid at the level of point sets, so a successful equal-cardinality endpoint must move guards or use another region. These are finite source statements, not scaffold substitutions.

## 2. The actual eight-point patch

Let `u,v` be unit vectors, choose `n=J u` with positive orientation, and write

```text
v=c u+s n,   0<=c<1/2,   s=sqrt(1-c^2)>0.
```

Thus `theta=arccos(c)` lies in `(pi/3,pi/2]`. Define the six cyclic guards

```text
g_0=-u, g_1=-v, g_2=u-v, g_3=2u, g_4=u+v, g_5=v,
```

and the two interior sites `p=0`, `q=u`. Let `F` be the convex hexagon with these six guards. In axial coordinates `x=a u+b v`,

```text
F = { |b|<=1, -1<=a+b<=2, -1<=a-b<=2 }.
```

Replacing `v` by `-v` leaves this guard set unchanged. Thus the results also apply to an obtuse unit basis with `pi/2<theta<2*pi/3`, after using `c=|u dot v|` and choosing the orientation of `n` accordingly.

Suppose a finite unit-separated actual source `A` contains all eight displayed sites. Additional source points may be arbitrary; they are not assumed to lie in this lattice. Let `D=diam(A)`.

The lattice generated by `u,v` is unit-separated: for nonzero integer `(a,b)`,

```text
|a u+b v|^2 = a^2+b^2+2cab >= 1.
```

Indeed, when both coefficients are nonzero this is at least `a^2+b^2-|ab|>=1`, with strict inequality if opposite signs could make that integer lower bound equal one, because `2c<1`. When one coefficient vanishes, its square is at least one.

For `c<1/2`, equality occurs only for the four axial unit directions. Consequently the eight-point patch has nine unit contacts and no unit triangle. Its diameter is three, realized by `{-u,2u}`. The patch itself is not asserted globally optimal.

### 2.1 Complete interior accounting

The following eight triangles triangulate `F`:

```text
(-u,-v,0), (-u,0,v), (-v,u-v,0), (0,u-v,u),
(0,u,v), (u,u+v,v), (u,u-v,2u), (u,2u,u+v).
```

Each has two unit sides and third side of squared length `2-2c` or `2+2c`. For the acute triangle with angle `theta` between the unit sides, the circumradius squared is `1/(2+2c)`. For the triangle with angle `pi-theta>=pi/2`, the circle whose diameter is the longest side encloses the triangle and has radius squared `(1+c)/2`. Since

```text
1/(2+2c) <= (1+c)/2 < 1,
```

every triangle has its vertices in a circle of radius at most `R_0=sqrt((1+c)/2)<1`.

If triangle vertices `z_i` lie in a radius-`R` circle about `o`, and `x=sum lambda_i z_i` is a convex combination, then

```text
sum_i lambda_i |x-z_i|^2
 = sum_i lambda_i |z_i-o|^2-|x-o|^2 <= R^2.
```

At least one vertex is within `R` of `x`. Applying this to the triangulation shows that every point of `F` is within `R_0<1` of one of the original eight labels. Hence

```text
A intersect F = {six guards, 0, u}.
```

In particular, no unaccounted core label or contact isolate lies in the cell interior.

### 2.2 Weak guard shield against every outside source label

The longest guard side has squared length `L^2=2+2c<3`. Let `x` be in the cell interior and at distance at least one from every guard. For any retained source point `y` outside `F` that is not a guard, the segment from `x` to `y` exits the wall at a side `ab` or a vertex.

At a side exit `z=(1-t)a+tb`, the exact weighted-square identity gives, for `w=x,y`,

```text
|w-z|^2=(1-t)|w-a|^2+t|w-b|^2-t(1-t)|a-b|^2
        >=1-L^2/4.
```

The two lengths add along the segment, so

```text
|x-y| >= 2 sqrt(1-L^2/4)=sqrt(2-2c)>1.
```

At a vertex exit, both distances to that vertex are at least one and add to at least two. No nonvertex source label can lie on a wall side, because a side shorter than two puts every interior side point within one of an endpoint. This is the weak-guard version of the weighted identity used in canonical `C-NONCONTACT-WALL-STRICT-SHIELD`; the strict edge cap supplies the strict exterior margin.

Every outside label, including every contact isolate, is covered by this argument.

## 3. Exact finite vacancy-hop threshold

**Theorem H (proved candidate / finite regression).** Delete the label at `u`, keep every other source label fixed except the label at `0`, and attempt to move that label from `0` to `u` while maintaining unit separation. Such a continuous hop is possible if and only if

```text
c<=1/4, equivalently theta>=arccos(1/4).
```

In the possible range there is an explicit path that satisfies every lower pair among retained labels and keeps diameter exactly the original `D` throughout. Its endpoint has coordinate set `A minus {0}` and cardinality `|A|-1`. It transfers a vacancy; it does not restore cardinality.

### 3.1 Constructive path when c<=1/4

Parameterize the path by the Euclidean `u` coordinate `t in [0,1]`. On `0<=t<=2c`, set

```text
y(t)=s-sqrt(1-(t-c)^2),
gamma(t)=t u+y(t)n.
```

On `2c<=t<=1-2c`, set `gamma(t)=t u`. On the last interval use central symmetry,

```text
gamma(t)=u-gamma(1-t).
```

The first arc runs on the unit circle about `v`, from `0` to `2c u`; it has `s-1<=y(t)<=0`. The last arc is its image under `x -> u-x`, and the middle segment is nonempty exactly when `c<=1/4`. The formula also covers `c=0`, when both arcs collapse and the path is straight.

On the first arc:

- distance to `v` is exactly one;
- distance squared to `-v` is `1+2|gamma|^2>=1`;
- the centers `v` and `u-v` have squared separation `5-4c>=4`, so distance to `u-v` is at least one;
- distance squared to `u+v` is at least `(1-c)^2+s^2=2-2c>=3/2`;
- distance to `-u` is at least one;
- distance to `2u` is at least `2-2c>=3/2`.

On the middle segment the potentially tight guard inequalities are

```text
|t u-v|^2-1=t(t-2c)>=0,
|t u-(u-v)|^2-1=(1-t)(1-t-2c)>=0.
```

The other four guards are farther away. Central symmetry permutes all six guards, proving the last-arc inequalities.

The path lies inside the old cell. On the first arc write `gamma=a u-b v`, where `b=-y/s>=0` and `a=t+cb>=0`. Since `s>=3/4`, `b<=1/3`, and

```text
a+b <= 2c+(1+c)(1-s)/s <= 1/2+5/12=11/12<1.
```

Thus the first arc is inside `conv{0,u,-v}`, the middle segment is in `[0,u]`, and the last arc is in the reflected triangle. All are contained in `F`.

Section 2.1 accounts for every interior source label. Section 2.2 supplies strict separation from all other exterior labels. Since all moving and fixed positions stay in `conv(A)`, whose diameter equals `D`, every upper pair—including a formerly inactive pair that might become active—has length at most `D`. This completes the full retained-source pair ledger.

Neither `0` nor `u` is an extreme point of the source hull, so neither can be an actual diameter endpoint, by the strict convexity identity in Proposition G below. Every original diameter pair is consequently fixed and retained; diameter stays exactly `D`. A separate reduced-source descent is still necessary after the hop.

### 3.2 Obstruction when 1/4<c<1/2

Every point of a guard-wall side lies within `L/2<1` of at least one guard. A continuous unit-clear path starting inside `F` therefore cannot exit `F`.

Any path from `0` to `u` must cross the line with Euclidean `u` coordinate `1/2`. At a crossing point `x=(1/2)u+y n`, the cell gives `|y|<=s`. If `y>=0`, use the guard `v`; if `y<=0`, use `u-v`. In either case the squared distance is at most

```text
(1/2-c)^2+s^2=5/4-c<1.
```

The crossing is impossible. This proves necessity independently of any upper constraints or any extra source labels. At `c=1/4`, the only admissible bisector crossing is `u/2`, where the two critical guard disks are tangent; the constructed weak path passes through that point.

## 4. Exact two-site capacity throughout the rhombic range

Define

```text
E={x in F: |x-g_i|>=1 for all six guards},
E_strict={x in F: |x-g_i|>1 for all six guards}.
```

**Theorem C (proved candidate).** For every `0<=c<1/2`,

```text
E subset {x: |x-u/2|<1/2} union {0,u}.
```

Hence a unit-separated two-point subset of `E` is exactly `{0,u}`. The weak guarded capacity is two with that unique point set; the strict guarded capacity is one. The statement allows arbitrary replacement coordinates, not just lattice sites or points along the hop.

### Proof of the containing disk

The cover in Section 2.1 puts each point of `E` within `R_0<1` of either `0` or `u`, since it is at least one from each guard. Central symmetry preserves `E`, so assume `x` is within `R_0` of `0`. Set `r=|x|`. For `r=0` the point is `0`.

For `r>0`, let `w=s u-c n` be the unit vector perpendicular to `v` with `u dot w=s`, and write `x=a v+b w`. The guards `v,-v` imply

```text
|a|<=r^2/2,
```

and the guard `-u` implies `u dot x>=-r^2/2`. We first show `b>0`. Otherwise

```text
u dot x = c a+s b
 <= c r^2/2-s r sqrt(1-r^2/4) < -r^2/2,
```

where the strict inequality is equivalent to `r^2<2(1-c)`, true because `r^2<=R_0^2<3/4<1<2(1-c)`. This contradicts the guard at `-u`.

With `b>0`, concavity of `a -> c a+s sqrt(r^2-a^2)` on `[-r^2/2,r^2/2]` gives

```text
u dot x >= -c r^2/2+s r sqrt(1-r^2/4).
```

The right side is positive: after squaring its two nonnegative terms, this is equivalent to `r^2<4s^2`, which holds since `r^2<3/4` and `4s^2>3`. Set `X=u dot x`, `Y=n dot x`. We have proved `0<X<1`, with the upper bound following from `|x|<1`.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that `x` lies outside or on the diameter disk. Set

```text
Z=r^2-X>=0,   h=1/2-c>0,   k=1/2+c<1.
```

Then `0<=Z<1` and `Y^2=X(1-X)+Z`. When `Y>=0`, the guards `v` and `u+v` give

```text
sY <= hX+Z/2,
sY <= k(1-X)+Z/2.
```

When `Y<=0`, the guards `-v` and `u-v` instead give

```text
-sY <= kX+Z/2,
-sY <= h(1-X)+Z/2.
```

In either case the two right sides are nonnegative and their product is at most

```text
hk X(1-X)+(k/2)Z+Z^2/4.
```

Multiplying the two inequalities and substituting `Y^2` gives

```text
(s^2-hk)X(1-X)+(s^2-k/2)Z-Z^2/4 <= 0.
```

But `s^2-hk=3/4`, and

```text
s^2-k/2-1/4=(1/2-c)(1+c)>0.
```

The left side is therefore at least `3X(1-X)/4+Z(1-Z)/4>0`, a contradiction. We have proved `Z<0`, equivalently `|x-u/2|^2<1/4`. Applying central symmetry deals with the points covered from `u`, completing the containing-disk proof for every `c<1/2`.

Two points in the displayed disk union can be at distance at least one only when both lie on its boundary; those boundary points in `E` are precisely `0,u`. The old pair is feasible, so the weak capacity is exactly two.

For strict capacity, all strict sites lie in the open diameter disk and no two can be unit-separated. A strict site exists: take `x=delta w`, where `delta=(1-2c)/100>0`. Its squared distances to `v,-v` are `1+delta^2`, and the contact at `-u` opens strictly. The other guards started at distance at least `d_min=sqrt(2-2c)>1`, and

```text
d_min-1=(1-2c)/(d_min+1)>(1-2c)/3>delta.
```

Their distance after the displacement still exceeds one. The axial coefficients are `delta/s` and `-c delta/s`; their absolute sum is at most `1/50`, using `delta<=1/100`, `1+c<3/2`, and `s>3/4`. Thus the point is inside `conv{0,u,-v}`. Strict capacity is exactly one.

This result does not follow by applying canonical `C-PARALLELOGRAM-UNIT-GUARD-CENTER-BOUND` to the four guards `-v,u-v,u+v,v`: that parallelogram has a side of length two, outside the canonical theorem's `sqrt(3)` cap. The additional two guards and the eight-site cover supply the missing localization here.

The initial proof used only the lower projection estimate above. It gives `X>=r^2` when `r^2<=T(c)=(1-c^2)/(5/4+c)`. The identity `T(c)-(1+c)/2=3(1-4c)(1+c)/(10+8c)` makes that argument sufficient only up to `c=1/4`. The exact certificate retains this identity and checks the additional guard that excludes the coarse estimate's critical equality point. The four-guard product argument is what extends the theorem to the whole rhombic range; extrapolating the first estimate does not.

### Full-source meaning

The weak shield proves every site in `E` is strictly separated from every retained exterior source label. The cell contains no extra original label, and `F subset conv(A)` proves every upper distance is at most `D`. Nevertheless the only two-site weak replacement inside the unchanged cell is the original point set. A strict packet of size two cannot restore the deleted label. This is a source-compatible capacity obstruction, not an inference from Oler allowance.

**Corollary B (proved candidate, fixed-guard bank obstruction).** Suppose an actual source contains `r` patches of this type, possibly with different angles in the whole rhombic range, whose cell interiors are pairwise disjoint and whose guards are all retained. Replace only their `2r` interior labels, keeping all new positions in the union of the cells and every other label fixed. A unit-separated same-cardinality replacement has exactly the old coordinate set.

Indeed, each new position belongs to one cell and has clearance from its six retained guards. Theorem C gives at most two new positions in each cell. Since `2r` labels must be placed, every cell receives two, forcing its original pair. Cell boundaries admit no unit-clear position, so there is no boundary ambiguity in this assignment. The resulting coordinate set has unchanged diameter and contact count. This also rules out a nonconstant equal-cardinality path supported only in such a fixed-guard bank. The statement does not apply after moving guards or placing labels outside the bank.

### Triangular limiting check

At the excluded triangular endpoint `c=1/2`, the same cell has `E={0,u}` and strict capacity zero. To verify the limiting claim, use the eight-triangle cover to place a candidate within radius `sqrt(3)/2<1` of `0` or `u`. Near `0`, the three unit guard directions `-u,v,u-v` have pairwise angles `2*pi/3`. For any nonzero vector of length `r`, at least one of these directions has dot product at least `r/2`; its unit-clear inequality requires that dot product to be at most `r^2/2`. Hence `r>=1`, contradicting the cover. Symmetry handles `u`. The strict site in the open rhombic range approaches an old site as `c` approaches `1/2`; no angle-uniform strict margin is claimed.

## 5. Sequential vacancy transfer and stress scope

Construct a graph on actual source sites by declaring an edge eligible when its endpoints and six guards form one of the above exact patches with `c<=1/4`. A single vacancy can be transferred along any supplied path in this graph. At each stage the coordinate set is `A` minus the current vacant site, all six guards of the next hop remain present, and the preceding proof applies to the same original hull and arbitrary outside labels. This proves joint sequential feasibility, including every retained contact isolate. No eligible path is extracted from the canonical geometry here.

The moving labels are permuted relative to the original site labels. At a hop endpoint, statements about KKT stresses use the bijection from retained labels to their current original-source coordinates. This is a reindexing of the endpoint configuration, not an assertion that a transported label kept its old coordinates.

**Proposition G (proved candidate, transport geometry).** Every vertex of this eligible graph is an unloaded actual contact vertex of degree exactly four, with its contact directions in two opposite unit pairs. Each connected component lies in one translate of one rhombic lattice with one fixed pair of unit directions and one fixed absolute cosine `c<=1/4`.

For an edge represented by `0,u`, the four contacts at `0` are precisely `-u,v,-v,u`, and those at `u` are precisely `0,2u,u+v,u-v`. All other displayed patch pairs are strictly above one; Section 2.2 excludes any exterior contact, and Section 2.1 excludes another interior label. Both endpoints are nontrivial convex combinations of guards. For any source point `y`, strict convexity gives

```text
|x-y|^2 = sum lambda_i |g_i-y|^2
          -sum lambda_i |g_i-x|^2 < D^2
```

at either endpoint `x`, using a convex representation involving at least two distinct guards. Hence neither endpoint supports an actual diameter pair.

At a vertex shared by two eligible edges, the first patch already determines its full four-neighbor contact set. The second edge direction must be one of those directions, and its transverse unit direction must be the other pair. Thus the two patches have the same unordered pair of unoriented unit directions. Induction along any graph path gives the fixed pair, and every edge displacement belongs to `{u,-u,v,-v}`. All component coordinates consequently belong to a translate of `Z u+Z v`.

In particular, this fully guarded transport graph cannot carry a vacancy onto an actual loaded endpoint or across a change of rhombic phase. A move that reaches such a site needs a different local cell or must change a guard.

**Proposition T (conditional source theorem, forced-channel transport).** Consider a supplied straight path of eligible edges in one fixed direction `u`. Suppose every normalized nonnegative source KKT stress assigns a positive contact weight to an edge of its `u` channel. Then deleting any vertex on that path kills all normalized source KKT stresses. Vacancy transport along the path therefore preserves the stress-transversal property even when the normalized stress polytope is not a singleton.

At every path vertex, unloaded equilibrium has the form

```text
(alpha_{+u}-alpha_{-u})u+(alpha_{+v}-alpha_{-v})v=0.
```

Independence of `u,v` forces equality in each opposite pair. Along the path, shared edge weights therefore make the `u`-channel weight constant in each stress, including the two end-guard contacts. If a stress survived deletion of a path vertex, extending it by zero on all deleted contact rows would give a normalized source stress: all diameter rows are retained because the vertex is unloaded, and equilibrium at the removed vertex is zero. The extended stress has zero channel weight, contradicting the hypothesis. Canonical `C-FINITE-KKT-STRICT-MOTION-ALTERNATIVE` then gives strict reduced-source descent at each hop endpoint.

The positivity hypothesis is on every normalized stress, including proper support faces. Full positive support of one relative-interior stress does not imply it. It is a finite source-specific support condition that can be checked by asking whether the KKT polytope intersects the zero-channel face; no assertion here says canonical geometry makes that face empty.

If the normalized canonical stress polytope is a singleton with full positive support, deleting any core vertex kills every normalized stress: a surviving stress would extend by zeros to that singleton while assigning zero to a removed positive contact row. Hence a vacancy walk through core vertices in this special branch relocates a stress-killing deletion to every reachable vertex. Canonical `C-FINITE-KKT-STRICT-MOTION-ALTERNATIVE` then supplies reduced-source strict descent at each endpoint. For a higher-dimensional stress polytope, vacancy transfer alone need not preserve the stress-transversal property.

Canonical rhombi only have angle strictly above `pi/3`; this does not supply the stronger `arccos(1/4)` threshold or the eight-point neighborhood at a desired deletion site. Furthermore Theorem C and Corollary B prevent a refill inside an unchanged hop cell or a disjoint bank of them. The exact next obligation is to extract a suitable vacancy path together with an actual moved-wall or outside-cell replacement that restores the deleted population and passes the complete lower/upper ledger.
