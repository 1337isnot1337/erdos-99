**Date:** 2026-09-09

**Run type:** exact tree-path / difference-body normal form  
**Target:** cyclic primitive phase `d_H=1`, P1  
**Depends on:** PR twin completions, AE rectangle-diagonal leaf theorem, corrected RC support-event theorem, TC two-interval cut theorem  
**Computation:** none

## LEVEL I STATUS

`STILL OPEN`

PR defines the common KKT core

$$
J=P_H(a,d)\cap P_H(b,c)
$$

inside the P1 diameter tree. AE shows that each rectangle diagonal `ac,bd` is incident to a full-H leaf. The leaf information determines the endpoints of `J` exactly.

More importantly, the corrected RC proof does not actually need `beta_e=0` once `e` lies in `J`: common-cycle membership, P1 all-circularity, and the full-tree cut suffice. Hence **every** edge of `J` carries an exact local diameter-lens cell.

### Theorem JP — common-path normal form

Let

$$
\ell_{ac}\in\{a,c\},\qquad x\in\{a,c\}
$$

be respectively the leaf and nonleaf endpoints of the diameter edge `ac`, and define

$$
\ell_{bd}\in\{b,d\},\qquad y\in\{b,d\}
$$

analogously for `bd`. Then:

1. each rectangle diagonal has **exactly one** leaf endpoint;
2. the PR common path is exactly
   $$
   \boxed{J=P_H(x,y);}
   \tag{JP.1}
   $$
3. its Euclidean endpoint distance is
   $$
   \boxed{|x-y|\in\left\{1,\sqrt{D^2-1}\right\};}
   \tag{JP.2}
   $$
4. LF/PR gives
   $$
   \boxed{|E(J)|\ge2;}
   \tag{JP.3}
   $$
5. every edge `e=sp` of `J` is a strict circular difference-body state lying on the odd cycle of **both** P1 antipodal refinements;
6. deleting any `e in J` partitions the rectangle vertices as
   $$
   \boxed{\{a,c\}\mid\{b,d\};}
   \tag{JP.4}
   $$
   the two components each occupy exactly two cyclic shell intervals;
7. at the two endpoints of `e`, its two incident support events cross this component cut. If those unit transition sides are `pp'` and `ss'`, then
   $$
   \boxed{|p's|=|ps'|=D,}
   \tag{JP.5}
   $$
   and
   $$
   \boxed{|p's'|=D-\frac1D,\qquad p's'\parallel sp.}
   \tag{JP.6}
   $$
8. for every **interior** edge of `J`, neither endpoint is a rectangle vertex, so the two event sides are exactly the two moving transitions of the four-block cut. Only the two end edges of `J` can use one of the fixed rectangle sides `ab,cd` as an event side.

Thus P1 has a single diameter path `J` joining two marked rectangle vertices at distance either `1` or `sqrt(D^2-1)`, and every one of its diameter edges is locally flanked in projective direction by exact minimum-gap diameter states generated by cut-transition unit sides.

---

## 1. Each rectangle diagonal has exactly one leaf endpoint

AE proves

$$
\min\{\deg_H(a),\deg_H(c)\}=1,
\qquad
\min\{\deg_H(b),\deg_H(d)\}=1.
\tag{1.1}
$$

Suppose both endpoints of `ac` had degree one. Then the connected component of the tree `H` containing `a` and `c` would consist only of the edge `ac`. Since the cyclic post-ZF regime has `q>2`, this contradicts connectedness and spanning of `H`.

Therefore exactly one endpoint of `ac` is a leaf and the other is nonleaf. The same applies to `bd`.

This defines `ell_ac,x,ell_bd,y` uniquely.

---

## 2. Compute the intersection of the two completion paths

Recall

$$
J=P_H(a,d)\cap P_H(b,c).
$$

The leaf edge `ac` can occur in a tree path only when one endpoint of that path is the leaf endpoint `ell_ac`. After that first/last edge, the path enters the tree through the nonleaf endpoint `x`. Likewise every path involving the leaf endpoint of `bd` enters the remaining tree through `y`.

There are four leaf-pattern cases, according to whether the leaf endpoint of `ac` is `a` or `c` and whether the leaf endpoint of `bd` is `b` or `d`. In every case, after removing the optional first/last leaf edges `ac,bd`, both paths

$$
P_H(a,d),\qquad P_H(b,c)
$$
contain the same unique core path

$$
P_H(x,y),
$$
while their remaining leaf-edge pieces are disjoint.

For example, if `a` and `b` are the leaf endpoints, then

$$
P_H(a,d)=ac\cup P_H(c,d),
$$

$$
P_H(b,c)=bd\cup P_H(d,c),
$$

so their intersection is `P_H(c,d)=P_H(x,y)`. The other three cases are identical after swapping the endpoints inside one or both rectangle diagonals.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{J=P_H(x,y).}
$$

This proves JP.1.

---

## 3. The two possible endpoint distances

The nonleaf endpoint `x` is one of `a,c`, and `y` is one of `b,d`.

The P1 rectangle has

$$
|ab|=|cd|=1,
$$

and

$$
|ad|=|bc|=\sqrt{D^2-1}.
$$

Thus the four possible choices of `(x,y)` give exactly two metric values:

- `x=a,y=b` or `x=c,y=d`: `|x-y|=1`;
- `x=a,y=d` or `x=c,y=b`: `|x-y|=sqrt(D^2-1)`.

This proves JP.2.

PR/LF already gives at least two distinct zeroable diameter coordinates in `J`, so JP.3 follows.

---

## 4. No rectangle diagonal belongs to `J`

Because `J=P_H(x,y)` and `x,y` are the nonleaf endpoints, the leaf edge `ac` cannot lie on `J`: traversing `ac` would enter its leaf endpoint and terminate there, but neither endpoint of `J` is that leaf. Likewise `bd` is not in `J`.

Therefore every `e in J` corresponds to a strict K-vertex away from the unique P1 merged K-edge. Both incident K-edges are ordinary unit Minkowski edges.

Moreover `e` lies on both tree paths `P_H(a,d)` and `P_H(b,c)`, hence on the unique odd cycle of each maximal completion

$$
H+ad,
\qquad
H+bc.
$$

This proves JP.5.

---

## 5. Every J-edge has the same four-block component partition

Fix

$$
e\in E(J).
$$

Since `e` lies on `P_H(a,d)`, deleting `e` separates `a` from `d`. Since it also lies on `P_H(b,c)`, it separates `b` from `c`.

But `ac` and `bd` remain full-H edges distinct from `e`. Hence

$$
a\sim c,
\qquad
b\sim d
$$
in `H-e`.

Therefore the two components contain the rectangle vertices exactly as

$$
\{a,c\}\mid\{b,d\}.
$$

This proves JP.4.

The four rectangle vertices alternate around the shell as `a,b,c,d`. Hence neither component can occupy a single cyclic interval. TC says each component of a convex-thrackle tree cut occupies at most two intervals. Therefore each occupies exactly two intervals, giving exactly four contact-cycle transitions.

This conclusion uses only `e in J`, not any KKT-zero condition.

---

## 6. Corrected RC applies to every J-edge

Orient `e=sp` from the `B` component to the `A` component.

Section 4 says `e` is a strict circular K-state on both odd cycles. Therefore its two incident support events change opposite endpoints of `sp`.

Let the `p`-event replace `p` by its shell neighbor `p'`. The neighboring K-state represents `p's`. P1 has no off-circle K-vertices, so

$$
|p's|=D.
$$

If `p'` remained in the `B` component, `p's` would be a second full-H edge crossing the cut of the tree edge `e`, impossible. Hence `p' in A`, and `pp'` is a cut-transition side.

Similarly the `s`-event replaces `s` by a shell neighbor `s' in B`, with

$$
|ps'|=D,
$$
and `ss'` another cut-transition side.

This proves JP.5.

The exact diameter-lens calculation from corrected RC then gives, after orienting the two neighboring projective directions on opposite sides of `e`,

$$
|p's'|=D-\frac1D,
\qquad
p's'\parallel sp.
$$

This proves JP.6.

Thus every edge of the entire common path, not just an endpoint-facet edge of the KKT segment, carries the regular local cell.

---

## 7. Only path-end edges can touch a fixed rectangle transition

The only rectangle vertices which can lie on `J=P_H(x,y)` are its endpoints `x,y`; the other two rectangle vertices are the leaf endpoints of `ac,bd` and cannot lie on the core path.

Therefore an interior edge of `J` has neither endpoint among `a,b,c,d`. Its two support-event shell sides cannot be the fixed rectangle sides `ab` or `cd`. They are therefore exactly the two moving transitions of its four-block component cut.

An end edge of `J` is incident to `x` or `y`. If the corresponding color block is a singleton, the support event at that rectangle vertex may use the fixed transition `ab` or `cd`; this is precisely the singleton case missed in the first RC version.

Hence all exceptional event choices are confined to the two path ends.

## 8. Mechanical consequence

Every KKT-coordinate facet still lies in `J`, but JP shows that the geometric structure of `J` is independent of which edges actually reach beta zero.

Write

$$
J:v_0=x,v_1,\ldots,v_m=y,
\qquad m\ge2.
$$

Then every path edge

$$
e_i=v_{i-1}v_i
$$
has length `D`. Its two adjacent strict antipodal directions are actual diameters exactly `delta` away, generated by shell transition sides at `v_{i-1}` and `v_i`. For `1<i<m`, both event sides are moving transitions.

The remaining global problem is therefore not to discover local geometry. It is to classify how these exact cells can be stitched along one convex-thrackle diameter path whose marked endpoints satisfy JP.2.

## 9. Regression audit

- **Singleton color blocks:** allowed only at path-end rectangle vertices; interior J-edges avoid the bug corrected in RC.
- **Graph adjacency vs projective adjacency:** JP says the two K-neighbor states of each `e` are at projective gap `delta`. It does **not** claim those neighboring diameter edges are the adjacent edges of `J`.
- **Both diagonal endpoints leaves:** excluded by connectedness of H.
- **KKT dependence:** Sections 4-7 use no endpoint stress and no beta-zero assumption; they are full geometric statements about every J-edge.

## 10. Next theorem

The P1 phase is now reduced to one marked equal-diameter path:

$$
v_0=x\to v_1\to\cdots\to v_m=y,
\qquad m\ge2,
$$

with

$$
|v_i-v_{i-1}|=D,
$$

$$
|x-y|\in\{1,\sqrt{D^2-1}\},
$$

and exact local minimum-gap support cells at every edge.

The next target is a **path sign/turn equation**. Orient every diameter direction into one projective half-plane. Consecutive path traversal vectors alternate sign because adjacent path edges meet at a source vertex with angle at most `pi/3`. The ordered-caterpillar theorem makes their projective directions monotone. Therefore `x-y` is an alternating signed sum of radius-D vectors from the exact P1 direction grid.

Combining that sum with the two possible values JP.2 should give a finite trigonometric constraint on the run composition of the common antipodal cycles. This is now closer to the SC Littlewood equation but uses only the mechanically relevant core path `J`.
