```math
\tag{CRC.2}
```


## 1. Every loaded terminal lies on the C17 Reuleaux boundary

Assume the Woodall cyclic branch.  The full loaded diameter graph `H` is connected and
unicyclic, consisting of an odd cycle `Gamma` with pendant leaves.  C17 associates to
`Gamma` a unique Reuleaux polygon `R=R_Gamma` of width `D` and proves

```math
A\subset R.
\tag{1.1}
```

The cycle vertices are precisely the corners/centers defining `R`.

Let `u` be any loaded terminal outside the cycle.  Woodall says `u` is a leaf of `H`,
attached to a cycle vertex `c`.  Thus

```math
|u-c|=D.
\tag{1.2}
```

Both points lie in the diameter-`D` convex body `R`.  Therefore `u` lies on
`partial R`.  More precisely, since `R` is the intersection of the closed radius-`D`
disks centered at the cycle vertices, equality in (1.2) puts `u` on the boundary arc
of `R` generated by the disk centered at `c`.

The cycle vertices themselves lie on `partial R`.  Hence

```math
\boxed{B_+\subset\partial R.}
\tag{1.3}
```

Since `R` is strictly convex, the cyclic order of the loaded terminals is their order
on `partial R`, and

```math
F=\operatorname{conv}B_+
\tag{1.4}
```

is the inscribed polygon obtained by replacing the intervening Reuleaux boundary
subarcs by chords.

## 2. Exact subarc partition of the force hull

Enumerate the `q=|B_+|` force-hull sides in boundary order.  For side `j`, let
`phi_j>0` be the central angle of the Reuleaux boundary subarc cut off by that side.
Its two endpoints lie on one radius-`D` arc with some cycle vertex `c_j` as center.
Consequently

```math
\boxed{s_j=2D\sin(\phi_j/2)}
\tag{2.1}
```

for the force-hull side length `s_j`.

The subarcs partition the whole Reuleaux boundary.  Barbier gives
`per(R)=pi D`, so

```math
\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^q\phi_j=\pi.}
\tag{2.2}
```

Both endpoints of side `j` are at distance `D` from `c_j`.  Thus the two actual
diameter pairs from `c_j` to those endpoints share an endpoint and make angle
`phi_j`.  FS.3 gives

```math
\boxed{0<\phi_j\le\Theta_D=O(D^{-1/4}).}
\tag{2.3}
```

In particular the force-hull perimeter itself has the exact representation

```math
\boxed{P_F=2D\sum_{j=1}^q\sin(\phi_j/2).}
\tag{2.4}
```

This strengthens the skeleton-only formula of the preceding report.

## 3. One outer pocket is trapped in one circular cap

Fix a missing force-hull side `AB`.  Let its Reuleaux subarc have central angle `phi`,
side length

```math
s=2D\sin(\phi/2),
\tag{3.1}
```

and center `c`.  OP gives one convex outer contact pocket `P` whose boundary consists
of the chord `AB` and an old unit-contact chain of length `ell`.

The pocket lies in the source hull and outside the force hull on the `AB` side:

```math
P\subset K\cap H_{AB}^{out}.
\tag{3.2}
```

C17 gives `K subset R`, while `R subset \overline B(c,D)`.  Therefore `P` lies in the
closed circular segment of the radius-`D` disk bounded by chord `AB` and the
corresponding minor arc.

Call this circular segment `C_{AB}`.  Both `P` and `C_{AB}` are convex and

```math
P\subset C_{AB}.
\tag{3.3}
```

Perimeter is monotone under inclusion of planar convex bodies, so

```math
s+\ell=\operatorname{per}P
\le\operatorname{per}C_{AB}
=s+D\phi.
```

Hence

```math
\boxed{\ell\le D\phi.}
\tag{3.4}
```

Subtracting the chord length,

```math
\boxed{
0\le\ell-s
\le D\left(\phi-2\sin(\phi/2)\right)
\le {D\phi^3\over24}.
}
\tag{3.5}
```

The last inequality is `sin x>=x-x^3/6`.

No monotonicity or small-slope assumption about the contact chain is used.  The entire
argument is convex containment plus perimeter monotonicity.

## 4. Global detour is sublinear

OD/DD give the exact identity

```math
b-P_F=\sum_{P}(\ell_P-s_P),
\tag{4.1}
```

where the sum runs over missing force-hull sides / outer pockets.  Apply (3.5), enlarge
the sum to all force-hull subarcs, and use (2.2)--(2.3):

```math
\begin{aligned}
0\le b-P_F
&\le {D\over24}\sum_{P}\phi_P^3\\
&\le {D\over24}\sum_{j=1}^q\phi_j^3\\
&\le {D\Theta_D^2\over24}\sum_{j=1}^q\phi_j\\
&={\pi D\over24}\Theta_D^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
```

This proves CRC.1.  In particular

```math
{b-P_F\over D}\to0.
\tag{4.3}
```

The same subarc partition independently reproves `P_F/D->pi`.

## 5. Outer-pocket area is always subquadratic

DD.2 gives

```math
H_{out}
\le {1\over\sqrt6}(x_B+a)^{3/2}\sqrt{b-P_F}.
\tag{5.1}
```

In the cyclic branch `h=q`.  The `q` convex force-hull sides have length at least one,
so

```math
q\le P_F\le\pi D.
\tag{5.2}
```

Also `b+Q=2h=2q`, hence `b=O(D)`, `x_B=b-q=O(D)`, and `a<=q=O(D)`.  By (4.3),

```math
\sqrt{b-P_F}=o(D^{1/2}).
```

Therefore

```math
H_{out}=o(D^{3/2})o(D^{1/2})=o(D^2),
```

proving CRC.2.  Using the explicit FS.3 rate in CRC.1 gives the stronger but unused
finite order `H_out=O(D^{7/4})`.

## 7. Exact loaded-contact ledger gives the 0.952890... floor

In `s_Z=d_H=0`, PC.2 reads

```math
\boxed{e_{BB}=d_P+Q+c_B.}
\tag{7.1}
```

All terms are nonnegative.  Consequently




This bound uses a mechanism absent from the previous scalar cavity optimizations:
C17/FS geometry removes outer-pocket area **before** optimizing any cavity perimeter,
and the exact PC ledger then identifies exceptional-face defect `Q` as a subset of the

## 8. Equality-near normal form

Assume hypothetically

```math
{e_{BB}+2s_C\over D}\to\lambda_Q.
\tag{8.1}
```

Every inequality in (7.3) must be asymptotically sharp, so

```math
\boxed{{Q\over D}\to\lambda_Q.}
\tag{8.2}
```

Subtract `Q` from the exact surplus using (7.1):

```math
e_{BB}+2s_C-Q=d_P+c_B+2s_C.
\tag{8.3}
```

Since the left side is `o(D)`, nonnegativity gives

```math
\boxed{
{d_P\over D}\to0,
\qquad {c_B\over D}\to0,
\qquad {s_C\over D}\to0.
}
\tag{8.4}
```

Thus asymptotically all ordinary surplus is the exceptional-face defect `Q` itself.

Next, the exact circuit identity and CRC.1 give

```math
2q=b+Q=P_F+Q+o(D).
```
Since `P_F/D->pi`, (8.2) yields

```math
\boxed{{q\over D}\to{\pi+\lambda_Q\over2}.}
\tag{8.5}
```

PC.1, specialized to `d_H=s_Z=0`, is

```math
q=a+d_P+Q.
\tag{8.6}
```

Hence

```math
\boxed{{a\over D}\to{\pi-\lambda_Q\over2}.}
\tag{8.7}
```

Finally PC.4 becomes

```math
a=c_I+c_B+s_C+2,
\tag{8.8}
```

so

```math
\boxed{{c_I\over D}\to{\pi-\lambda_Q\over2}.}
\tag{8.9}
```

This proves CRC.4.

Additional equality information follows from (6.4)--(6.7): the attachment-cell upper
bound and exceptional-face area upper bound must both be asymptotically saturated, and

```math
H_{out}=o(D^2).
```
Therefore a putative threshold sequence has linearly many nearly triangular outer
pockets but they carry no quadratic area; the macroscopic uncovered area is forced
into the exceptional bounded faces.

## 9. Binding regressions and scope

### Regular odd shell

The regular odd shell is still retained.  It has a cyclic diameter graph but `s_Z=2`,
so the exact circuit identity and PC specialization used in Sections 6--8 do not apply.
The geometric cap statements CRC.1--CRC.2 are compatible with it.

### Dense isolated fillings

C17 traps contact-isolated points in the same Reuleaux polygon, but CRC never assumes
they are force-hull vertices or counts them in `q`.  Their presence does not invalidate
the cap containment of the actual OP pockets.

### Thin pockets

No positive area floor per pocket is used.  CRC proves the opposite kind of statement:
the Reuleaux cap limits the **total allowable detour**, which DD converts into a
subquadratic aggregate pocket area.

### Exceptional-face concentration remains possible

CRC does not prove that `Q=o(D)`.  A linear `Q` can still carry quadratic area through
a macroscopic exceptional face.  That is exactly the surviving endpoint mechanism and
must not be silently discarded.
