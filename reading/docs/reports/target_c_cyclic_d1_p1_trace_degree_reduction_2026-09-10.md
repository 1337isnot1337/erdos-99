# Erdős Problem #99 — P1 Littlewood closure forces a half-degree trace root

**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Run type:** exact algebraic reduction inside cyclic `d_H=1` P1  
**Depends on:** SC P1 Littlewood closure; PA geometric polynomial notation  
**External literature:** none  
**Computation:** none

## 1. Result

Put

$$
n:=q-2,
\qquad
z=e^{i\delta},
\qquad
0<\delta<\pi,
\qquad
t:=z+z^{-1}=2\cos\delta.
$$

SC proves that every P1 survivor supplies a Littlewood polynomial

$$
P(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_jZ^j,
\qquad
\varepsilon_j\in\{+1,-1\},
$$

with

$$
P(z)=0.
$$

PA records that the same `z` satisfies

$$
G_n(z)=0,
$$

or equivalently that `t` is a root of

$$
\boxed{
H_n(T):=D_n(T,1)+4T-6,
}
\tag{TR.1}
$$

where

$$
D_n(z+z^{-1},1)=z^n+z^{-n}.
$$

The new reduction is:

### Theorem TR — half-degree trace obstruction

Every P1 survivor must satisfy

$$
\boxed{
[\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q]
\le
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{TR.2}
$$

Consequently P1 is excluded for any `n` for which the distinguished small-angle root

$$
t_n=2\cos\delta,
\qquad
\cos(n\delta/2)=2\sin(\delta/2),
$$

of `H_n` has algebraic degree

$$
\boxed{
[\mathbb Q(t_n):\mathbb Q]
>
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{TR.3}
$$

Thus the exact remaining arithmetic target is only a **half-degree** lower bound for one real root of the degree-`n` trace polynomial. Full irreducibility of `H_n`, and degree `>=n` for `z`, are both stronger than necessary.

## 2. The minimal polynomial of `z` is reciprocal

Let

$$
M(Z)\in\mathbb Q[Z]
$$

be the monic minimal polynomial of `z` over `Q`, of degree `m`.

Because `P(z)=0` and `P in Z[Z]`,

$$
M\mid P
$$

in `Q[Z]`. Hence

$$
\boxed{m\le n-1.}
\tag{2.1}
$$

The coefficients of `M` are rational and therefore real. Since

$$
|z|=1,
$$

complex conjugation sends

$$
z\mapsto\bar z=z^{-1}.
$$

Thus

$$
M(z^{-1})=0.
$$

Because `z` is nonzero, the reversed polynomial

$$
M^*(Z):=Z^mM(Z^{-1})
$$

is nonzero and has `z` as a root. The irreducible polynomials `M` and `M^*` therefore share a root, so they are associates in `Q[Z]`. After normalizing leading coefficients,

$$
\boxed{M^*=\pm M.}
\tag{2.2}
$$

Thus the Galois conjugates of `z` are stable under inversion.

Because `z` is nonreal (`0<delta<pi`), `M` cannot have degree one. An irreducible reciprocal polynomial of odd degree has `1` or `-1` as a root: evaluating the reciprocal identity at `Z=-1` for the `+` case or at `Z=1` for the `-` case gives the linear factor. Therefore an irreducible reciprocal polynomial of degree greater than one has even degree.

Hence

$$
\boxed{m=2d}
\tag{2.3}
$$

for some positive integer `d`.

## 3. The trace field has exactly half the degree

The trace

$$
t=z+z^{-1}
$$

belongs to the real field generated by `z`. Clearly

$$
\mathbb Q(t)\subseteq\mathbb Q(z).
$$

Moreover `z` satisfies

$$
\boxed{X^2-tX+1=0}
\tag{3.1}
$$

over `Q(t)`. Therefore

$$
[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q(t)]\le2.
$$

It is not one. Under the distinguished complex embedding,

$$
t=2\cos\delta\in\mathbb R,
$$

so every element of the concretely embedded field `Q(t)` is real. But

$$
z=e^{i\delta}\notin\mathbb R.
$$

Thus

$$
z\notin\mathbb Q(t)
$$

and

$$
\boxed{[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q(t)]=2.}
\tag{3.2}
$$

By the tower law,

$$
[\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q]
=\frac12[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]
=\frac m2.
\tag{3.3}
$$

Combine this with (2.1):

$$
[\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q]
\le\frac{n-1}{2}.
$$

The left side is integral, so

$$
\boxed{
[\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q]
\le
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
$$

This proves TR.2. `square`

## 4. `t` is a root of the degree-`n` trace polynomial

PA derives

$$
G_n(z)=0,
$$

where

$$
G_n(Z)
=Z^{2n}+4Z^{n+1}-6Z^n+4Z^{n-1}+1.
$$

Divide by `z^n`:

$$
z^n+z^{-n}+4(z+z^{-1})-6=0.
$$

By the Dickson identity

$$
D_n(t,1)=z^n+z^{-n},
$$

we obtain

$$
\boxed{H_n(t)=0.}
$$

Since `H_n` has degree `n`, TR changes the target from a reciprocal degree-`2n` factor problem to the following exact statement:

> the particular root `t_n in (-2,2)` closest to `2` selected by the unsquared P1 equation must not lie in any irreducible factor of `H_n` of degree at most `floor((n-1)/2)`.

No statement about the other roots of `H_n` is required.

## 5. Relation to PA

For

$$
n=2^k,
$$

PA proves the stronger statement

$$
[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\ge n.
$$

Hence

$$
[\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q]\ge n/2.
$$

Since

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
=\frac n2-1,
$$

TR immediately recovers PA's contradiction.

The advantage is conceptual and technical: for general `n`, it is enough to obtain a trace degree lower bound just above `n/2`, which may be accessible through factor-degree information even when `H_n` is reducible.

## 6. A useful factor-language reformulation

Factor

$$
H_n(T)=\prod_j F_j(T)
$$

into irreducibles over `Q`. Let `F_{geom}` denote the unique factor containing the small real P1 root `t_n`.

Then P1 existence implies

$$
\boxed{
\deg F_{geom}
\le
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{6.1}

Therefore any theorem proving

\[
\boxed{
\deg F_{geom}\ge\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil
}
\tag{6.2}
$$

eliminates P1 for that `n`.

This is considerably weaker than the computationally suggested statement that `F_geom` has degree `n` (or `n-1` after the irrelevant `t-1` factor when `6|n`).

## 7. Regression audit

### Arbitrary unimodular Littlewood roots

TR does not claim they are roots of unity or cyclotomic. It uses only real coefficients, irreducibility, and `|z|=1`.

### Self-reciprocal versus reciprocal factor

The Littlewood polynomial `P` itself need not be self-reciprocal. Only the **minimal polynomial of its particular unit-circle root** is forced to be reciprocal.

### Real roots `z=+-1`

Excluded by the P1 geometry. The selected angle satisfies `0<delta<pi` and in fact is small, so `z` is nonreal.

### Other factors of `H_n`

Irrelevant. TR only constrains the irreducible factor containing the distinguished small root.
