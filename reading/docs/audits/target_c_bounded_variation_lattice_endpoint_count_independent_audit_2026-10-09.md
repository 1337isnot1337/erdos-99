## Exact claims

Let `A` be a finite unit-separated planar source of diameter `D`, let `B` be its set of actual diameter endpoints, and put

```math
W=\bigcap_{a\in A}\overline B(a,D).
```

### Local curved-boundary lattice capacity

There are absolute constants `C,D_0` such that, for `D>=D_0`, `c in W`, `sqrt(D)<=r<=D`, and `0<=epsilon<=1/8`, every unit-separated

```math
S\subset B\cap \overline B(c,r/2)
```

whose points are each within `epsilon` of one translate of a lattice generated by unit vectors with angle in `[pi/3,2pi/3]` satisfies

```math
|S|\le C\bigl(r\epsilon+(Dr)^{1/3}+1\bigr).
```

The constants are uniform over the source, patch center, lattice basis, orientation, and translation.

### Bounded-variation additive chart

For any sequence of finite unit-separated sources with `D->infinity`, consider one chart

```math
p_{ij}=p_{00}+iu_0+jv_0+a_i+b_j,\qquad 0\le i\le m,\quad 0\le j\le n,
```

where `u_0,v_0` are unit vectors with angle in `[pi/3,2pi/3]`, `a_0=b_0=0`, and

```math
\sum_{i=1}^m|a_i-a_{i-1}|+\sum_{j=1}^n|b_j-b_{j-1}|\le M
```

for one fixed `M`. An arbitrary subset of chart indices may be occupied, provided each occupied position is a distinct actual diameter endpoint of the full source. Then the number of occupied endpoints is at most `C_M D^(4/5)` for all sufficiently large `D`. The onset and constant may depend on `M`; they are uniform over the source, chart dimensions, occupied subset, and allowed basis.

## Independent reconstruction

### The local capacity input

Each actual diameter endpoint belongs to `partial W`, since its partner defines an active radius-`D` disk. If `o` is a diameter-pair midpoint, the two active disks give `W subset closed_ball(o,sqrt(3)D/2)`, while the concentric ball of radius `(1-sqrt(3)/2)D` lies in `W`. For `E=W intersect closed_ball(c,r)`, convex perimeter monotonicity gives `per(E)<=2 pi r`. The homothetic image of the convex hull of `c` and that inball supplies an inball for `E` of radius `(1-sqrt(3)/2)r/4`.

Set `s=(D/r^2)^(1/3)` and `t=epsilon+s`. Since `r>=sqrt(D)`, `s<=1`; for all sufficiently large absolute `D`, `t` is smaller than the local inradius. The boundary of `E` is made of circular arcs of radii at most `D`, with convex corners. Its outer parallel body at distance `t` and inner erosion at distance `t` have the same form with curvature-radius bound at most `2D`.

The divergence theorem, splitting tangent angles at `|cos(theta)|=eta` and integrating by parts on the remaining arcs, gives the uniform Fourier estimate `|hat(1_F)(xi)|<=C sqrt(D)|xi|^(-3/2)` for these parallel bodies. Smooth lattice counting with a nonnegative unit-mass bump of radius `s`, Poisson summation, and uniform separation of the dual lattices then give discrepancy `O(sqrt(D)s^(-1/2))`. Round each endpoint in `S` to its lattice point; the rounded points are distinct because `2epsilon<1`. The radius-`s` neighborhoods of these lattice points are in the outer parallel body and outside the inner erosion, by the supporting line at each point of `partial W`. The area difference is `O(r(epsilon+s)+1)` by Steiner's outer-area formula and inner-parallel coarea. Therefore

```math
|S|\le C\{r(\epsilon+s)+\sqrt D\,s^{-1/2}+1\}.
```

The selected `s` makes `rs=sqrt(D)s^(-1/2)=(Dr)^(1/3)`, proving the local claim. This is the lattice-qualified Lemma 4.1 argument from Audit.201, reconstructed here as a separate canonical input. No unqualified no-lattice count on `partial W` is used.

### Strip count for actual diameter endpoints

For any strip of width `w`, let `x_0,x_1` be actual diameter endpoints in the selected subset at the extreme strip-axis projections. For another endpoint `q`, let `q_*` be the point on `x_0x_1` with the same projection. Then `|q-q_*|<=w`. If `p` is a diameter partner of `q`, the weighted square identity gives

```math
(x-x_0)(x_1-x)
=(1-\lambda)|p-x_0|^2+\lambda|p-x_1|^2-|p-q_*|^2
\le D^2-|p-q_*|^2\le 2Dw,
```

where `q_*=(1-lambda)x_0+lambda x_1`. Thus every selected projection lies within `sqrt(2Dw)` of one extreme. Every actual diameter endpoint is strictly exposed in the full source by its partner direction: equality in the convexity/triangle argument would force every point in a convex representation to equal the endpoint. Hence the selected endpoints in each of the two end groups are in convex position. Unit separation makes each cyclic side at least one, so each group fits the perimeter bound for a rectangle of sides `sqrt(2Dw)` and `w`, with an additive two for groups of size at most two. Consequently a strip of width `w` contains at most `4sqrt(2Dw)+4w+4` actual diameter endpoints. This is the independently audited `C-ACTUAL-DIAMETER-ENDPOINT-STRIP-BOUND`.

### Bounded-variation chart count

Put `r=D^(4/5)` and `delta=r/(8D)`. Greedily partition each shore into consecutive blocks of total internal variation at most `delta`. At every cut, the variation of the preceding block together with the omitted crossing increment is greater than `delta`; these charge sets are disjoint across all cuts on both shores. Thus the number `J` of cuts is `O(M/delta+1)=O_M(D/r)`.

Let `alpha,beta` be the dual coordinate functionals of `u_0,v_0`. Their norms are at most two and at least one. Since `a_0=b_0=0`, every displacement `a_i+b_j` has norm at most `M`; its two dual-coordinate errors are therefore at most `2M`. For each half-integer shore cut, remove endpoints in the physical-coordinate strip of half-width `2M+1`, centered at the corresponding translated cut line. Its Euclidean width is at most `4M+2`. The strip theorem bounds all removed endpoints by

```math
O_M(J\sqrt D+J)=O_M(D^{7/10}).
```

Let `F` be the convex hull of the remaining counted endpoints. Every such endpoint is exposed in the full source and hence remains extreme in this sub-hull. If there are at least three, `diam(F)<=D`, `per(F)<=pi D`, and `F subset W` by convexity. The shore cut lines partition its boundary into `O(J+1)` arcs. On each open arc the physical coordinates stay on fixed sides of every cut; endpoints outside the removed strips have index coordinates on the same sides because the coordinate error is at most `2M`. Thus all counted endpoints on one arc use one fixed block from each shore.

Within a pair of such blocks, choose base indices. The displacement from their base sum is at most `2delta`, so all chart endpoints on that arc lie within `epsilon=2delta=r/(4D)` of one translated rhombic lattice. Subdivide each arc into pieces of arclength at most `r/2`, each contained in a disk of radius `r/2` centered on the same arc; these centers lie in `W`. The perimeter and arc count give at most `O_M(D/r+1)` patches. For large `D`, `sqrt(D)<=r<=D` and `epsilon<=1/8`, so the local capacity theorem bounds each patch by

```math
O\bigl(r\epsilon+(Dr)^{1/3}+1\bigr)=O(D^{3/5}).
```

The unremoved count is therefore `O_M(D^(4/5))`; the removed strips contribute only `O_M(D^(7/10))`. If the counted hull is a segment, it has at most two extreme endpoints and the result is immediate. This proves the chart theorem.
