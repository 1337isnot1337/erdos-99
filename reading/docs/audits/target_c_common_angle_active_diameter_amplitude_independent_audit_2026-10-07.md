## Audited statement

Take the canonical contact-minimal, then actual-diameter-pair-minimal triangle-free C-II representative with `D>=9`. Let `V` be its nonisolated positive-contact core, with every actual contact retained and partitioned into complete stopped trains. Let `x_tau` be a nonconstant `{0,1}` assignment to those trains and suppose a vertex field `y` on `V` is integrated with

`y_j-y_i = x_tau*(p_j-p_i)`

on every oriented contact `ij` in train `tau`. To compare with the promoted quarter-fold, write `X_i(phi)=cos(phi)*p_i+sin(phi)*U_i` and `U_j-U_i=s_tau*J(q)` on a contact edge `q=p_j-p_i`. Rotate the whole endpoint by `R_phi`, which does not change any distance. If `s_tau=+1`, the rotated edge is `R_(2phi)q`; if `s_tau=-1`, it is `q`. Thus with `theta=2phi` and `x_tau=(s_tau+1)/2`, its edge increment is `(1-x_tau)q+x_tau*R_theta(q)`, exactly the common-angle family generated by `y_j-y_i=x_tau*q`. Conversely, the promoted face-integration theorem supplies such a potential whenever its face-closure hypotheses hold. Keep every contact-isolated source label in the full configuration and set its displacement to zero.

For each actual diameter pair `h=ij`, oriented consistently in the definitions below, put

`r_h=p_i-p_j`, `s_h=y_i-y_j`, `A_h=(r_h-s_h) dot s_h`, and `B_h=r_h dot J(s_h)`, where `J` is counterclockwise rotation by `pi/2`. Let `beta_h>0` be the diameter coefficients of a relative-interior normalized KKT stress. Then

1. `sum_h beta_h*B_h=0`;
2. the values `B_h` include both a positive and a negative value;
3. if an angle `theta` with `0<theta<pi` passes every old actual-diameter upper constraint, then every `h` with `B_h>0` has `A_h>0` and

   `tan(theta/2) >= tau_+ := max_(h:B_h>0) B_h/A_h > 0`;

4. if `-pi<theta<0` passes every old actual-diameter upper constraint, then every `h` with `B_h<0` has `A_h>0` and

   `|tan(theta/2)| >= tau_- := max_(h:B_h<0) -B_h/A_h > 0`.

If any denominator required in the relevant maximum is nonpositive, that sign of angle has no feasible value. At `theta=pi` or `-pi` the separate necessary conditions are `A_h>=0` for every old actual diameter. The thresholds are finite and strictly positive for each source/pattern where their denominators are positive, but no uniform lower bound over sources is asserted.

These are necessary conditions for old diameter pairs to stay at most `D`. They do not establish the lower constraints, the other full-source upper constraints, or that any angle clearing the gap exists. In particular, contact isolates remain in the full endpoint ledger even though they do not enter `V`, the stress, or the amplitude sums.

## Independent proof

Choose a relative-interior normalized KKT stress on the canonical source. Its contact weights are positive on every contact, and its diameter weights satisfy `beta_h>0` on every actual diameter row. For each contact edge `e`, the proposed velocity `u_i=J(y_i)` has

`(p_i-p_j) dot (u_i-u_j) = (p_i-p_j) dot J(x_tau*(p_i-p_j)) = 0`.

Equilibrium, paired with this velocity, therefore cancels the contact terms and gives `sum_h beta_h B_h=0`.

If all `B_h` were zero, `u` would annihilate both every contact row and every actual-diameter row. The canonical C-ZRF mixed-rigidity theorem, whose `D>=9` hypothesis follows from the zero-transfer exclusion, makes `u` a Euclidean velocity on `V`: `u_i=b+omega*J(p_i)`. Consequently `y_i=-Jb+omega*p_i`, and each contact increment gives `x_tau=omega`. Since each bit is zero or one and the contact core is connected, every train bit would be the same. This contradicts the assumed nonconstant assignment. Thus at least one `B_h` is nonzero; positivity of all `beta_h` and their zero weighted sum forces both signs.

For a common-angle endpoint `p'_i=p_i+(Rot(theta)-I)y_i`, put `t=tan(theta/2)`. Direct expansion gives, for each labelled pair,

`|p'_i-p'_j|^2-|p_i-p_j|^2 = 4*t/(1+t^2)*(B-t*A)`.

An old actual diameter must have nonpositive change, so `t*(B_h-t*A_h)<=0`. For `t>0`, every positive `B_h` forces `A_h>0` and `t>=B_h/A_h`. For `t<0`, every negative `B_h` forces `A_h>0` and `|t|>=-B_h/A_h`. Taking the indicated finite maxima proves the two bounds. At a half-turn, `Rot(theta)=-I` and the change is `-4*A_h`, giving the stated separate condition.
