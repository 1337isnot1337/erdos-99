## Exact claim promoted

Fix positive constants $c_1,C_1,C_2$, a constant $K\ge0$, and an integer $J\ge1$. Let $D$ tend to infinity, and let $E_D$ be an intersection of finitely many closed disks whose radii are at most $C_1D$. Suppose $E_D$ contains a disk of radius $c_1D$ and is contained in a disk of radius $C_2D$; the two disk centers need not coincide. Let $X_D$ be a finite unit-separated set with

$$
\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial E_D)\le \rho_D\quad(x\in X_D),\qquad 0\le\rho_D\le1.
$$

Suppose each $x$ in $X_D$ is assigned to one of at most $J$ charts
$$
\phi_j(\lambda)=\lambda+g_j(\lambda),\qquad \lambda\in X_j\subset\Lambda_j,
$$
where each $\Lambda_j$ is generated by unit vectors with angle in $[\pi/3,2\pi/3]$, and
$$
|g_j(\lambda)-g_j(\mu)|\le (K/D)|\lambda-\mu|
$$
on its domain. More generally, allow a residual $\tau_D$ with $0\le\tau_D\le1/16$: every assigned $x$ is within $\tau_D$ of its assigned chart image $\phi_j(\lambda)$. Then

$$
|X_D|\le C_{J,K,c_1,C_1,C_2}\bigl(D^{4/5}+D\rho_D+D\tau_D\bigr)
$$
for all sufficiently large $D$. Corollary 8.1 is the exact-chart case $\tau_D=0$. No endpoint, optimality, contact, stress, or full-occupancy assumption is required.

## Independent reconstruction

The estimate follows by patching the canonical local near-boundary capacity theorem; no Fourier estimate or source geometry needs to be repeated.

First recenter at the center of the inball. If the stated containing disk has a different center, that center lies within $C_2D$ of the inball center, so the entire body lies in a disk of radius $2C_2D$ about the inball center. Thus the hypotheses of the canonical near-boundary theorem hold with a changed fixed outer-radius constant. Convex perimeter monotonicity gives $per(E_D)=O_{C_2}(D)$.

Take $r=D^{4/5}$. Cover $\partial E_D$ by arclength patches of length at most $r/4$, centered at boundary points. There are $O_{C_2}(D/r+1)$ patches. Assign each point of $X_D$ to a patch containing one of its nearest boundary points. Since $\rho_D\le1$ and $r$ tends to infinity, the assigned points lie in the radius-$r/2$ disk about that patch center for all sufficiently large $D$. Assignments are made once, so overlaps do not cause double counting.

Fix a patch and one chart, and choose one assigned point $x_0$ with label $\lambda_0$. For every other assigned $x$ with label $\lambda$, the two points lie in the same radius-$r/2$ disk, hence $|x-x_0|\le r$. If $y=\phi_j(\lambda)$ and $y_0=\phi_j(\lambda_0)$, then $|y-y_0|\le r+2\tau_D$. The displacement bound gives
$$
|y-y_0|\ge (1-K/D)|\lambda-\lambda_0|,
$$
so, for large $D$, $|\lambda-\lambda_0|\le 2(r+2\tau_D)$. Consequently $x$ is within
$$
\epsilon_D=\tau_D+\frac{2K}{D}(r+2\tau_D)
$$
of the single translated lattice $\Lambda_j+g_j(\lambda_0)$. Since $r/D\to0$, $\tau_D\le1/16$, and $K$ is fixed, eventually $\epsilon_D\le1/8$. Also $\sqrt D\le r\le D$.

Apply `C-NEAR-BOUNDARY-LATTICE-LOCAL-CAPACITY` to this unit-separated patch/chart subset, with boundary error $\rho_D$. Its size is
$$
O_{c_1,C_1,C_2}\!\left(r(\epsilon_D+\rho_D)+(Dr)^{1/3}+1\right).
$$
Summing over at most $J$ charts and $O_{C_2}(D/r+1)$ patches gives
$$
O_{J,K,c_1,C_1,C_2}\!\left(Kr+D\rho_D+D\tau_D+
 D^{4/3}r^{-2/3}+D/r+1\right).
$$
For $r=D^{4/5}$, the first and fourth terms are $O_K(D^{4/5})$, while $D/r=D^{1/5}$. The term $K\tau_D$ from summation is absorbed by $D\tau_D$ for large $D$. This proves the claim.
