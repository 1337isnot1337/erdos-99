## Exact claim

Fix a constant `K<infinity`. There is no unbounded sequence of canonical post-ZF Target C Type-II sources, with `D -> infinity`, together with a nonempty unloaded positive-contact component `I_D` in each source and a unit triangular frame

```math
U_D=\{\pm u_D,\pm v_D,\pm(v_D-u_D)\},\qquad |u_D|=|v_D|=1,
\quad \angle(u_D,v_D)=\pi/3,
```

such that every actual unit-contact edge internal to `I_D`, and every actual unit-contact edge from `I_D` to one of its distinct loaded attachments `A_{I_D}`, lies within Euclidean distance `K/D` of a member of `U_D`.

The frame may vary with the source. This is a statement about all actual contacts in one whole cell and its attachment contacts. It makes no assertion about selected grids, contacts in other cells, loaded-loaded contacts, or a common phase across different cells. All contact-isolated source points remain in the full source.

## Canonical inputs

The proof uses exactly two already canonical claims:

1. `C-UNIFORM-HARMONIC-CELL-AREA` gives, for each fixed `c<sin(pi/12)/2`, the uniform bound `|A_I| >= cD/3` eventually for every nonempty unloaded component in either C-II branch. In particular all members of `A_I` are actual diameter endpoints.
2. `C-TYPEII-FACE-TAIL-PORT-MULTIPLICITY` says that for every fixed `lambda,eta>0` there is a fixed `rho>0` such that fewer than `eta D` attachment incidences `(a,I)` fail to have an actual contact route of length `<lambda D` from `a` to every `z in I intersect closed_ball(a,rho D)`. The route starts at `a` and otherwise stays in `I`.
3. Only for the chart theorem's canonical C-II corollary, `C-ZRF` gives `h>=ceil(D)` and `C-WOODALL` gives `h<=q`, hence `q>=ceil(D)`. The general chart-count estimate itself does not require a minimizer or triangle-free hypothesis.

## Independent reconstruction

### Local curved-boundary capacity

Let `A` be the full unit-separated source of diameter `D`, and let

```math
W=\bigcap_{a\in A}\overline B(a,D)
```

be its radius-`D` ball hull. Every actual diameter endpoint belongs to `partial W`: a diameter partner supplies an active disk constraint there. If `o` is the midpoint of a diameter pair, the two active radius-`D` disks give `W subset closed_ball(o,sqrt(3)D/2)`. Every source point lies in that same outer disk, so the concentric ball of radius `c_*D`, `c_*=1-sqrt(3)/2`, lies in every defining disk and hence in `W`.

For `c in W`, `sqrt(D)<=r<=D`, and `0<=epsilon<=1/8`, I reconstructed the estimate

```math
\#S\le C\{r\epsilon+(Dr)^{1/3}+1\},
\tag{1}
```

for every unit-separated `S subset partial W intersect B(c,r/2)` whose points lie within `epsilon` of one translate of a lattice generated by unit vectors with angle in `[pi/3,2pi/3]`. Here `C` is absolute and the estimate is uniform in the basis, orientation, and translation. The triangular lattice used by the single-cell theorem is a special case.

Set `E=W intersect closed_ball(c,r)`. Its perimeter is at most `2 pi r`. Its circular boundary arcs have radii at most `D`. It has an inball of radius `c_*r/4`: take the ball of radius `c_*D` in `W`, form its convex hull with `c`, and take the homothetic copy with factor `r/(4D)`. Its center is within `sqrt(3)r/8` of `c`, so this ball lies in the patch disk. Since `r>=sqrt(D)`, for sufficiently large absolute `D` the inradius exceeds `t=epsilon+s`, where `s=(D/r^2)^(1/3)<=1`.

For any convex body whose boundary consists of circular arcs with `ds<=R dtheta` and convex corners, the divergence theorem and a tangent-angle split at `|cos(theta)|=eta` give

```math
|\widehat{1_E}(\xi)|\le C\sqrt R\,|\xi|^{-3/2}.
\tag{2}
```

On the small-cosine arcs, `ds<=R dtheta` bounds the total length by `CR eta`. On the complementary bounded number of arcs, the tangent projection is monotone with magnitude at least `eta`; integration by parts in that projection and bounded variation of the normal factor give `C/(|xi| eta)`. Choosing `eta=(R|xi|)^(-1/2)` proves (2), with the direct perimeter estimate covering `R|xi|<4`. The same estimate, with `R<=2D`, applies to the outer parallel body `E_t^+` and inner erosion `E_t^-`; their boundary pieces have radii at most `D+t` and `D-t` (or the patch-radius analogues), and their corners contribute zero arclength.

For the stated lattice class, every nonzero lattice vector has length at least one because `m^2+n^2-|mn|>=1` for nonzero integer `(m,n)`. The basis Gram matrix has eigenvalues `1±cos(theta)`, so the singular values lie in `[1/sqrt(2),sqrt(3/2)]`; the determinant lies in `[sqrt(3)/2,1]`, and the dual lattices are uniformly separated. Smooth lattice counting with a nonnegative unit-mass bump supported in the unit disk, scaled to radius `s`, then gives by Poisson summation

```math
\left|\sum_{l\in\Lambda}(1_F*\psi_s)(l)-\frac{area(F)}{det(\Lambda_0)}\right|
\le C\sqrt D\,s^{-1/2}
\tag{3}
```

for either parallel body `F=E_t^+,E_t^-`. The dual lattices are uniformly separated; dyadic shell counting with the bump's `O((1+s|\xi|)^{-3})` Fourier decay makes the nonzero Fourier series absolutely summable with the stated bound.

Round each point of `S` to its nearby lattice point; the rounded points are distinct because `2epsilon<1`. The radius-`s` ball around each rounded point lies in `E_t^+`, while the supporting half-plane at the original boundary point puts that ball strictly outside `E_t^-`. Thus the difference of the two smoothed indicators equals one at every rounded point and is nonnegative elsewhere. Steiner's outer-area formula and inner-parallel coarea give

```math
area(E_t^+)-area(E_t^-)\le 2t\,per(E)+\pi t^2\le C(rt+1).
```

Combining this with (3) bounds `|S|` by `C(r(epsilon+s)+sqrt(D)s^(-1/2)+1)`. The choices of `s` make `rs=sqrt(D)s^(-1/2)=(Dr)^(1/3)`, proving (1). This local argument counts only actual unit-separated endpoints on `partial W`; it does not assume that a curved boundary is itself a lattice chart.

### Slow lattice charts on the full diameter-endpoint set

The independent chart-count theorem is the following. Let `A_D` be any sequence of finite unit-separated sources with diameter `D->infinity`, and let `B_D` be the set of all actual diameter endpoints. Apart from `B_bad`, suppose `B_D` is covered by at most a fixed number `J` of charts

```math
b=\lambda+g_j(\lambda),\qquad \lambda\in X_j\subset\Lambda_j,
```

where each `Lambda_j` is generated by unit vectors with basis angle in `[pi/3,2pi/3]`, and for one fixed `K>=0` each displacement field obeys `|g_j(lambda)-g_j(mu)| <= (K/D)|lambda-mu|` on its chart domain. The domains and charts may vary with the source, and no bound is imposed on absolute displacement or translation. Then

```math
|B_D|\le |B_{bad}|+C_{J,K}D^{4/5}
\tag{4}
```

for all sufficiently large `D`.

To prove it, cover the boundary of `conv(B_D)` by `N<=C(D/r+1)` disks of radius `r/2`, centered on that boundary, with `r=D^{4/5}`. Actual diameter endpoints are extreme points of `conv(B_D)`: if `b` has a diameter partner `a` and `b` were a convex combination of other endpoints `b_i`, then `D=|b-a|=|\sum_i t_i(b_i-a)|<=\sum_i t_i|b_i-a|<=D`. Equality forces every `b_i-a` to lie on the same ray at distance `D`, so every `b_i=b`, a contradiction. Also `conv(B_D) subset W` and the convex perimeter bound gives `per(conv(B_D))<=pi D`; hence the centers lie in `W` and the cover bound holds. Assign every covered endpoint to a patch and one chart that covers it. In each nonempty assigned set choose `b_0=lambda_0+g_j(lambda_0)`. Any other endpoint `b=lambda+g_j(lambda)` in that same patch satisfies `|b-b_0|<=r`, so

```math
|\lambda-\lambda_0|\le |b-b_0|+(K/D)|\lambda-\lambda_0|\le2r
```

for `D>=2K`. Its displacement from the translated lattice point `lambda+g_j(lambda_0)` is at most `2Kr/D`. Since `2Kr/D=2KD^{-1/5}<=1/8` eventually, (1) bounds this patch/chart set by `C(Kr^2/D+(Dr)^{1/3}+1)`. Summing over at most `JN` sets gives

```math
|B_D\setminus B_{bad}|
\le C J(D/r+1)\{Kr^2/D+(Dr)^{1/3}+1\}
=O_{J,K}(D^{4/5}),
```

which proves (4). In particular, if `|B_bad|=o(D)`, then `|B_D|=o(D)`. For canonical C-II sources this contradicts `q>=ceil(D)`, which follows from `C-ZRF`'s `h>=ceil(D)` and `C-WOODALL`'s `h<=q`. The statement is conditional on the chart cover; it does not extract any such cover from grain or contact data.

### Contradiction for one Type-II cell

Assume such a sequence and frame exist. Fix small positive `lambda,eta`, take `rho` from the canonical port-multiplicity theorem, and decrease it to at most one. Put `r=rho D/2`. The loaded-hull boundary has perimeter at most `pi D`; cover it by `N<=C(D/r+1)` disks of radius `r/2` centered on the boundary. These centers lie in `W`. Assign each member of `A_I` to one covering disk.

The disks with no good attachment together contain fewer than `eta D` attachments, because each attachment was assigned once and all its incidences are bad. In every other disk choose a good attachment `a_0`. For each assigned attachment `a`, choose an actual neighbor `z in I`. Since the disk diameter is `r`,

```math
|a_0-z|\le r+1\le rho D
```

for sufficiently large `D`. The good incidence `(a_0,I)` therefore supplies an actual contact route from `a_0` to `z` of length `<lambda D`; append the actual edge `za`.

Replace each oriented edge vector along this path by a nearest element of `U_D`. The reference sum belongs to the unit triangular lattice generated by `u_D,v_D`; the accumulated vector error is at most

```math
epsilon_D=K(lambda+1/D).
```

The last edge is a contact from `I` to an attachment, and all preceding edges are internal or attachment contacts, so every edge used is covered by the stated frame hypothesis. No consistency between paths is needed: one path is selected separately for each attachment. If `epsilon_D<1/8`, unit separation makes the resulting lattice labels distinct. Thus the attachment points in this disk lie within `epsilon_D` of one translated triangular lattice.

Apply (1) in every disk having a good attachment and charge all other disks to the fewer than `eta D` bad incidences:

```math
|A_I|\le \eta D+C(D/r+1)\{r\epsilon_D+(Dr)^{1/3}+1\}.
\tag{4}
```

At fixed `lambda,eta,rho`, send `D` to infinity. Since `r=rho D/2`, the last two terms in braces contribute `o(D)` after summing over patches, while

```math
\limsup |A_I|/D\le\eta+C'K\lambda,
```

with absolute `C'` (because `(D/r+1)r/D=1+r/D<=3/2`). Choose `eta<c/12` and choose `lambda` so that `Klambda<1/16` and `C'Klambda<c/12`. For `K=0`, any sufficiently small positive `lambda` works. The upper bound is then less than `c/6`, contradicting the canonical `|A_I|/D>=c/3`. This proves the exact exclusion.

## Adversarial scope checks

- The frame must cover every actual contact internal to this one cell and every contact to its own attachments. A coherent selected grid, a set of selected trains, or the contacts of a small subgrain do not satisfy this premise automatically.
- Path labels may depend on the attachment. The proof needs only a local positional error `K(lambda+1/D)` inside each patch; it does not infer global zero holonomy or an injective source-to-scaffold map.
- Other cells, loaded-loaded edges, and all contacts outside `I` are unrestricted. Thus the theorem does not rule out separate cells with different frames or local grids coexisting with many non-frame contacts.
- Contact-isolated points are retained in `A` and hence in `W`; they are not deleted, moved, or counted as attachments.
- The proof gives no quantitative lower bound on the number or angular distribution of non-frame contacts, no compatible motion, no capacity payment, no strict sub-`D` competitor, and no C-II or Level I closure.
