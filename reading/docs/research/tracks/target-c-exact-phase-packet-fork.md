## What the route establishes, at candidate scope

1. **A nested combinatorial flag.** The threshold staircase has a strict nested
   span flag of simple gap-tree intervals with lengths `1,...,m`. This exhausts
   the quotient combinatorics; it does not identify the intervals with source
   geometry or produce a move. See the [nested-span proof](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_nested_span_flag_2026-10-02.md).
2. **Many bounded KKT packets.** Edgewise transverse multipliers balance
   within support types. The staircase gives linearly many bounded-multiplicity
   packets: internal packets span the opposite shore; the alternative has a
   linear ordered family on an extreme layer. The
   [edgewise balance](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_edgewise_kkt_balance_2026-10-02.md)
   and [flux-packet report](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_edgewise_flux_packets_2026-10-02.md)
   contain the full statements and hypotheses.
3. **Positive packet resultants, without a uniform gap.** The one-peak cut
   decomposition gives a strictly positive longitudinal resultant for each
   selected packet. It gives no uniform lower bound for an individual
   resultant, so a linear packet count cannot yet be converted into a uniform
   stress payment. See [strict packet resultants](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_strict_packet_resultants_2026-10-02.md).
4. **An opposite-shore common load.** In the internal-packet case, a common
   internal load `P_int` enters every opposite-shore cut resultant. Any change
   from exact phase must be paid by non-internal rows, localized to boundary or
   layer terms. The [cross-shore packet decomposition](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_cross_shore_packet_resultants_2026-10-02.md)
   does not rule out those correction rows.
5. **A conditional two-cut escape.** If the opposite shore is not exact phase,
   a finite two-coordinate multishear preserves contacts and unit separation for
   sufficiently small amplitude while shortening a linear family of internal
   diameter rows. This does not improve every actual diameter pair and is not
   a global contradiction. The exact missing step is a global cut equation or
   endpoint argument that controls all inactive upper constraints and prevents
   replacement diameter pairs. See [cross-shore escape](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_cross_shore_escape_2026-10-02.md).
6. **A transverse matching strengthening.** The nested-staircase proof gives
   an `Omega(D^(1/4))` pairwise-crossing matching of actual diameter edges with
   one common A-cut and either full B-support or nested terminal B-support.
   The existing destination cubic-budget report has a separate support-count
   conclusion (linear full-B rows or `Omega(sqrt(D))` boundary carriers); that
   does not give this matching or its nested order, and neither result subsumes
   the other. See [transverse nested matching](../../reports/target_c_exact_phase_threshold_transverse_nested_matching_2026-10-02.md)
   and the [cubic support budget](../../reports/target_c_global_diameter_star_cubic_budget_2026-10-02.md).
7. **A complete-grid alternative.** On the complete-grid branch, the
   [r2 joint-switch proof](../../reports/target_c_complete_grid_joint_switch_budget_2026-10-01_r2.md)
   gives `t_A(h)+t_B(h) <= 2` for each actual diameter edge, total switch rows
   at most `2h`, and a linear family of bounded switching cusps under its
   stated hypotheses. Shared switches are corner-localized. The unresolved
   step is to couple the ordered cusp family to those corner rows and the
   opposite complete shore; split-star and inactive-interface cases remain.
   The older r1 report is retained only as a byte-identical historical version
   at [the source archive](../../../../docs/archive/source-e99-main-42f0296d/docs/reports/target_c_complete_grid_joint_switch_budget_2026-10-01.md.source);
   r2 is the active candidate because it contains the complete four-host proof

## What would close this route

Any successful continuation must supply at least one of the following under the
canonical full-source hypotheses:

- a lower bound on enough packet resultants, or a proof that boundary/layer rows
  cannot supply the opposite-shore correction;
- an exact incompatibility between the two shore equations in the
  double-exact-phase case, including the split-star and inactive-interface
  cases; or
- a same-cardinality planar competitor or discrete replacement retaining all
  labels, including contact isolates, satisfying every source lower-distance
  constraint and every old and new actual-diameter upper constraint.

A strict sub-`D` endpoint contradicts global minimality. At diameter exactly
`D`, the endpoint must stay triangle-free and strictly improve the
contact/actual-diameter-pair lexicographic objective. A finite packet count,
positive local stress, or infinitesimal feasible shear alone is not an endpoint.
