# Target C-II — the adjacent-strip KKT reaction is generated by the angular-extreme switching bars

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Status:** proof-only exact structural strengthening
**Scope:** canonical full-support adjacent O--O strip
**Dependencies:** adjacent switching open-semicircle theorem; adjacent-strip exact KKT reaction; C65; uniqueness of a planar diameter pair in one projective direction
**LEVEL I STATUS:** `STILL OPEN. C-II REMAINS OPEN.`

The sparse-force theorem says a nonzero adjacent-strip KKT reaction can be represented
by one or two switching diameter vectors, but its planar Caratheodory proof does not say
which bars can be chosen. The open-semicircle theorem gives a canonical two-bar choice.

Orient every switching diameter vector from its exterior endpoint into its middle-layer
endpoint:

```math
n_h=Dd_h,
\qquad |d_h|=1.
```

The open-semicircle theorem lets us unwrap all switching directions into one angular
interval of length strictly below `pi`. Write

```math
\theta_-:=\min_h\theta_h,
\qquad
\theta_+:=\max_h\theta_h.
```

Parallel-diameter uniqueness implies that distinct switching edges have distinct
projective directions. Thus, when at least two switching edges occur, unique edges
`h_-`,`h_+` attain these angular extremes.

Let

```math
G=Aa+Bb
=\sum_{h\in H_{sw}}\beta_hn_h,
\qquad \beta_h>0.
\tag{ER.1}
```

Then:

- if there is one switching edge `h`, trivially
  ```math
  G=\beta_hn_h;
  \tag{ER.2}
  
  ```
- if there are at least two switching edges, the direction of `G` lies strictly between
  the two extreme angles,
  ```math
  \boxed{
  \theta_-<\arg G<\theta_+,
  }
  \tag{ER.3}
  
  ```
  and there are unique positive scalars `lambda_-,lambda_+` such that
  ```math
  \boxed{
  G=\lambda_-n_{h_-}+\lambda_+n_{h_+},
  \qquad
  \lambda_-,\lambda_+>0.
  }
  \tag{ER.4}
  
  ```

Thus the two angular-extreme switching diameter bars always provide a canonical exact
force certificate. This does **not** exclude a separate one-bar representation by an
interior switching direction that happens to be collinear with `G`.

## 1. Distinct switching edges have distinct rays

Suppose two distinct switching edges had parallel oriented directions. Their unordered
actual diameter segments would be parallel in one projective direction. The audited
parallel-diameter uniqueness lemma says two distinct diameter pairs in a finite set
cannot be parallel. Hence distinct switching edges have distinct projective directions.

The open-semicircle theorem already excludes antipodal directions globally, so after
unwrapping the common semicircle the angles `theta_h` are pairwise distinct.

## 2. Positive full support puts the reaction in the cone interior

Rotate coordinates so

```math
\theta_-=0,
\qquad
0<\theta_h<\theta_+<\pi
```

for every nonextreme switching direction.

Take the oriented planar cross product. From (ER.1),

```math
 d_{h_-}\times G
 =D\sum_h\beta_h(d_{h_-}\times d_h).
```

Every summand is nonnegative, and at least the `h_+` summand is strictly positive.
Because every `beta_h>0`,

```math
 d_{h_-}\times G>0.
\tag{ER.5}
```

Similarly,

```math
 G\times d_{h_+}
 =D\sum_h\beta_h(d_h\times d_{h_+})>0.
\tag{ER.6}
```

Equations (ER.5)--(ER.6) are exactly the strict angular inequalities (ER.3). Hence `G`
lies in the relative interior of the pointed cone generated by the two extreme rays.

## 3. The extreme-ray representation is positive and unique

The two nonparallel vectors `n_{h_-},n_{h_+}` form a basis of the plane. Since `G` lies
strictly inside their positive cone, its coordinates in that basis are both strictly
positive. This gives (ER.4), and linear independence makes those two coefficients
unique.

There may still exist a different one-bar representation if an interior switching edge
has direction exactly `arg G`. For example, a symmetric positive combination of three
directions can point along the middle direction. The theorem makes no contrary claim.

## 4. Hull-order localization of the extreme bars

The support-normal proof of the open-semicircle theorem gives geometric locations for
the extreme rays.

- When both switch orientations occur and the shorter normal sweep is the common middle
  arc, `h_-` and `h_+` occur at the two angular extremes of the participating middle
  support normals. The middle endpoint order is the audited right-prefix / optional-hub
  / left-suffix order.
- If the complementary exterior arc supplies the shorter sweep, the antipodal support
  normals at the exterior endpoints give the same conclusion in exterior cyclic order.
- With only one switch orientation, use whichever of its single exterior block and the
  complementary middle-containing hull interval supplies the shorter normal sweep.

At polygon vertices a normal cone can contain several diameter directions incident to
the same vertex; the theorem claims angular extremality, not that the two extreme bars
must have distinct middle endpoints.

## 5. Frontier effect

The adjacent-strip exact reaction now has the normal form

```text
one switching edge -> that edge carries the full reaction;

at least two switching edges ->
    reaction lies strictly inside their pointed open-semicircle cone
    and is positively generated by the two angular-extreme switching bars.
```

Combined with the one-arc switching order, a closing argument no longer has to inspect
an arbitrary sparse force pair. It can attack the two hull-order extreme diameter
chords directly.

No C-II closure is claimed. The next target is to compare these two extreme reaction
bars with the two Section-157 boundary cut resultants and the nearly antipodal boundary
contact directions on the live phase-close adjacent pair.
