## LEVEL I STATUS

`STILL OPEN`

The current small-residual low-`t` branch contains a linear population `V_flat` of
**distinct** almost-flat pentagon centers. The existing local theorem partitions these
centers into three actual source-vertex types:

- `V_L`: centers carrying positive diameter load;
- `V_3`: unloaded centers of contact degree three; and
- `V_4`: centers of contact degree four (automatically unloaded).

Thus

```math
V_{flat}=V_L+V_3+V_4.
\tag{ET.1}
```

The previous degree-four activation theorem extracts endpoint-disjoint hidden pairs by
a generic conflict graph. Keeping the physical multiplicity of the local objects gives
a stronger global statement.

Let

- `E_L` be the set of positive diameter edges incident to at least one loaded flat
  center;
- `E_3` be the set of distinct exterior contact edges selected at unloaded degree-three
  flat centers; and
- `P_4` be the set of distinct hidden source pairs generated by the three
  complementary neighbor pairs at all degree-four flat centers.

Then, on the diagonal flatness scale `epsilon_D -> 0`, every sufficiently large source
satisfies

```math
\boxed{|E_L|\ge {V_L\over2},}
\tag{ET.2}
```

```math
\boxed{|E_3|\ge {V_3\over2},}
\tag{ET.3}
```

and

```math
\boxed{|P_4|\ge {3V_4\over2}.}
\tag{ET.4}
```

Moreover every pair `xy in P_4` is inactive and near-unit,

```math
1<|x-y|\le1+\epsilon_D,
\tag{ET.5}
```

with the aggregate gap bound

```math
\boxed{
\sum_{xy\in P_4}(|x-y|-1)=o(D).
}
\tag{ET.6}
```

For every edge `mx in E_3` choose one degree-three flat-center endpoint `m` that
selected it. If the face-edge contact multipliers at `ml,mr` are `alpha_l,alpha_r`,
then the existing local KKT theorem gives

```math
\boxed{
{\alpha_{mx}\over\min(\alpha_l,\alpha_r)}
\le
{\sin\epsilon_D\over\sin(\pi/3-\epsilon_D)}=o(1).
}
\tag{ET.7}
```

Combining (ET.1)--(ET.4), every such source obeys the edge-level three-way alternative

```math
\boxed{
|E_L|\ge {V_{flat}\over6}
\quad\text{or}\quad
|E_3|\ge {V_{flat}\over6}
\quad\text{or}\quad
|P_4|\ge {V_{flat}\over2}.
}
\tag{ET.8}
```

This is stronger than a vertex-count pigeonhole: the third branch contains one hidden
near-contact for every two flat centers in the full population, not merely an
endpoint-disjoint `V_flat/9` subfamily.

## 1. Loaded centers force distinct positive diameter edges

A loaded center is, by definition here and in the parent theorem, incident to at least
one diameter edge of positive KKT multiplier. Choose one such edge from every center
in `V_L`. One undirected diameter edge has only two endpoints, so it can be chosen by
at most two loaded centers. Therefore

```math
|E_L|\ge V_L/2,
```

which is (ET.2). No lower bound on the positive diameter multiplier is used.

## 2. Degree-three centers force distinct weak exterior contacts

An unloaded degree-three flat center has the two almost-antipodal pentagon face
contacts plus one unique exterior contact edge. Choose that exterior edge from every
center in `V_3`. Again one undirected contact edge has only two endpoints, so at most
two centers can choose the same edge. This proves (ET.3).

The flat-center contact-degree theorem gives, center by center,

```math
{\alpha_x\over\min(\alpha_l,\alpha_r)}
\le {\sin\epsilon_D\over\sin(\pi/3-\epsilon_D)}.
```

If an edge was chosen at both endpoints, retain either endpoint as its representative.
The same inequality remains valid there. Hence every distinct edge in `E_3` has at
least one flat-center endpoint satisfying (ET.7).

## 3. Degree-four centers force `3/2` distinct hidden pairs per center

At a degree-four flat center `m`, list the four unit-contact neighbors cyclically so
that the large pentagon sector is the gap from `l` to `r`; use the complementary order
from the parent theorem. The three consecutive complementary neighbor pairs are actual
source pairs. Denote their distances by `d_1,d_2,d_3`. The parent theorem gives

```math
1<d_j\le1+\epsilon_D
```

and, more sharply,

```math
\sum_{j=1}^3(d_j-1)\le\epsilon_D.
\tag{ET.9}
```

Across all `V_4` centers there are exactly `3V_4` pair occurrences before
deduplication.

Fix one unordered source pair `{x,y}` among these occurrences. Every center generating
this pair is a point `m` satisfying

```math
|m-x|=|m-y|=1.
```

The two unit circles centered at `x` and `y` have at most two intersection points.
Therefore the same hidden source pair can be generated by at most two distinct flat
centers. It follows that

```math
|P_4|\ge {3V_4\over2},
```

proving (ET.4).

For the aggregate gap, sum (ET.9) over all degree-four centers. Since
`V_4=O(D)` and `epsilon_D->0`, the sum over all pair **occurrences** is `o(D)`.
Choose one occurrence for every distinct pair in `P_4`; deleting duplicate
occurrences cannot increase the nonnegative sum. This proves (ET.6).

## 4. Global pigeonhole

At least one of `V_L,V_3,V_4` is at least `V_flat/3`. If it is `V_L`, (ET.2) gives
`|E_L|>=V_flat/6`. If it is `V_3`, (ET.3) gives `|E_3|>=V_flat/6`. If it is `V_4`,
(ET.4) gives `|P_4|>=V_flat/2`. This proves (ET.8).

The alternatives are not asserted to be exclusive. In particular, a source may pay
more than one of these currencies simultaneously.

## 6. Frontier effect

The small-residual low-`t` branch can no longer hide the flat-pentagon sea in a vague
local-degree statement. At the current endpoint it must realize at least one of:

```text
>0.08244 D actual positive diameter edges touching flat centers,
>0.08244 D actual exterior unit contacts with an asymptotically weak flat-center end,
or
>0.24732 D distinct inactive 1+o(1) source pairs with only o(D) total gap.
```

The first branch is a direct target for the near-extremal Woodall diameter topology.
The second is a quantitative stress-degeneration branch. The third is a much denser
simultaneous-activation target than the endpoint-disjoint matching alone.

This theorem does not yet create an exact unit contact, an exact unit equilateral
triangle, or a same-cardinality strict sub-`D` competitor. The next closing step must
use one of the three physical populations above rather than another scalar ledger.

## 7. Scope audit

- `loaded` means positive diameter load in the same KKT normalization as the parent
  flat-center theorem.
- The `E_L` and `E_3` multiplicity-two bounds use only the two endpoints of one
  undirected edge.
- The hidden-pair multiplicity-two bound is exact Euclidean geometry: two unit circles
  have at most two intersection points.
- No endpoint-disjointness is claimed for `P_4`; its gain comes from retaining all
  distinct hidden pairs rather than thinning them.
- The aggregate gap bound counts each distinct hidden pair once and is therefore no
  larger than the occurrence-counted sum from the degree-four centers.
- The numerical `0.0009` endpoint constants inherit the proof-only status of the
  phase-star atomic-gap theorem.
