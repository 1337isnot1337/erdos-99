## 1. A singleton P--O train is a legitimate face-angle variable

A complete stopped train with bounded endpoint `F` and outer endpoint `O` occurs in
exactly one bounded nonquadrilateral terminal equation, namely the closure equation of
`F`. If the train is singleton, it contains one contact edge and no strict rhombus.
Rotating that unit edge therefore changes no rhombus equation and no other bounded
exceptional-face equation.

It need not be a bridge. The proof does not use a one-edge shore translation. It uses
the train-angle cycle representation: several P--O singleton and nonsingleton train
vectors are varied together so that the bounded face closes, after which the complete
edge-vector assignment is integrated. Thus the earlier bridge-only singleton shear
restriction is irrelevant here.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 2. Three or more outer spokes give exactly `j-2` local closure dimensions

For a bounded face with `j>=3` P--O terminal trains, the selected side vectors are
`u_1,...,u_j` and the exact closure map is

$$
\Phi(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^jR_{\theta_i}u_i+c.
$$

Canonical bounded-face geometry makes the face strictly convex. A line direction can
occur on at most two sides of a strictly convex polygon: oriented edge directions are
strictly ordered around the circle, and a projective direction has only the two
opposite orientations. Hence among any three selected sides there are two nonparallel
vectors.

Therefore

$$
D\Phi(0)=[Ju_1\ \cdots\ Ju_j]
$$

has rank two. The ordinary implicit-function theorem gives a smooth local zero
manifold of dimension `j-2`. There is no folded rank-one case under the candidate's
strict-convexity hypothesis.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 3. Different face manifolds use disjoint variables

A P--O train has one bounded terminal face. It cannot be a P--O variable for two
bounded faces. Thus the P--O variable sets selected at different bounded faces are
disjoint.

An O--O train has no bounded exceptional terminal occurrence at all. Its angle is a
separate free variable. A B--B train is never varied in the candidate construction.

Consequently the local zero manifolds and the O--O angle box are a genuine Cartesian
product in train-angle coordinates, not merely a dimension count with overlapping
variables.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 4. Crossings in strict rhombi create no nonlinear coupling

Two varied trains may cross in a strict rhombus. This is harmless. If their current
unit direction vectors are `u` and `v`, the cyclic rhombus edge-vector sum is

$$
u+v-u-v=0.
$$

After arbitrary independent train-angle changes it remains

$$
R_\alpha u+R_\beta v-R_\alpha u-R_\beta v=0.
$$

Thus every strict-rhombus closure equation vanishes identically on the whole product.
No laminarity or pairwise noncrossing assumption is being smuggled into the finite
construction.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 5. Bounded-face closure is sufficient for global edge-vector integration

For a connected plane graph, the real cycle space is generated by the bounded facial
walks. Bridges contribute no cycle. In the canonical positive-contact core the bounded
faces are the strict rhombi and the bounded nonquadrilateral faces.

Section 4 shows that every rhombus closes identically. The product construction
imposes exact closure on every bounded nonquadrilateral face. Hence the assigned
oriented unit edge vectors have zero sum on every graph cycle. Fixing one core vertex
therefore integrates them to vertex positions.

Every old contact remains exactly unit length. Every old noncontact, including a pair
involving a contact-isolated source point, starts with strict distance greater than
one. For each finite source, continuity gives a sufficiently small product
neighborhood preserving all those inequalities while the isolated points are held
fixed. No uniform-in-`D` neighborhood is required.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 6. The Euclidean quotient does not consume one of the counted dimensions

The product varies only O--O and P--O train angles; every B--B train angle is fixed.

$$
E_{BB}-L_O
\ge(0.3237862869\ldots-o(1))D,

A nonzero global infinitesimal rotation gives the same nonzero angular rate to every

Translations have already been removed by fixing one core vertex. Hence the full
product dimension

$$
M_D=L_O+\sum_F(o(F)-2)_+

survives in the non-Euclidean quotient. The candidate does not need to subtract one.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 7. The transport lower bound is occurrence-safe

The exact endpoint multigraph identities are

$$
\sum_Fo(F)=E_{BO},
\qquad E_{BO}+2L_O=b.
$$

For every integer `j>=0`, `(j-2)_+>=j-2`. Therefore

$$
M_D
=L_O+\sum_F(o(F)-2)_+
\ge b-L_O-2f.
$$

Also `M_D>=L_O`. Taking the larger of the two bounds gives

$$
M_D\ge{b-2f\over2}.
$$

This uses outer **occurrences**, so an O--O loop contributes two to `b`, exactly as it
should, and no boundary-vertex identification is made.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 8. Correlated scalar minimization checks

Terminal correlated stability gives

$$
a+2(r+\delta)\le5\varepsilon,
\qquad a,r,\delta\ge0,
$$

$$
{f\over D}={M_*+2a-\delta-r\over5}+o(1),
\qquad
{b\over D}=\pi+\delta+o(1).
$$

Thus

$$
{b-2f\over2D}
={\pi\over2}-{M_*\over5}
+{7\over10}\delta+{1\over5}r-{2\over5}a+o(1).
$$

For fixed `r,delta`, the expression decreases with `a`, so the worst point saturates
the stability budget. Substituting

$$
a=5\varepsilon-2r-2\delta
$$

gives

$$
{\pi\over2}-{M_*\over5}-2\varepsilon
+{3\over2}\delta+r.
$$

The minimum is therefore at `r=delta=0`, proving the candidate live floor. In the
small-complement branch, `delta>0.01725` and `a+2r<=0.001`; substituting the maximal
allowed `a` leaves a positive coefficient of `r`, so the worst point is `r=0`,
`a=0.001`, `delta=0.01725` in the closed relaxation. The certificate checks both
constants with exact rational enclosures.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## 9. Diameter-locking inheritance is legitimate but not a closure theorem

The product is an exact contact-preserving manifold in the same core scope as the
existing live diameter-locking theorem. Its tangent basis therefore gives an
actual-diameter derivative matrix of full column rank by canonical mixed
infinitesimal rigidity. The relative-interior KKT stress supplies the strictly positive
row dependence, so the existing positive-spanning, Hall matching, and two-sided signed

This still does not imply a common first-order diameter-decreasing tangent. Positive
spanning says the opposite: every nonzero product tangent increases at least one
active diameter row to first order. The candidate correctly leaves the remaining
problem on the active-diameter geometry/higher-order side.

**Verdict:** PASS with this limitation retained.
