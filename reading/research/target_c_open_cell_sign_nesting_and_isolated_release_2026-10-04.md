## 1. Setup

Work in the canonical contact-minimal, then diameter-pair-minimal C-II source, with
every source point retained. Fix one retained shore of the fixed-star complete O--O
rectangle after common open-witness-cell extraction. Let

```math
\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_m
```

be its pairwise disjoint proper train crosscuts in complement-dual order, with physical
layers $`0,\ldots,m`$. Orient the layers so that the common witness vertex $`v`$ is
in exterior layer $`0`$.

The common-witness theorem supplies two actual diameter edges

```math
h_-=vx_-,\qquad h_+=vx_+
```

crossing every retained cut with opposite strict transverse signs. Thus
$`x_-,x_+`$ lie in exterior layer $`m`$. In the open-cell case their projective
diameter directions are the two consecutive global diameter directions bounding the
common cell $`J`$.

Choose an ordinary lift through the $`v`$-star. Its diameter rays lie in an angular
interval of width at most $`\pi/3`$, so after relabeling,

```math
\arg(x_--v)<\arg e<\arg(x_+-v)
```

for every unit direction $`e\in J`$. Put $`w=Je`$.

For an actual diameter $`h=ab`$ with endpoint layers $`p_h<q_h`$, orient the row
from the lower layer to the higher layer and set

```math
I_h=\{p_h+1,\ldots,q_h\}.
```

The open cell contains no actual diameter direction, hence $`r_h\cdot w\ne0`$ for
every nonempty-support row.

## 2. The common witness vertex is the unique e-minimum

Write $`r_\pm=x_\pm-v`$. For every source point $`z\ne v`$,

```math
|z-x_\pm|^2\le D^2=|r_\pm|^2
```

gives

```math
(z-v)\cdot r_\pm\ge {|z-v|^2\over2}>0.
```

Because $`e`$ lies strictly in the ordinary cone generated by $`r_-,r_+`$, it is a
positive linear combination of them up to scale. Therefore

```math
(z-v)\cdot e>0
```

for every $`z\ne v`$. Hence

```math
\boxed{v\text{ is the unique minimum }e\text{-support point of the full source.}}
\tag{SN.1}
```

## 3. Every e-maximum lies in exterior layer m

The nested proper crosscuts give the usual boundary-layer order

```math
0,1^+,\ldots,(m-1)^+,m,(m-1)^-,\ldots,1^-.
```

Both full-support guard endpoints $`x_-,x_+`$ lie on the common far boundary arc
of layer $`m`$. The two side arcs from $`v`$ to these endpoints have layer labels
nondecreasing away from $`v`$.

Take a hull point $`z`$ on the side arc from $`v`$ to $`x_-`$. Ray order from one
vertex of a convex polygon gives

```math
|\arg(z-v)-\arg e|\ge|\arg r_- -\arg e|.
```

Also $`|z-v|\le D=|r_-|`$. Therefore

```math
(z-v)\cdot e\le r_-\cdot e.
```

The symmetric statement holds on the $`v`$-to-$`x_+`$ side arc. Thus an
$`e`$-maximum lies on the opposite boundary arc between $`x_-`$ and $`x_+`$, which
is contained in exterior layer $`m`$. Therefore

```math
\boxed{\operatorname{argmax}_z z\cdot e\subseteq\text{layer }m.}
\tag{SN.2}
```

## 4. Signwise support intervals are nested

Parameterize every undirected actual-diameter line by its unique ordinary direction
$`\theta\in[\arg e,\arg e+\pi)`$, oriented from the unique $`\theta`$-minimum
support endpoint to the unique $`\theta`$-maximum endpoint. Uniqueness follows
directly from the diameter inequality: if $`ab`$ is a diameter vector $`r=b-a`$,
then for every $`z\ne a`$,

```math
(z-a)\cdot r\ge {|z-a|^2\over2}>0,
```

and similarly $`b`$ is the unique maximum in direction $`r`$.

As $`\theta`$ increases through a half-turn, convex support order moves the minimum
endpoint monotonically along one boundary arc from the $`e`$-minimum to the
$`e`$-maximum, while the maximum endpoint moves monotonically along the opposite
boundary arc in the reverse direction. By (SN.1)--(SN.2) and the boundary-layer
order, their layer labels $`a(\theta),b(\theta)`$ satisfy

```math
a(\theta)\ \text{nondecreasing},\qquad
b(\theta)\ \text{nonincreasing}.
\tag{SN.3}
```

For $`\theta\in(\arg e,\arg e+\pi)`$, the naturally oriented diameter has positive
dot product with $`Je`$. If $`a(\theta)<b(\theta)`$, the lower-to-higher layer
orientation is the natural orientation, so its transverse sign is positive and its
support is $`[a+1,b]`$. These intervals shrink monotonically and are nested.

If $`a(\theta)>b(\theta)`$, the lower-to-higher orientation reverses the natural
diameter vector, so its transverse sign is negative and its support is
$`[b+1,a]`$. These intervals expand monotonically and are again nested. Rows with
$`a=b`$ have empty support.

Hence

```math
\boxed{\{I_h:r_h\cdot Je>0\}\text{ is a chain under inclusion},}
\tag{SN.4}
```

```math
\boxed{\{I_h:r_h\cdot Je<0\}\text{ is a chain under inclusion}.}
\tag{SN.5}
```

The two fixed guard rows have opposite strict signs and both cross every retained
cut. Therefore

```math
\boxed{[1,m]\text{ occurs in both sign chains}.}
\tag{SN.6}
```

This is only a support theorem. It does not assert equality of the two chains or
bundlewise KKT balance.

The full-support guards are essential. Existing nonconstant-phase regressions show
that positive cut balance and full derivative rank alone do not force this
conclusion.

## 5. Proper isolated interval SCCs are impossible

Use the directed gap graph of the open-cell phase-flux theorem. Its vertices are the
physical layers $`0,\ldots,m`$; every actual diameter joining distinct layers gives
one directed edge, with direction determined by its strict transverse sign.

Prefixes and suffixes are already not source/sink components because main's
cut-divergence theorem gives both edge directions across every selected cut. It
remains to consider an interior proper interval

```math
S=\{p,\ldots,q-1\},\qquad 1\le p<q\le m.
```

Assume $`S`$ is isolated in the directed gap graph. Then no old actual diameter has
exactly one endpoint in $`S`$.

The exact interval flux identity on main is

```math
\Phi(S;\mu)
=A_q\sin(\theta_q-\mu)-A_p\sin(\theta_p-\mu).
```

Isolation makes the left side zero for every $`\mu`$ in the open witness cell, so

```math
A_q u_q=A_p u_p.
```

The masses are positive and the directions use one coherent lift of width
less than $`\pi/2`$. Thus

```math
\boxed{u_p=u_q=:u,\qquad A_p=A_q.}
\tag{IR.1}
```

Translate every source point in physical layers $`S`$ by $`\varepsilon Ju`$, and
fix every source point outside $`S`$. The exact laminar slab-cutset property says
that the only old contacts with one endpoint in the moving slab are the two boundary
train families. Oriented from the fixed side to the moving side, their vectors are
$`u`$ and $`-u`$. After translation they become

```math
u+\varepsilon Ju,\qquad -u+\varepsilon Ju,
```

each of squared length $`1+\varepsilon^2>1`$. Thus both boundary contact families
open strictly, while every other old contact is unchanged.

Because no old actual diameter has exactly one endpoint in $`S`$, every old diameter
has both endpoints moved or both fixed and therefore remains exactly $`D`$.

All remaining source pairs were strict before the move. The source is finite, so for
all sufficiently small $`\varepsilon>0`$, every previously strict lower inequality
stays $`>1`$ and every previously strict upper inequality stays $`<D`$. Hence the
translated source is unit separated, has the same diameter $`D`$, creates no new
unit contact, and loses the two nonempty boundary contact families.

This contradicts the canonical contact-minimal choice. Therefore

```math
\boxed{\text{no proper isolated interval can be an SCC of the open-cell gap graph.}}
\tag{IR.2}
```

This is a finite full-source argument. It does not use core ZF and does not extend
rigidity to contact isolates.
