The high-`t` flower theorem leaves a network problem: linearly many source-empty pentagons have strict rhombi on all five sides. This note converts that face population into an exact alternative about the stopped-train endpoint network.

Let `\mathcal F` be any set of fully rhombic bounded pentagons and put

$$
F:=|\mathcal F|.
$$

Every side of every face in `\mathcal F` is the terminal occurrence of one non-singleton complete stopped train. By the audited no-bounded-return theorem, a nontrivial train cannot have both terminal occurrences on the same bounded face.

Let

- `E_int` be the number of complete trains whose two terminal faces both lie in `\mathcal F`;
- `E_esc` be the number of complete trains having exactly one terminal face in `\mathcal F`.

Then the terminal handshake is exact:

$$
\boxed{5F=2E_{int}+E_{esc}.}
\tag{XF.1}
$$

This already gives a useful dichotomy. More importantly, internal flower trains support their face-closure columns only on the `2F` scalar rows belonging to the flower faces. Therefore too many internal trains force contact-flex dimension.

## 1. Internal flower trains create a closure-kernel space

Let `C_{\mathcal F,int}` be the submatrix of the canonical exceptional-face closure matrix formed from

- the `2F` scalar closure rows of the faces in `\mathcal F`; and
- the `E_int` columns of trains joining two faces of `\mathcal F`.

An internal train has no stopping occurrence on any exceptional face outside `\mathcal F`, so its full `C_face` column is zero outside these `2F` rows. Hence every vector in

$$
\ker C_{\mathcal F,int}
$$

extends by zero to a vector in the global train-rate kernel `\ker C_face`.

Pure dimension counting gives

$$
\dim\ker C_{\mathcal F,int}
\ge E_{int}-2F.
$$

Using (XF.1),

$$
\boxed{
\dim\ker C_{\mathcal F,int}
\ge {F-E_{esc}\over2}.
}
\tag{XF.2}
$$

The global all-trains rotation vector spans at most one dimension of this supported kernel. By the audited train-rate/contact-flex isomorphism, the dimension of non-Euclidean contact infinitesimal flexes generated by internal flower-train rates is therefore at least

$$
\boxed{
\dim \mathcal V_{flower}
\ge {F-E_{esc}\over2}-1.
}
\tag{XF.3}
$$

No claim of spatial vertex support is made: the localization is in train-rate coordinates. The integrated vertex velocity field can propagate through the connected contact graph.

## 2. A symmetric escape-or-flex corollary

Taking the midpoint threshold `E_esc=F/2`, every flower set satisfies

$$
\boxed{
E_{esc}\ge{F\over2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\dim\mathcal V_{flower}\ge{F\over4}-1.
}
\tag{XF.4}
$$

Thus a linear full-flower population cannot remain an inert bounded-face decoration. Either a linear number of non-singleton rhombic trains leave the flower set, or the flower-to-flower train subsystem alone carries a linear-dimensional non-Euclidean contact-flex space that must be blocked by the actual diameter graph.

More generally, for every fixed `0<=eta<=1`,

$$
E_{esc}<\eta F
\Longrightarrow
\dim\mathcal V_{flower}>{1-\eta\over2}F-1.
\tag{XF.5}
$$

This Pareto form is likely more useful downstream than the symmetric row.

## 4. Why this is a genuine reduction but not closure

The escape branch supplies a linear family of trains crossing from fully rhombic pentagons to the rest of the stopping-host network. The flex branch supplies a linear-dimensional exact first-order contact-flex space in train coordinates. Canonical mixed rigidity implies that the diameter derivative map is injective on this flex space modulo Euclidean motions, so the flex branch requires a correspondingly large independent diameter-blocking image.

Neither alternative is impossible from rank alone. In particular:

- escaping trains can terminate on other bounded exceptional faces or on the outer face;
- a linear-dimensional contact-flex space can still be blocked by a linear actual-diameter family;
- no lower bound on individual diameter KKT weights follows from dimension alone.

The gain is that the high-`t` full-flower branch is now reduced to two concrete global mechanisms instead of an unstructured incidence graph. A closing theorem can target either host-network escape or diameter blocking of the internal flower flex space.
